# Q4 - Battery & equipment performance

Script: `analysis/q4_battery_equipment.py` (runs in ~16 s; full printed output in `outputs/tables/q4_run_log.txt`).
All wear and CM levels are under our usage-based battery-depreciation assumption: wear = price x SoH loss per cycle / (initial SoH - 70), no salvage value. Where we cap that depreciation at a pack's purchase price, we say so.

## Headline
The battery problem is a **lot-level defect**: Kyron lots KY-2407/08/09 cover 1,461 of 1,638 Kyron packs (89%), including every Kyron 2W pack. They lose health **3.8x faster per day** (3.26x per issue) than every other lot, at the same purchase price. Kyron's 177 later-lot 3W packs are normal. By Jun-2025 the defect had destroyed **₹2.94 Cr of battery value** with depreciation capped at purchase price. The brief's per-swap model gives ₹4.31 Cr, but ₹1.37 Cr of that is wear charged after packs had passed 70% SoH. On the capped basis, H1-2025 CM1 was **+₹1.64 per swap against +₹10.25** without the defect; the uncapped per-swap model gives -₹6.39. The packs recorded as "retired" on 15-Jun-2025 were still being issued at **60.6% SoH** on 30-Jun-2025, and **99.94%** of June Kyron 2W issues were below end-of-life.

## Key findings

1. **Fleet structure: the only pack addition in 18 months was Kyron 2W, all at once.**
   - All 5,115 other packs first appear in the swap log on 1-11 Jan 2024, at ~99.1% SoH.
   - All 1,385 Kyron 2W packs first appear on 10-12 Sep 2024. That is +33.5% on a 2W fleet of 4,133, so Kyron was 25.1% of the 2W fleet afterwards.
   - The batteries master is unreliable on dates: 5,154 of 6,500 packs were used before their recorded commission date. We measure age from first appearance in the log instead.
   - Every pack is present from its first week and none drops out before Jun-2025, so the curves have no left-censoring and no survivorship bias.
   - Tables: `q4_cohort_supplier.csv`, `q4_cohort_lots.csv`.

2. **Kyron 2W fades 3.8x faster per day; SoH falls with calendar time, not with how often a pack is issued (hero figure `q4_01`).**
   - **Per day** (each pack's own SoH-vs-calendar-time slope): 0.1324 vs 0.0351 SoH points per day for Cellora/Amptek, **3.775x**. Binned curves: 13.1 vs 3.4 points per 100 days.
   - **Per issue** (the brief's per-cycle slope): 0.0947 vs 0.0290, **3.26x** (n = 1,385 vs 3,035 Cellora 2W packs; Mann-Whitney p ≈ 0). Binned curves: 9.3 vs 2.9 points per 100 cycles.
   - Life to 70% SoH:
     - The median Kyron pack reaches 70% after **221 days** (308 issues). The Kyron cohort mean crosses 70% at about 245 days (about 330 issues).
     - Cellora and Amptek are **projected** to reach 70% at 832 and 833 days (about 1,005 and 1,004 issues). That is an extrapolation: no Cellora pack has been issued more than 725 times, and the cohort sits at about 80% SoH (80.3% after 539 days).
   - Why per day is the right unit: within Cellora 2W, all 3,035 packs share the same service window but were issued 558-725 times each.
     - The elasticity of SoH lost by 30-Jun-25 with respect to issue count is **-0.03**. Per-cycle wear would give 1.
     - Spearman(issues, loss per issue) is -0.61, but Spearman(issues, loss per day) is -0.02.
     - Kyron 2W (-0.66 vs -0.02) and Cellora 3W (-0.62 vs -0.08) show the same pattern.
     - So "per cycle" figures depend on how often an ID happens to be issued. Kyron IDs are issued 1.37 times a day against 1.18 for Cellora, which is why the per-issue ratio (3.26x) understates the per-day ratio (3.8x).

3. **It is a lot-level defect, not a firmware one; for 2W it cannot be separated from supplier or climate (`q4_02`).**
   - The lot flag is data-driven: a lot is flagged when its median slope exceeds 1.5x the Cellora/Amptek median. Only KY-2407, KY-2408 and KY-2409 are flagged, at 3.24-3.29x for 2W and 2.29-2.41x for 3W.
   - These three lots are **1,461 of 1,638 Kyron packs (89.2%)**: all 1,385 Kyron 2W packs plus 76 3W packs. The 177 Kyron packs from later lots (KY-2410 to KY-2418) are all 3W.
   - 3W, the clean comparison:
     - The three lots fade 2.38x faster per issue than Cellora 3W (0.0775 vs 0.0326; p = 4.3e-45), and 2.40x per day.
     - The later lots fade at **1.00x per issue** (0.0324 vs 0.0326; p = 0.50) and 1.00x per day.
     - Climate exposure is equal: 48.8% of the bad-lot 3W packs' issues were in the hot Kyron-heavy cities, against 47.6% for Cellora 3W and 48.1% for the later lots.
   - 2W is confounded. Every Kyron 2W pack comes from these lots, and Kyron 2W IDs were issued **83.5%** in the hot heavy cities against 44.5% for Cellora. So for 2W, lot, supplier and climate cannot be separated. Within Cellora 2W, exposure barely varies (SD 0.020; Spearman with fade +0.047), so climate cannot be tested there either.
   - Within each cohort, lots do not differ:

     | Cohort | Lots | Kruskal-Wallis p |
     |---|---|---|
     | Cellora 2W | 27 | 0.58 |
     | Cellora 3W | 27 | 0.14 |
     | Amptek 2W | 16 | 0.44 |
     | Amptek 3W | 16 | 0.35 |
     | Kyron fast-fade lots, 2W | 3 | 0.62 |
     | Kyron fast-fade lots, 3W | 3 | 0.21 |
     | Kyron later lots, 3W | 9 | 0.06 |

     Across all 12 Kyron 3W lots, however, H = 166.4 and p = 6.5e-30.
   - **BMS firmware has no effect**, tested with lot fixed effects: 2W F = 1.10, p = 0.33; 3W F = 1.41, p = 0.24. The largest version effect is +1.1% of the median slope.
   - All 53 Kyron 3W packs the master lists as retired come from the fast-fade lots.

4. **Kyron was not cheaper and was not healthier at purchase.**
   - 2W price: ₹31,981.68 for Kyron vs ₹31,986.49 for the others (Welch p = 0.90).
   - 3W price: ₹67,920.95 vs ₹67,918.65 (p = 0.99).
   - Initial SoH: 99.20 vs 99.18 for 2W (p = 0.29).
   - So no discount offsets the shorter life.

5. **Range is proportional to SoH: each SoH point lost costs about 1% of new-pack range, about 0.7 km per 2W swap and about 1.0 km per 3W swap (`q4_03`).**
   - Main estimate: Cellora/Amptek returned packs, compared on the same day in the same city. SoH is each pack's de-noised value, read from its own fitted SoH-vs-time line at the return time.

     | km per SoH point | 2W, all cities | 2W, light cities | 3W, all cities | 3W, light cities |
     |---|---|---|---|---|
     | Same day x city | **0.66** | 0.69 | **0.98** | 0.94 |
     | Same month x city | 0.73 | 0.72 | 1.02 | 1.01 |

   - "Range ∝ SoH" predicts 0.71 km per point for 2W (70.5 km at 98.9% SoH) and 1.00 for 3W (98.6 km at 99.0%). The estimates equal 0.94-1.03% (2W) and 0.95-1.04% (3W) of new-pack range per point.
   - Corroboration: in the light cities from Oct-24 to Jun-25, when swaps per active day were flat (1.313-1.325), km per swap fell **0.75 km per SoH point** (endpoints; 0.77 fitted across the 9 months). That window spans seasons, so it is a check only.
   - **Why the earlier year-on-year figure (0.86 km for 2W, 1.31 for 3W) was too steep:** it compares Jan-Jun 2024 with Jan-Jun 2025, across Kyron's arrival.
     - Light-city 2W swaps per active rider-day stepped up from 1.288 to **1.313 (+1.9%)** when Kyron arrived (3W: 1.147 to 1.162). Over Jan-Jun they were +2.1% (2W) and +1.0% (3W) year on year.
     - More frequent swapping shortens km per swap without any change in range.
     - Holding km per active day fixed brings the year-on-year slope to 0.77 (2W) and 1.20 (3W). The rest is other year-on-year change, which the same-day comparison removes.
   - Reading noise: a regression on the raw single SoH reading (month and city fixed effects) gives 0.551 [0.518, 0.585]. That is attenuated, because the reliability of raw readings within month x city is **0.70** for 2W (0.66 for 3W).
   - SoC at return has no association with km (r = 0.0049).
   - In Q2-2025 a returned Kyron 2W pack had delivered a median **39.5 km**, against 53.4 km for Cellora/Amptek (**-26.0%**; mean SoH 68.2 vs 82.4).

6. **Kyron's range per SoH point is near-normal; the apparent gap is mostly city mix (a Simpson's-paradox trap).**
   - Pooled across cities, at the same month and SoH, Kyron 2W delivered **-4.84 km**.
   - Matched within city x month x 1-point SoH bin, the gap shrinks to **-1.79 km (-3.2%)** (177,652 swaps). On de-noised SoH it is -1.76 km (-3.2%; 137,928 swaps).
   - 3W fast-fade lots: -0.33 km. 3W later lots: -0.01 km.
   - Kyron's problem is how fast it loses SoH, not how far it goes per point of SoH.

7. **Riders in Kyron-heavy cities swap about 10% more often for the same daily distance. The jump came the week Kyron arrived and never tracked Kyron's fade (`q4_04`).**
   - Where Kyron went: Kyron was **39.2%** of 2W packs issued in Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur, against 8.5% in Bengaluru, Mumbai and Pune (Oct-24 to Jun-25).
   - Main estimate: within-rider difference-in-differences, 20 Aug-9 Sep vs 10-30 Sep 2024. Both windows are in the same season and contain no pricing change.
     - Effect: **+0.125 swaps per active rider-day [+0.115, +0.135]**, p = 1e-132, 5,278 riders.
     - That is +9.8% on a base of 1.281.
   - Same distance: within-rider km per active day barely moved (heavy cities 84.3 km, -2.0%; light cities 84.0 km, -0.9%).
   - Checks:
     - Placebo on the 3 previous weeks: +0.011 [+0.002, +0.021]. Two further placebos are null: Apr/May-24 +0.0026 [-0.0085, +0.0137]; Jun/Jul-24 -0.0013 [-0.0115, +0.0088].
     - **Negative control:** 3W riders in the same cities and windows show +0.006 [-0.012, +0.024], p = 0.52 (817 riders). The jump is specific to 2W.
     - **Not near-duplicate records:** no heavy-city swap records under 5 km before or after. Median km fell 65.7 → 57.3 and the median gap to the rider's previous swap fell 1,370 → 1,146 minutes. Same-station re-swaps within 30 minutes moved only from 1.15% to 1.64%. These are real, earlier visits.
     - No fleet-partner contract changes fall in the window, and station openings were 6 in heavy cities vs 9 in light cities.
   - **The extra swaps are not caused by the fast fade.**
     - The heavy-light gap was already full-size in the first full Kyron week (w/c 16-Sep-24): +0.133, when Kyron packs were at **98.3% SoH**, healthier than the rest of the 2W fleet (91.6%).
     - It stayed flat at a mean of +0.129 through Jun-25, and was +0.113 in the last week, with Kyron at 61.2%.
     - Weekly r(gap, Kyron SoH) = **-0.04** (p = 0.79; 41 weeks).
     - So pulling the bad lots may not remove these extra swaps.
   - **City "dose-response" is a two-group contrast.** The 6 cities sit in two tight clusters:
     - Heavy: +0.152 to +0.157. Light: +0.022 to +0.036.
     - Kyron share differs by at most 1.2 points within a cluster but about 31 points between them.
     - So the earlier city line (+0.041 per 10 points, R² 0.995) only restates the two-group difference. It is not dose-response evidence.
   - The effect cannot be traced to the specific pack a rider received:
     - Rider-week fixed effects (rider + city-week; 285,224 rider-weeks): the Kyron share of packs received gives +0.0002 [-0.006, +0.006].
     - First pack of the day being Kyron raises the chance of 2+ swaps that day by only +0.14 pp [-0.06, +0.34].
     - In the heavy cities, km per swap fell for Cellora/Amptek packs too: 65.9 to 56.6 km at constant SoH (92.3 to 91.6), against 65.9 to 63.4 in light cities.
     - Pack IDs do not follow physical packs: the returned pack matches the one last issued to that rider in only 0.03% of 3,566,737 consecutive swap pairs.
     - So this is a city-level shift that coincided with Kyron's arrival. Its mechanism is unidentified. Treat it as an association, not a proven cause.

8. **Gradual ageing of the fleet does not make riders swap more; it means less distance per swap.**
   - The median gap between same-day swaps is flat at 4.36-4.53 h across returned-pack SoH from 60 to 95% (r = 0.0019).
   - In the light cities, between Oct-24 and Jun-25, issued SoH fell from 91.2 to 79.2% and km per swap from 63.3 to 54.4. Swaps per active day stayed at 1.313-1.325, so km per active day fell from 83.3 to 71.8.

9. **Network-wide extra swaps in Q2-2025: about 35,600, or 392 a day.**
   - Calculation: the difference-in-differences effect x 284,492 active 2W rider-days in heavy cities = **35,644 extra swaps**.
   - That is 8.6% of heavy-city 2W swaps (412,104), 4.4% of all 2W swaps (803,480) and **4.0% of all swaps** (896,205).
   - 55,754 (6.2% of all swaps) is an upper bound. It extrapolates the two-group line, so it assumes the light cities' +0.02-0.04 step was also Kyron-related.
   - Riders and fleets paid about **₹21.0 L** for these extra swaps in Q2-2025 (₹58.9 per heavy-city 2W swap). For riders that is roughly a 10% higher cost per km.
   - Because the gap does not track the fade (finding 7), these swaps are not a benefit to expect from pulling the bad lots.

10. **The 15-Jun-2025 "retirement" never happened in operations (`q4_05`).**
    - The batteries master marks all 1,385 Kyron 2W packs plus 53 Kyron 3W packs as end-of-life retired on 15-Jun-2025.
    - The swap log still issued **all 1,385 Kyron 2W packs** after that date (35,183 swaps, 15-30 Jun). All 253 Kyron 3W packs were issued too (4,342 swaps).
    - Before vs after 15 Jun: 2,169 vs 2,199 Kyron swaps a day; 1,095 vs 1,099 distinct Kyron packs a day; Kyron share 24.0% vs 24.2%.
    - Sub-end-of-life issuing: **122,204 swaps handed riders a pack below 70% SoH**, starting on 3-Apr-2025:
      - 121,172 were Kyron 2W and 1,032 Kyron 3W.
      - That was 62.4% of Kyron 2W issues in Q2-2025, **99.94% of June's** (65,504 of 65,543), and 21.9% of all swaps in Jun-2025 (66,473).
    - No inventory shock is visible:
      - Within-rider swap frequency, heavy vs light, 15-30 Jun vs the 3 weeks before: -0.004 [-0.013, +0.004].
      - Triple difference against the same June windows in 2024: charged-2W telemetry +0.05 packs per station-hour; stockouts +0.74 pp (2025: +0.18 pp; 2024: -0.56 pp).
    - Adding the 1,385 Kyron packs in Sep-2024 did not move charged-2W telemetry either (+0.004 packs per station-hour) or stockouts (-0.00 pp) in the cities that received them.
    - **The same 90 stations a year apart (commissioned before Jun-24)** show the same thing.
      - Heavy cities: June stockouts 10.08% (2024) → 9.93% (2025), charged-2W packs per station-hour 7.81 → 7.78.
      - Light cities: stockouts 2.10% → 2.19%, charged-2W 9.08 → 9.08.
      - The all-station figure for the heavy cities (10.08% → 7.46%) falls only because of stations opened after Jun-24 (composition).

      The capped 3W figure of ₹28.6 L covers both 3W cohorts.

    - **The per-swap model over-charges.** It keeps charging about ₹104 per Kyron swap after a pack has passed its own 70% point.
      - Kyron 2W swaps were charged **₹5.78 Cr of wear against a ₹4.43 Cr purchase value**.
      - **₹1.37 Cr** of the ₹4.31 Cr is wear charged after packs passed their own end-of-life.
      - Capping each pack's depreciation at its purchase price gives the value actually destroyed to date: **₹2.94 Cr**.
      - With a per-day reference, which matches the calendar mechanism, the figures are ₹3.16 Cr capped and ₹4.64 Cr uncapped.
    - H1-2025 margin effect:
      - Capped: the gap is **₹8.61 per swap**, and actual CM1 was **+₹1.64** against a counterfactual +₹10.25.
      - Uncapped per-swap model: ₹16.64 per swap, and CM1 -₹6.39 against +₹10.25.
    - After Apr-25 the capped monthly excess turns negative (May -₹7.9 L, Jun -₹19.8 L). The Kyron packs are fully written off, while a Cellora-like pack would still be depreciating. So capped June CM1 (+₹17.96) even exceeds the counterfactual (+₹11.42). That is an accounting artefact of running sub-EOL packs, not an improvement.
    - Timing: the fade was visible within two months. Kyron mean issued SoH went 98.2 → 95.7 → 92.5 from Sep to Nov 2024, against 91.7 → 90.8 → 89.8 for Cellora/Amptek. From Nov-24, ₹2.26 Cr (capped) of excess accrued; it is ₹3.63 Cr under the per-swap model.
    - Payback: a Kyron 2W pack earns ₹44.17 per swap before wear, so ₹13,604 over its 308 cycles to end-of-life. That recovers **42.5% of its ₹31,982 price**. A Cellora pack earns ₹44.41 per swap over a projected 1,005 cycles, recovering 139.5%. Both need about 720 cycles to break even.
    - **Lost-life value** is purchase value x (1 - 1/fade ratio):
      - 2W: **₹3.07 Cr** (per-issue ratio) to **₹3.26 Cr** (per-day ratio).
      - 3W: ₹30.1-30.3 L.
      - Total: ₹3.37-3.56 Cr.

12. **Thermal quarantine has no Kyron link.**
    - Packs are quarantined only when the cabinet is at 39°C or hotter (0.66-1.24% of station-hours in those bands; zero below).
    - Kyron's arrival changed none of the following: quarantine (+0.000), cabinet temperature (+0.13°C), charge minutes (+0.000).
    - Kyron went to the hottest, most stockout-prone cities. At the same stations, June stockouts there did not improve a year later (10.08% → 9.93%, against 2.10% → 2.19% in the light cities; see finding 10).

13. **The defect was detectable within 6-8 weeks of a lot entering service (supports Rec 3).**
    - We measured each pack's per-day fade over its first weeks and compared lot medians with Cellora/Amptek over their own first weeks:
      - First 8 weeks: the three lots run at 2.47-4.50x (2W 4.43-4.50x, 3W 2.47-2.88x). Every other lot is at or below 1.37x.
      - First 6 weeks: 2.40-4.46x against at most 1.42x.
    - Fade varies by season (the Cellora/Amptek reference was 0.0219 points per day in their first 8 weeks in Jan-Feb 2024). The fair comparison uses the same calendar weeks: Kyron 2W 0.0980 vs Cellora 0.0301 points per day from 10-Sep to 4-Nov-2024, which is **3.26x**.
    - At single-pack level a 1.5x rule flags 99.4% of bad-lot packs but also 4.47% of good packs (10.4% at 6 weeks). The rule therefore belongs on lot medians.

## Primary vs secondary drivers

| Role | Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Manufacturing lot: KY-2407/08/09, 89% of Kyron packs | 3.8x faster fade per day in 2W and 2.4x in 3W. This drives the range loss, the battery value destroyed (₹2.94 Cr capped) and sub-end-of-life issuing. |
| Secondary | Calendar ageing of the whole fleet | About 1% of range per SoH point (about 0.7 km for 2W), which lowers km per swap and per active day. |
| Secondary (unexplained) | City fleet mix: Kyron-heavy cities | +9.8% swaps per active rider-day from Kyron's arrival week. Quasi-experimental and city level. It does not track Kyron's fade, so the mechanism is unidentified. |
| Not separable (2W) | Supplier and hot-city exposure | Every Kyron 2W pack is from the bad lots and is issued 83.5% in hot cities. In 3W, at equal exposure, later Kyron lots fade at 1.00x. |
| Not drivers | BMS firmware, purchase price, initial SoH, SoC at return, thermal quarantine | All tested null (see above). |

## Method notes
- **Degradation.**
  - Each pack's own OLS slope of issued SoH on issue index (from `src/clean.py`; every pack has at least 320 issues), and on calendar time in days (computed here).
  - Calendar-vs-cycling test: within Cellora 2W, the log-log elasticity of fitted SoH lost by 30-Jun-25 on issue count, plus Spearman correlations of issue count with loss per issue and with loss per day.
  - Other tests: Mann-Whitney (Kyron vs Cellora), Kruskal-Wallis (lots within a cohort), Welch t-test (price, initial SoH), and OLS with lot fixed effects plus BMS version, with a type-II ANOVA F-test.
- **Trajectories.** Mean SoH at issue in 14-day bins of days in service (`q4_soh_vs_days.csv`) and in 20-issue bins (`q4_soh_vs_cycles.csv`). Each cohort's slope is fitted on the bins that cover at least 50% of its packs. Days to 70% = (initial SoH - 70) / the pack's per-day slope; for Cellora/Amptek this is a projection.
- **Early warning.** The same per-day slope, using only each pack's first 42 or 56 days in service. Lot medians are compared with the Cellora/Amptek median over the same window, and for 2W also over the same calendar weeks.
- **Range.**
  - Main estimate: within-group (fixed-effects) slope of km since last swap on de-noised SoH, Cellora/Amptek returned packs, groups = date x city (and month x city as a check; `q4_range_denoised.csv`).
  - De-noised SoH is each pack's linear SoH-vs-time fit from its issue readings, evaluated at the return time.
  - Reliability = within-group variance of de-noised SoH / within-group variance of the raw reading.
  - The year-on-year comparison is kept with a swap-frequency adjustment: 2025 km x (2025/2024 light-city swaps per active day in the same month).
  - Kyron comparison: matched within city x month x 1-point SoH bin, at least 30 swaps on each side, on raw and on de-noised SoH.
- **Swap frequency.**
  - Within-rider difference-in-differences (riders with at least 3 active days in each 3-week window); OLS of the per-rider change on a heavy-city dummy, HC1 errors.
  - Three placebo windows and a 3W negative control. Near-duplicate check on km and the gap to the rider's previous swap.
  - Weekly heavy-minus-light gap correlated with Kyron's weekly mean issued SoH (`q4_swapfreq_gap_vs_kyron_soh.csv`).
  - Two-way fixed effects (rider + city-week, alternating projections) with errors clustered by rider. Rider-day fixed effects for the first pack of the day.
- **Extra swaps.** Difference-in-differences effect x Q2-2025 heavy-city active rider-days. The two-group line gives an upper bound.
- **Retirement check.**
  - Daily issuing before and after 15 Jun.
  - Triple difference (heavy vs light, late vs early June, 2025 vs 2024) on stockouts, abandonment, queue time and charged-2W telemetry. Telemetry uses only status = ok.
  - Same-station June 2024 vs 2025 comparison for the 90 stations commissioned before Jun-24.
- **Excess wear.**
  - Per swap: the issued pack's wear minus its counterfactual wear, i.e. the same price and initial SoH faded at the Cellora/Amptek median for its pack type (0.0291 for 2W, 0.0327 for 3W).
  - Capped version: wear x clip(pack's EOL cycle - (issue index - 1), 0, 1), so a pack is never depreciated beyond its price.
  - Per-day version: price x (own per-day loss x days in service, capped at the 70% headroom - reference per-day loss x days) / (initial SoH - 70).
  - Replacement maths: June 2025 counterfactual wear on the bad-lot swaps against their capped wear.

## Caveats & traps handled
- **Pack IDs are not physical** (0.03% chain match). Per-pack SoH trajectories are valid; per-pack location and per-rider exposure are not. That is why swap frequency is attributed at the level of city fleet mix.
- **City-mix confounding:** the Kyron range penalty is -4.84 km pooled but -1.79 km within city.
- **Mechanical link:** km *since last swap* falls whenever swaps become more frequent. Light-city swap frequency was not stable across Kyron's arrival (+1.9%). It was flat only from Oct-24 (1.313-1.325). The main range estimate therefore compares packs on the same day in the same city.
- **Reading noise:** raw SoH readings scatter 0.53 points (2W issue readings) around each pack's trend, and the reliability of raw readings within month x city is 0.70. We use de-noised SoH rather than a reliability correction.
- **Cycles vs calendar time:** fade is calendar-driven, so per-cycle ratios and "cycles to EOL" depend on how often an ID is issued. We report per-day figures first.
- **Right-censoring:** Cellora/Amptek life (832 days, about 1,005 issues) is a projection beyond the observed range (at most 725 issues, about 80% SoH).
- **Confounding of lot, supplier and climate in 2W:** only the 3W comparison holds exposure equal.
- **Seasonality:** both difference-in-differences windows sit in the Monsoon and avoid the price changes on 1-Jul and 1-Oct. The early-warning reference uses the same calendar weeks.
- **Placebo is not exactly zero:** +0.011 (p = 0.02), about 9% of the main effect. Two further placebos are null.
- **Master data contradicts the log:** commission dates (5,154 packs used before commissioning) and retirement dates (retired packs still issued). Lot codes nevertheless carry real signal.
- **Wear levels depend on the depreciation assumption.** The per-swap model charges wear past end-of-life. We report capped figures alongside it, and differences between cohorts are robust to the choice.

## Recommendations
1. **Head of Battery Ops: put a hard block in cabinet firmware so no pack below 70% SoH is issued, and actually pull lots KY-2407/08/09.** That is 1,385 2W packs plus 76 3W packs.
   - The case is **service and safety, not margin**:
     - 99.94% of June Kyron 2W issues (65,504 of 65,543) were below 70% SoH, which made 66,473 sub-end-of-life swaps in June (21.9% of all swaps).
     - A returned Kyron pack had delivered a median 39.5 km against 53.4 km (-26.0%) in Q2-2025.
     - Running packs below the end-of-life threshold the company itself recorded is also a safety and liability exposure, although this data cannot measure it.
   - Pull with 1:1 replacement, city by city, starting with Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur. Kyron is 39% of packs issued there, and June stockouts at the same stations were still 9.93% (light cities 2.19%).
   - Cost: like-for-like replacement is about ₹4.43 Cr for 2W plus ₹51.9 L for 3W.
   - Do **not** expect a CM1 gain. Almost all of June's per-swap excess (₹48.3 L of ₹49.9 L) sat on packs already past end-of-life, so the replacement adds about **₹19.8 L a month** of depreciation: ₹22.8 L on new packs against ₹2.9 L still depreciating on the bad lots, about -₹6.5 CM1 per June swap.
   - Expected impact: no more sub-end-of-life issues (about 66,000 a month). The roughly 2,200 daily swaps that now get a Kyron pack lose their 26% distance gap (39.5 vs 53.4 km); a new pack delivers about 70.5 km.
2. **Procurement / Finance: file a warranty or quality claim on the three lots.**
   - Basis: lost-life value of **₹3.07-3.26 Cr for 2W plus ₹30.1-30.3 L for 3W** (₹3.37-3.56 Cr), i.e. purchase value x (1 - 1/fade ratio).
   - Supporting evidence: 3.8x faster fade per day (3.26x per issue) at an identical price (p = 0.90) and initial SoH. Capped value destroyed to date is ₹2.94 Cr, and the packs recover only 42.5% of their price before end-of-life.
   - **Blacklisting Kyron is not justified by the 3W evidence, but clearing it needs lot-level acceptance testing (Rec 3).** The later 3W lots perform like Cellora (1.00x), but that rests on 177 3W packs, and for 2W the data cannot separate lot from supplier.
3. **Quality: add lot-level acceptance testing and an early-warning rule on per-day fade over the first 6-8 weeks in service.**
   - Rule: flag any lot whose median per-day SoH loss exceeds 1.5x the incumbent fleet's over the same calendar weeks.
   - Back-test: at 8 weeks the three lots ran at 2.47-4.50x while every other lot stayed at or below 1.37x. Against the same calendar weeks, Kyron 2W ran at 3.26x by 4-Nov-2024.
   - Apply the rule to lot medians, not single packs: a single-pack rule mislabels 4.5-10.4% of good packs.
   - Expected impact: the lots would have been flagged by early Nov-2024. After that, about **₹2.3 Cr (capped; ₹2.26 Cr) of value accrued**. That is recoverable only through a warranty or replacement claim, because replacing without one simply writes off the packs' remaining value.
   - Early replacement would also have avoided all 122,204 sub-end-of-life swaps, which started in Apr-2025.
4. **CX / Pricing: Kyron-heavy cities had a hidden ~10% price rise per km.** Riders made +9.8% more swaps for the same daily distance, paying about ₹21.0 L in Q2-2025.
   - Offer goodwill swap credits to high-frequency riders there. Q6 should test whether new-rider churn rose after Sep-2024.
   - **Expected impact: pulling the bad lots may not remove these extra swaps.** The gap appeared when Kyron packs were new (98% SoH, healthier than the 92% fleet) and did not move as they faded to 61% (r = -0.04).
   - Treat the pull as a test: track the heavy-light gap afterwards. If it persists, investigate other city-level causes, such as cabinet or BMS behaviour with Kyron packs, station configuration or fleet dispatch, before promising riders a fix.
5. **Data / Engineering: make pack IDs follow physical packs, and sync the battery master with what cabinets actually issue.** Today the log cannot support per-pack warranty evidence or location analysis, and a recorded retirement never reached the field.
6. **Budget (b) "more batteries": do not size a pack purchase from pack-ID counts.**
   - Adding 1,385 pack IDs (+33.5% of the 2W fleet) left charged inventory (+0.004 packs per station-hour) and stockouts (-0.00 pp) unchanged when they arrived.
   - A year later, at the same 90 stations, heavy-city June charged-2W inventory was 7.81 → 7.78 and stockouts 10.08% → 9.93%.
   - Fund the Kyron **replacement** (about ₹4.43 Cr + ₹51.9 L), and base any expansion on station telemetry instead (Q3).
