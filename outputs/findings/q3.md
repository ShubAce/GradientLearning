# Q3: Station and geographic patterns, expansion-wave effectiveness, competitor entry

Script: `analysis/q3_stations_geography.py` (runs in about 12 s; output is deterministic). Figures: `outputs/figures/q3_*.png`. Tables: `outputs/tables/q3_*.csv`. Revised after verification (see the **Verification** section at the end).

## Headline
**Station failures are driven by heat × Gen1 chargers. In Apr-Jun 2025 the 36 Gen1 stations in Delhi NCR, Jaipur and Hyderabad produced 52.6% of all network failed attempts (40,723 of 77,445) from 27.4% of attempts, and 77.1% of the failures above the 3.91% off-season baseline (30,248 of 39,207).**

- **Mechanism:** Gen1 chargers nearly double pack turnaround once the cabinet passes about 41 °C (88 → about 168 min at 50 °C).
- **Rate vs counts:** the network failure rate fell year-on-year (Apr-Jun 9.52% → 7.92%) only through dilution by new low-failure stations. Failed attempts still rose from 39,820 to 77,445 (+94.5%).
- **Hotspots:** the 23 hotspot stations stayed at 16.70% → 16.61% while absorbing 51% more attempts (3.51 → 5.30 per slot-day). Within a Gen1 station, load does not move the failure rate (−0.03 pp, p = 0.94). Their failures rose 18,465 → 27,710 (+50.1%). These rates are set by heat × Gen1, so no siting pattern could have fixed them.
- **Expansion:** separately, both waves were need-blind and followed the existing footprint.

## Key findings

1. **Thermal derating is the battery-turnaround mechanism, and it is specific to Gen1** (Fig q3_01). The hinge fit on 1,415,325 telemetry-ok station-hours (96.8% of all) gives:
   - **Gen1:** 88.1 min flat, then about **9.2-9.5 min per °C** above a knot of 41.25-41.5 °C. On the 0.5 °C knot grid this is +9.46 min/°C above 41.5 °C (R² 0.884); on a 0.25 °C grid it is +9.16 min/°C above 41.25 °C (R² 0.885). Both give **about 168 min at 50 °C**, i.e. 52% of normal charging capacity. The empirical Gen1 mean at 49-51 °C is 166.1 min.
   - **Gen2:** 64.0 min, then +0.96 min/°C above 40 °C, so 73.6 min at 50 °C.
   - **Gen3:** 42.0 min, then +0.18 min/°C, so 43.7 min at 50 °C.

   **When and where it is hot:**
   - In summer, 19.8% of Gen1 station-hours exceed 40 °C. In those hours mean charge time is 131.1 min vs 88.0 min below 40 °C.
   - It is a summer-afternoon problem: 50.0% of summer station-hours at 15:00 are above 40 °C, against 0% at 06:00 and 0% in every winter hour.
   - Summer share of hours above 40 °C by city: Delhi NCR 26.7%, Jaipur 26.5%, Hyderabad 21.8%, Pune 12.5%, Bengaluru 8.2%, Mumbai 5.3%.

2. **Heat-driven derating turns directly into stockouts at Gen1 stations** (table q3_heat_vs_stockouts_gen1). Each Gen1 station-day is compared with the attempts-weighted Gen2/Gen3 mean in the same city on the same day. That removes demand, weather and seasonality.
   - **No hot hours:** Gen1 stockouts are 2.35% vs 2.32%, so there is no excess.
   - **8 or more hot hours:** **12.2% vs 4.8%** (+7.4 pp), and total failures 18.1% vs 7.5%.
   - **Within a station** (station + month fixed effects): each extra hour above 40 °C adds **+0.81 pp** excess stockout rate (95% CI 0.78-0.85; n = 25,170 station-days).
   - **Quarantines:** almost only in hot hours. There were **1,315 quarantined pack-hours** in hot hours vs 16 in all other hours, and 1.08% of hot station-hours had a quarantine. These are summed hourly counts, not distinct packs.
   - **Ruled out:** chargers online below installed slots (1.0% of hours) and outages (0.02%) are the same across generations, so they do not explain the gap.

3. **Raw attribute gaps are confounded. Gen1's penalty appears only in heat, and Wave-2 and city gaps vanish once heat × generation is modelled. Location, host and connectivity hardly matter** (Fig q3_02). The model is a station-month WLS on n = 2,003 station-months with city and month fixed effects, a log-utilisation control and SEs clustered by station.
   - **Base model:** Gen1 +1.35 pp vs Gen2 (p < 0.001), Wave2 −2.52 pp, Delhi +2.64 pp, Jaipur +3.05 pp, Hyderabad +1.82 pp vs Bengaluru. Location, host and connectivity terms are all non-significant (|coef| ≤ 0.23 pp). Utilisation is also non-significant (−0.07, p = 0.56).
   - **Thermal spec:** adding hot-hour share × generation lifts R² from **0.552 to 0.945**. Per +1 pp of hot station-hours, failures rise **+0.36 pp at Gen1**, +0.15 pp at Gen2 and +0.07 pp at Gen3.
     - Gen1's main effect (its gap at 0% hot hours) becomes −0.25 pp.
     - The implied Gen1 − Gen2 gap is +1.79 pp at 10% hot hours, +3.84 pp at 20% and +5.88 pp at 30%.
     - Wave2 drops to +0.20 pp (n.s.), and city effects shrink to ≤ 0.29 pp.
   - **Why raw numbers mislead:** 70.6% of Gen1 stations are in Delhi NCR, Jaipur or Hyderabad, against 20.5% of Launch Gen2 stations. Utilisation (4.22 vs 4.06 attempts/slot-day) and location mix (80.4% vs 79.5% commercial hub or market) are similar. The raw Apr-Jun 2025 city ranking (Jaipur 12.10%, Delhi 11.47%, Hyderabad 9.59% vs Mumbai 4.02%) is mostly where the Gen1 chargers sit.
   - **Queue wait and system errors do not separate stations.** Queue wait is 243.3-244.2 s and the system-error rate is 0.28-0.31% at every attribute level.

4. **Complaints rise with failures: about one extra ticket per 10 extra failed attempts.** Each +1 pp of station failure rate adds **+1.005 tickets per 1,000 completed swaps** (95% CI 0.961-1.049; mean 11.48). A +1 pp rise is 10 extra failed attempts per 1,000 attempts, so this is about one ticket per 9.9 extra failed attempts, not one ticket per failure.
   - **By generation, Apr-Jun 2025:** Gen1 stations logged 21.38 tickets per 1K swaps vs 13.21 at Gen2 and 13.49 at Gen3. Per 1K *attempts*, which removes the denominator effect of fewer completed swaps, the figures are 18.63 vs 12.44 vs 12.80.
   - **Base model:** Gen1 +1.26 tickets per 1K (p < 0.001); Delhi +4.59, Jaipur +5.16 and Hyderabad +3.50 vs Bengaluru.

5. **Both expansion waves were need-blind: new slots followed the existing footprint, not the failure hotspots** (Fig q3_03, tables q3_zone_supply_demand, q3_wave_placement_vs_need).
   - **Hot cities:** Delhi NCR, Jaipur and Hyderabad held **67.8% of Jan-Jun 2024 failures** but 49.0% of active riders and 48.8% of legacy slots. They received **50.4% of new slots** (Wave 1 48.6%, Wave 2 52.3%), about their footprint share.
   - **Wave 1** (need = Jan-Jun 2024):
     - The highest-need third of zones held 49.1% of failures but got 35.6% of Wave-1 slots (158). The lowest third held 15.5% and got 20.3%.
     - Spearman ρ between zone failure rate and new slots per legacy slot = 0.15 (p = 0.46).
   - **Wave 2** (need = same-season Apr-Jun 2024):
     - ρ = **0.05 (p = 0.82)**. The highest-need third of zones held **54.5% of Apr-Jun 2024 failures but got 35.5% of Wave-2 slots** (140). The lowest third held 11.4% and got 34.0%.
     - Using Jan-Jun 2024 need instead gives ρ = 0.06 (p = 0.76).
     - The Jul-Dec 2024 zone rates are not a usable need measure: they are flat noise (mean 3.91%, SD 0.16 pp vs 2.66 pp in Jan-Jun 2024; Spearman with the Jan-Jun rate −0.09).
   - **Scale, not intensity:** new slots scaled with zone size. ρ with zone failure counts is 0.40 for Wave 1 (p = 0.05) and 0.33 for Wave 2 (p = 0.10).
   - **Unserved zones:** three zones had resident riders but no Launch station (DEL-Central, JAI-Central, PUN-Kharadi; 392 riders active in H1-2024). They got only 1 Wave-1 and 2 Wave-2 stations.

6. **New-station utilisation follows unmet zone demand, not site type. Wave 1's fuel-pump and residential sites in already-served zones are the fixed-cost drag** (Fig q3_04, tables q3_new_station_siting_model, q3_new_station_subgroup_economics, q3_wave_economics_q2_2025).
   - **Wave-1 site mix:** 27 of 30 Wave-1 stations are highway fuel pumps (14) or residential (13). **26 of the 27 sit in zones that already had stations.**
     - Site type was not a need signal: legacy Jan-Jun 2024 failure *rates* were similar across site types (fuel pump 6.1%, residential 7.1%, commercial hub 7.1%, market 7.2%; 5.8-7.2% across all six).
     - Rents were not cheaper: mean ₹18,649 for Wave 1 vs ₹17,510 for Launch.
   - **Apr-Jun 2025 economics** (swaps per slot-day; fixed cost per swap):

     | Group | Stations | Swaps/slot-day | Fixed cost/swap |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | Legacy (Launch) | 90 | 4.54 | ₹11.33 |
     | Wave 1, all | 30 | 3.74 | ₹14.73 |
     | Wave 1, fuel pump + residential | 27 | 3.52 | ₹15.59 |
     | Wave 2, all | 30 | 5.51 | ₹10.71 |
     | Wave 2, market sites only | 14 | 5.46 | ₹10.16 |
     | Wave 2 without its 2 unserved-zone fuel pumps | 28 | 4.97 | **₹11.90 (above legacy)** |

   - **Ramp vs same-city legacy stations:**
     - Wave 1 sat at 82-85% over months 1-8 (79-85% on attempts).
     - Wave 2 ran at **116-123% in months 1-3** (111-116% on attempts, which removes the cap that legacy stockouts put on completed swaps).
   - **Unmet demand is what pays:**
     - The 3 stations in formerly unserved zones (**all highway fuel pumps, n = 3**: STN-JAI-148 21.57, STN-DEL-058 9.39, STN-PUN-106 8.73) run **11.56 swaps/slot-day (n = 3)** at ₹4.31 per swap.
     - That compares with 4.56 for all new stations (2.54×) and **3.05 (₹19.19 per swap) for the 16 fuel pumps placed in already-served zones**.
     - Across the 60 new stations (WLS weighted by slot-days, city fixed effects, HC1 SEs, R² 0.70), utilisation rises **+5.17 swaps/slot-day per log-unit of resident riders per zone slot** (95% CI 3.14-7.19, p < 0.001).
     - Once demand is controlled, fuel pump vs market is **−0.50 (p = 0.60)** and residential vs market is −1.01 (p = 0.34).
     - **Inside already-served zones (n = 57)**, site type does matter. Fuel pump is **−1.78 (p = 0.003)**, residential −1.69 (p = 0.025), tech park −1.62 and transit hub −1.68 vs market (all p ≤ 0.03). Commercial hub is +3.14 (p = 0.013; only 5 such new sites).
   - **Cost drag:**
     - New stations carry 40.0% of fixed cost for 37.7% of swaps and lift network fixed cost per swap from ₹11.33 to ₹11.76.
     - Wave 1's excess fixed cost vs the legacy rate is **₹5.05 lakh in Apr-Jun 2025 (₹1.68 lakh/month)**.
     - 16 new stations vs 10 legacy stations exceed ₹20 per swap.
     - CM1 per swap, under our usage-based battery-depreciation assumption, is −₹5.61 legacy, −₹3.52 Wave 1 and −₹7.01 Wave 2. CM2 is −₹16.94, −₹18.25 and −₹17.72.

7. **The network rate fell only through dilution. The hotspots did not improve, and failure counts nearly doubled** (Fig q3_05, tables q3_network_vs_busiest, q3_legacy_did_results).
   - **Same-season failure rates (Apr-Jun 2024 → Apr-Jun 2025):**
     - Network 9.52% → 7.92%; legacy 9.52% → 9.55%.
     - 23 hotspot legacy stations (top quartile of H1-24 failure rate) 16.70% → 16.61%.
     - 36 hot-city Gen1 stations 15.48% → 15.20%.
     - Busiest 10% of legacy stations (9 stations) 10.09% → 9.67%.
     - New stations 5.08%.
     - Outside Apr-Jun, every group runs at 3.85-3.93%.
   - **Counts:** failed attempts rose 39,820 → 77,445 network-wide (+94.5%), 39,820 → 59,281 at legacy stations (+48.9%), **18,465 → 27,710 at the hotspots (+50.1%)** and 26,746 → 40,723 at the 36 hot-city Gen1 stations (+52.3%).
   - **Load does not set the hotspot rate:**
     - Hotspot attempts rose 110,567 → 166,837 (+50.9%; **3.51 → 5.30 per slot-day**) with an unchanged rate. Legacy attempts rose +48.4% (3.40 → 5.04 per slot-day).
     - Within Gen1 stations (station + month fixed effects, heat share held fixed, n = 918 station-months), log attempts per slot-day moves the failure rate by **−0.03 pp (95% CI −0.70 to 0.65, p = 0.94)**.
     - The hotspot rates are therefore set by heat × Gen1. Relieving load by adding stations nearby could not have lowered them. All 23 hotspot stations are Gen1 stations in Delhi NCR, Jaipur or Hyderabad.
   - **DiD design A** (same-season long difference for all 90 Launch stations; controls for change in heat × generation and city fixed effects; SEs clustered by zone): **−0.26 pp** per +1.0 of zone new-slot intensity (95% CI −0.46 to −0.05, p = 0.013).
     - That is −0.16 pp at the median intensity of 0.60.
     - The extra effect at hotspots is +0.56 pp (p = 0.29).
     - Legacy attempts per slot-day change by +0.38 (p = 0.29), so there is no measurable cannibalisation.
   - **DiD design B** (Wave-1 staggered TWFE, 74 treated vs 16 control legacy stations, Jan 2024-Jan 2025): **+0.145 pp** (95% CI −0.13 to +0.42, p = 0.31).

8. **Competitor entry (2025-01-15) caused no statistically detectable loss** (Fig q3_06, tables q3_competitor_did, q3_competitor_event_study, q3_competitor_rider_level). The sample is 8 treated stations open before entry vs 21 untreated stations of the same city and wave, over 34 weeks, with station and stratum × week fixed effects.
   - **DiD:**
     - Completed swaps **−3.9%** (95% CI −10.7% to +3.3%; cluster p = 0.28; within-stratum permutation p = 0.22 over 2,000 draws).
     - Unique riders −1.3% (p = 0.72); failure rate +0.22 pp (p = 0.08).
     - The minimum detectable effect is about a 9.9% decline, so a small effect cannot be ruled out.
   - **Event study:** pre-trends are flat (−1.8% and −1.2%). Post-entry point estimates drift from −4.6% (weeks 5-8) to −7.3% (weeks 21-24), but every CI includes zero. With station-specific linear trends the effect is **+1.7% (p = 0.58)**, so the drift is not robust evidence of erosion.
   - **Rider level:** 647 riders whose most-used pre-entry station is treated vs 1,927 controls.
     - Change in network-wide swaps per week: −0.045 (95% CI −0.220 to 0.130).
     - Still active after entry: +0.5 pp (p = 0.70).
     - Home-station share fell 71.4% → 67.8% for treated riders vs 72.0% → 68.5% for controls. Riders did not leave the network.
   - **Bengaluru Wave-2 stations opened beside the competitor:** STN-BLR-028 and STN-BLR-031 (n = 2) run 3.84 swaps/slot-day vs 5.89 for the 4 other Bengaluru Wave-2 stations. This is descriptive only.

## Primary vs secondary drivers (station-level service failures)
- **Primary: cabinet heat × Gen1 chargers.** This term raises model R² from 0.55 to 0.95. The 36 hot-city Gen1 stations produce 52.6% of Apr-Jun 2025 failures and 77.1% of the failures above the off-season baseline. The effect is seasonal (Apr-Jun) and peaks in the afternoon.
- **Secondary: heat at Gen2** adds +0.15 pp per +1 pp of hot hours, about 2.3× less than Gen1.
- **Context, not a failure driver: zone supply gaps.** Unserved or under-slotted zones (JAI-Central, DEL-Central, PUN-Kharadi; HYD-West at 27.1 resident riders per legacy slot) drive utilisation and access rather than failure rates.
- **Not drivers once controlled:**
  - location type, host type and connectivity tier;
  - load (within Gen1 stations −0.03 pp, p = 0.94);
  - queue wait and system errors.

  Wave and age effects vanish once heat is modelled.

## Method notes
- **Data:** only `telemetry_status == 'ok'` hours were used (96.8% of station-hours); blanks are never zero-filled.
  - Station-month panel from `station_month.parquet` (≥ 15 active days and ≥ 100 attempts for the models). Failure = stockout + abandoned + system error, over attempts.
  - Tickets = 41,161 in-window tickets at real stations (94.9% of all tickets carry a station_id).
- **Hinge model:** `charge_min = a + b·max(0, T − k)`, with the knot found by grid search for each generation. The main fit uses a 0.5 °C grid; a 0.25 °C robustness grid for Gen1 moves the knot to 41.25 °C and the slope to 9.16 min/°C. The level at 50 °C is unchanged.
- **Heat → stockouts:** each Gen1 station-day is compared with the same-city same-day Gen2/Gen3 mean. The within-station slope uses station + month fixed effects.
- **Attribute model:** WLS weighted by attempts (queue wait and tickets by completed swaps), with city + month fixed effects, log attempts per slot-day and SEs clustered by station. Generation is identified within wave (Launch Gen1 vs Gen2, Wave-1 Gen2 vs Gen3).
- **Counterfactual** (table q3_gen1_upgrade_counterfactual): thermal-spec predictions for the 36 hot-city Gen1 stations with generation switched to Gen2 or Gen3.
  - Both alternatives are inside heat support: there are 8 Launch Gen2 and 19 Gen3 stations in the same hot cities.
  - Gen3 is outside Launch-site support only.
  - A naive same-city comparison anchors the model; both are quoted in Recommendation 1.
- **Placement:** "need" means the pre-wave legacy failure rate and failure counts by zone.
  - Wave 1 uses Jan-Jun 2024. Wave 2 uses the same-season Apr-Jun 2024 rate, because failures are an Apr-Jun phenomenon and the Jul-Dec 2024 zone rates carry no ranking information.
  - Latent demand = riders by home zone. Spearman correlations and terciles use the 25 zones with legacy stations.
- **Siting model:** Apr-Jun 2025 swaps per slot-day of the 60 new stations regressed on log(resident riders active in H1-2024 / all zone slots after both waves), site type (ref = market), wave and city. WLS by slot-days, HC1 SEs. A second fit excludes the 3 stations in formerly unserved zones.
- **Legacy DiD:** design A is a same-season long difference. Design B is a TWFE with generation × month fixed effects, because Gen1 has its own heat seasonality.
- **Load test:** Gen1 station-months, with failure rate on log attempts per slot-day plus hot-hour share, station and month fixed effects, and SEs clustered by station.
- **Excess failures:** Apr-Jun 2025 failures minus attempts × the network failure rate outside Apr-Jun (3.91%).
- **Competitor:** weekly panel with the entry week dropped and strata = city × wave. The two treated stations opened after entry are excluded from the DiD. Robustness adds station-specific linear trends.
  - Rider home station = most-used pre-entry station, with ties broken by station_id so the result is deterministic.

## Caveats & traps handled
- **Confounding:** Gen1 is concentrated in the three hottest cities. Raw Gen1, city and "Wave 2 is better" gaps were separated with fixed effects plus heat × generation. Wave 2 opened in summer 2025, when Gen1 failures spike, so its raw advantage reflects timing and Gen3 resilience, not site quality.
- **Seasonality:** all year-on-year comparisons are same-season (Apr-Jun). Wave-2 need is also measured same-season. An off-season need measure produced a spurious "Wave 2 avoided high-failure zones" result (ρ −0.45), which is now dropped.
- **Simpson's paradox / composition:** the network rate fell while legacy and hotspot rates did not, and failure *counts* nearly doubled. Low-failure new stations dilute the average (Fig q3_05).
- **Regression to the mean:** hotspots were selected on the pre-period, so any bias favours improvement. The "no improvement" finding is conservative.
- **Association, not causation:** heat × generation is a strong, mechanistic association (telemetry turnaround → stockouts within station), not an experiment. The siting model is cross-sectional (n = 60), and its unserved-zone evidence rests on 3 stations, one of them an outlier (21.57 swaps/slot-day).
- **Small samples:**
  - DiD B has only 16 control stations in 5 zones.
  - Design A's zone clustering has 25 clusters.
  - The competitor DiD has 8 treated stations and an MDE of about 10%: its null means "no large effect", not "no effect".
  - The naive Gen2 anchor rests on 8 hot-city Launch Gen2 stations.
- **Placement terciles** rest on 25 zones, and new-slot shares are sensitive to individual large stations.
- **CM levels** depend on the usage-based battery-depreciation assumption (no salvage, 70% EOL). Fixed cost per swap does not depend on it.

## Recommendations
1. **Ops/Engineering: fix the 36 Gen1 cabinets in Delhi NCR, Jaipur and Hyderabad before summer 2026, starting with the 23 hotspot stations.** Options are cooling, shading, ventilation, or replacement with Gen2/Gen3 chargers.
   - This is the only lever that lowers the hotspot failure rate, because that rate does not respond to load (−0.03 pp, p = 0.94).
   - Expected impact for an Apr-Jun quarter at 2025 volumes:

     | Option | Model estimate | Naive same-city check |
     |---|---|---|
     | Gen2-like thermal behaviour | −7.36 pp, **19,718 failed attempts (25.5% of network failures)** and about 1,672 tickets avoided | −7.19 pp, 19,266 (24.9%) |
     | Gen3-like | −10.36 pp, 27,751 (35.8%) and about 2,353 tickets avoided | −10.14 pp, 27,158 (35.1%) |

2. **Ops: run a summer heat playbook from March.**
   - Pre-charge buffers before noon, since hot hours peak at 15:00 (50% of summer station-hours above 40 °C).
   - Raise the Gen1 inventory target on forecast heat-alert days.
   - Route riders to nearby Gen2/Gen3 stations in the afternoon.

   Each hot hour costs about +0.81 pp in Gen1 stockouts.
3. **Network planning: site by unmet demand.**
   - **Evidence for unmet demand:** the 3 stations in formerly unserved zones (all highway fuel pumps, n = 3) run 11.56 swaps/slot-day vs 3.05 for the 16 fuel pumps placed in already-served zones. Across the 60 new stations, utilisation rises +5.2 swaps/slot-day per log-unit of resident riders per zone slot (p < 0.001), and fuel pump vs market is −0.5 (p = 0.60) once demand is controlled.
   - **How to rank zones:** use resident riders per slot and same-season failures. HYD-West, at 27.1 riders per legacy slot, is the model case: its Wave-2 commercial hub runs 13.89 swaps/slot-day.
   - **Inside already-served zones:** prefer market or commercial-hub sites (fuel pump −1.8, residential −1.7 vs market, p ≤ 0.03).
   - **Why it matters:** Wave 1's 27 fuel-pump and residential sites, 26 of them in already-served zones, run 3.52 swaps/slot-day at ₹15.59 per swap vs ₹11.33 legacy.
4. **Finance: review the 16 new stations above ₹20 fixed cost per swap.** Renegotiate rent or relocate slots to under-served zones. Wave 1's excess fixed cost is ₹1.68 lakh/month.
5. **Leadership KPI: report failed-attempt counts and the hotspot failure rate, not only the network average.** The network average improved by 1.6 pp, yet failed attempts nearly doubled (39,820 → 77,445, +94.5%). Hotspot failures rose 18,465 → 27,710 (+50.1%) at an unchanged 16.6% rate.
6. **Commercial: keep the Bengaluru competitor stations on a watch-list; do not fund a retention offer yet.** There is no significant loss (−3.9%, CI −10.7% to +3.3%), riders stay on the network, and the late event-study drift disappears with station-specific trends (+1.7%, p = 0.58). Re-test after summer 2025 data, given the roughly 10% MDE.

**Cross-question links:**
- **Q1/Q2:** the Apr-Jun failure spikes, and the "network improved" reading, are this heat × Gen1 composition effect. Counts nearly doubled.
- **Q6:** exposure to hotspot stations is a candidate retention driver. About one extra ticket arrives per 10 extra failed attempts, so ticket volumes understate failure exposure.
- **Q4:** pack quarantines occur almost only in hot hours (pack-hours).
- **Q8:** Gen1 stations carry +50% tickets per 1K attempts.
- **Budget deep dive:** the load test (hotspot rates flat while attempts rose 51%) is the cleanest evidence against "more stations" as a service fix. It favours targeted Gen1 remediation, and demand-led siting for growth.
