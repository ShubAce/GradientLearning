# Q5 - Pricing and partner economics (+ fleet-partner value deep dive)

Script: `analysis/q5_pricing_partners.py` (runs in ~25 s) · Tables: `outputs/tables/q5_*.csv` · Figures: `outputs/figures/q5_*.png`.
All margin levels are **under our usage-based battery-depreciation assumption** (no salvage, 70% SoH end of life); trends and differences are the robust part.

## Headline
Pricing works as a **revenue lever, not a congestion lever**: the peak/off-peak pilot raised revenue per independent swap by **₹7.90 (+11.7%)** while moving only **2.5 pp (4%) of swaps out of peak** and leaving peak stockouts unchanged (**+0.05 pp**, CI −0.09 to +0.18). The **largest fleet partner by swaps, ZipDrop (15.5% of network swaps), is the least valuable**: after its Nov-2024 amendment (28% discount, no peak surcharge) it has the **largest total loss (cm1 −₹69.3 L over Nov-24 to Jun-25, 12/12; cm2 after working capital −₹1.09 Cr, 12/12)** and the **worst per-swap margin of any 2W partner (−₹20.6 cm1/swap)**. Only the 3W cargo partners CargoTuk (−₹22.2) and HaulKing (−₹21.4) are lower per swap, so ZipDrop is 10/12 per swap.

## Key findings

1. **The pilot schedule in the data is not "12:00-22:59".**
   - **Bands:** PEAK (+₹15) applies at **12:00-13:59 and 19:00-22:59**. OFFPEAK (−₹8, not recorded in `discount_inr`) applies at **23:00-05:59 and 14:00-16:59**. STD applies otherwise.
   - **Billing:** for independents, charged minus list is exactly +15.00 at PEAK and −8.00 at OFFPEAK. Partners with `peak_surcharge_billable = Y` are billed **+₹15.00 at peak and −₹8.00 off-peak** in the pilot cities. **ZipDrop and Swiggle Go (N) pay ₹0 extra.**
   - **Congestion by band:** network-wide, the evening window has more failures than the midday one: **6.17% at 19-22h vs 4.95% at 12-13h** (5.30% off-peak, 5.26% STD; 3,814,982 attempts, Jan-24 to Jun-25). That gap is **not network-wide**. It comes from Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur in Summer and Monsoon (finding 12).

   (`q5_pilot_tariff_schedule.csv`, `q5_partner_surcharge_check.csv`, `q5_congestion_by_tariff_band.csv`)

2. **The base price rise (1 Jul 2024; 2W ₹60→65, 3W ₹100→110) passed straight into revenue.**
   - **Revenue:** per independent 2W swap it went from ₹60.00 to ₹65.00, and for 3W from ₹100 to ₹110 (Apr-Jun vs Jul-Aug 2024). Partner 2W revenue per swap went from ₹53.53 to ₹57.97.
   - **Margin:** independent 2W cm1/swap rose from ₹9.59 to ₹15.15.
   - **Usage barely moved.** We compared independents with partner-billed riders (whose employer pays), with rider and month fixed effects over Jan-Aug 2024:
     - **2W:** **+0.2% in logs** (elasticity **+0.02**, CI −0.30 to +0.35) and **−2.0% in levels** (elasticity **−0.24**, CI −0.45 to −0.03; p = 0.024). This is within the pre-period wobble of the event study (−2.4% to 0%).
     - **3W:** not significant (+5.4% in logs, elasticity +0.55; +0.3% in levels).
     - **Continuation:** month-to-month continuation of independents was unchanged at **+0.01 pp** (CI −1.28 to +1.30), against a base of 91.8%.

   Short-run demand is **price-inelastic**: |elasticity| < 0.5 in every *significant* specification. (Fig `q5_03`)

3. **Pilot: timing shifted a little, and partly out of the wrong window.**
   - **Size:** independents in Bengaluru and Pune moved **−2.49 pp** of their swaps out of PEAK hours (CI −3.11 to −1.86; base 61.9%, so −4.0%). Their OFFPEAK share rose **+2.50 pp** (base 9.9%).
   - **Robustness:** the result holds across specifications:
     - Mumbai-only control: −1.98 pp
     - Long pre-period (Jan-Sep 2024): −2.69 pp
     - Triple difference against non-billable partner riders: −2.12 pp
     - Full post-period to Jun-25: −2.33 pp
   - **Where it came from:** **−0.98 pp from the uncongested 12-13h window** (CI −1.51 to −0.45; base 21.3%) and **−1.51 pp from 19-22h** (CI −2.15 to −0.86; base 40.6%). Riders moved to 14-16h (+1.10 pp) and 23-05h (+1.40 pp).
   - **It fades:** event-study coefficients average +0.61 pp before the pilot and range from −1.90 to −2.36 pp in each month from Oct-24 to Mar-25 (mean −2.18). By May and Jun-25 they are −1.35 and −1.27 pp.
   - **Inference:** city-permutation p = **0.067**. This is the most extreme of the 15 possible 2-of-6 city assignments, so it is the smallest p-value this design can produce.

   (Fig `q5_01`, hero)

4. **Pilot: revenue rose, and that is most of the effect.**
   - **Independents:** revenue per swap rose **+₹7.90** (CI 7.84 to 7.95; +11.7% on ₹67.45). It ranged from ₹7.74 to ₹7.90 across specifications.
   - **Mechanical check:** ₹15 × 59.4% peak − ₹8 × 12.4% off-peak = ₹7.92. At pre-pilot timing it would have been ₹8.49, so the timing shift cost only ₹0.57 per swap of surcharge.
   - **Midday window:** **₹3.05 of the ₹7.90 comes from the 12-13h window** (20.3% of swaps × ₹15).
   - **Partners:** billable partners' revenue rose **+₹5.43/swap (+8.4%)**. Non-billable partners' revenue rose **+₹0.22 (not significant)**.

5. **Pilot: congestion did not improve.** We used a station × month panel of peak-hour attempts, with station and month fixed effects, Oct-24 to Mar-25:
   - Stockout rate: **+0.05 pp** (CI −0.09 to +0.18; permutation p = 0.60; base 2.21%)
   - Abandonment: +0.04 pp (permutation p = 0.67)
   - Failure rate: +0.07 pp (permutation p = 0.73; base 3.72%)
   - Queue wait: +0.5 s (CI −1.0 to +2.0)

   **Robustness (stockouts):**
   - **Mumbai-only control** (same ~10% Kyron share as the pilot cities): **−0.07 pp** (CI −0.25 to +0.11).
   - **Balanced panel** of the 90 stations open in all 18 months: +0.07 pp (CI −0.07 to +0.21).
   - **Evening window only (19-22h):** **+0.02 pp** (CI −0.14 to +0.17).
   - **Monthly gaps:** on the balanced panel, treated minus control vs Sep-24 is +0.02, 0.00, +0.01, +0.15, −0.06 and +0.08 pp for Oct-24 to Mar-25.

   **Summer 2025 is a heat artefact.** Including Apr-Jun 2025 makes stockouts look **−1.90 pp** better (station-clustered p < 0.001), but the city-permutation p is 0.33. The same gap existed *before* the pilot. On the balanced panel vs Sep-24:

   | | Apr | May | Jun |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 2024 (before pilot) | −3.38 pp | −5.95 pp | −7.47 pp |
   | 2025 | −3.50 pp | −6.93 pp | −7.30 pp |

   Bengaluru and Pune simply have milder summers. (Fig `q5_01`)

6. **Who responded? The timing response follows the bill.**
   - **Independents:** 2W riders moved −2.57 pp out of peak. Prepaid-pack riders moved −3.23 pp and pay-as-you-go riders −2.15 pp. The independent 3W estimate is imprecise (−1.04 pp, not significant).
   - **Partner Y riders** (employer billed the surcharge) **shifted as much as independents** (−2.25 pp peak, +1.73 pp off-peak).
   - **Partner N riders did not move** (−0.38 pp, CI −1.11 to +0.35).
   - **Revenue impact** was the same across independent segments (+₹7.87 to +₹8.11 per swap).

   (Fig `q5_02`)

7. **Trap: Kyron batteries make naive pilot margins look about 2.4x too good.** Kyron packs went mostly to **Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur** (37.5-38.3% of issued packs, Oct-24 to Mar-25), but only 10.2-10.4% in Bengaluru, Pune and Mumbai.
   - **Margin:** a naive cm1 DiD credits the pilot with **+₹19.08/swap**. After controlling for Kyron share it is **+₹7.87**, and with Mumbai as the only control it is **+₹7.43**, which equals the revenue effect.
   - **Swaps per active rider fall everywhere in the pilot cities.** The naive DiD is −6.3% in logs, but riders who face *no* price change fall as much: Partner-N −8.2%, Partner-Y −7.4%. The triple difference against Partner-N riders is **+4.85%** (CI −1.14 to +10.84; p = 0.11). So the drop is a **city-wide shock of about −10%**, not a price response.
   - **Volume pre-trends are unstable** (event-study pre-mean +5.0 log-points). We therefore claim **no** pilot volume effect in either direction.
   - **Continuation of independents** is unchanged (+0.38 pp, CI −1.37 to +2.12). Retention detail is in q6.

8. **The largest partner is not the most valuable.**
   - **Size:** ZipDrop is the **largest by swaps**: 336,422 swaps in Nov-24 to Jun-25, which is **15.5% of network and 23.4% of partner swaps**. **FeastFly is the largest by revenue** (₹2.01 Cr vs ₹1.64 Cr) and by riders (2,271 vs 1,992).
   - **Totals:** ZipDrop has the **largest total loss**. It ranks **12/12 on total cm1 (−₹69.3 L)** and **12/12 on cm2 after working capital (−₹1.09 Cr)**. Over the full window it is also 12/12 (cm1 −₹75.1 L, cm2 after WC −₹1.49 Cr).
   - **Per swap:** ZipDrop is the **worst of the 10 partners with 2W or mixed fleets** but **10/12 overall**. Only the 3W cargo partners are lower, on every per-swap measure:

     | Per swap | ZipDrop | CargoTuk | HaulKing |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | cm1 | −₹20.60 | −₹22.2 | −₹21.4 |
     | cm2 after WC | −₹32.42 | −₹35.05 | −₹34.90 |
     | Steady-state cm1 | −₹4.18 | −₹14.70 | −₹13.69 |

   - **ZipDrop unit economics:** revenue ₹48.64, energy ₹18.16, wear ₹51.08 and fixed ₹11.10 per swap, giving **cm1 −₹20.60** and **cm2 after working capital −₹32.42**.
   - **Comparators:** independents earn **+₹1.43 cm1/swap (+₹10.6 L in total)**. The best partner on cm1/swap is DabbaXpress (−₹2.84).

   (Fig `q5_04`, hero)

9. **Cost to serve is the same for every partner; the contract decides the margin.**
   - **Within-cell comparison:** we compared each partner with peer partners in the same city × month × vehicle class × tariff-band cell. ZipDrop's revenue gap is **−₹14.70/swap** while its cost gap is only **+₹0.10**, giving a cm1 gap of −₹15.42. Every partner's cost gap lies between −₹0.71 and +₹0.80.
   - **Working capital is a minor factor:** ₹0.32-1.52 per swap (12% a year, our assumption, on 15-45-day terms; ZipDrop ₹0.72).
   - **By segment** (current terms), cm1/swap is:
     - Quick commerce: **−₹17.51** (24.5% discount, 72.6% of swaps at peak)
     - Cargo 3W: −₹22.01
     - E-commerce logistics: −₹10.54
     - Food delivery: −₹5.33
     - Bike taxi: −₹4.15
     - Independents: +₹1.43

   (Fig `q5_05`, `q5_segment_economics.csv`, `q5_partner_mix_standardised_gaps.csv`)

10. **3W swaps lose money even with healthy batteries.** 3W revenue is ₹101.31/swap against energy ₹39.55 and wear ₹84.25, so cm1 is −₹22.49. With non-Kyron packs it is still **−₹14.90** (energy ₹39.98 + wear ₹76.23 = ₹116.21 > ₹110 list). For 2W the steady-state cm1 is +₹12.23. (`q5_vehicle_class_economics.csv`)

11. **The ZipDrop amendment (12% → 28% from Nov-2024) cost ₹10.4 per 2W swap.**
    - **Price:** the realised discount went from 12.0% to 28.0%. Revenue per 2W swap fell from ₹57.20 to ₹46.80, and cm1/swap from −₹2.23 to −₹19.05 (all cities).
    - **Effect vs other partners:** DiD in the **four non-pilot cities**, rider-city + month fixed effects:
      - **Revenue: −₹10.40 per 2W swap** (CI −10.41 to −10.40). This is exactly 16 pp × ₹65 list.
      - **cm1: −₹9.77** (CI −10.45 to −9.09).
      - The aggregate version gives −₹10.44 and −₹9.75.
    - **Why only the non-pilot cities:**
      - Across all cities, the comparison group's revenue rose from ₹58.75 to ₹59.97 (+₹1.22). That is because billable partners in Bengaluru and Pune began paying the pilot surcharge in Oct-24.
      - In the non-pilot cities it was flat (₹58.20 → ₹58.24).
      - The all-city DiD is therefore **inflated by about ₹1.2-1.3 per swap**: −₹11.62 revenue and −₹11.03 cm1 (rider FE −₹11.66 and −₹11.19).
    - **Cost:** **₹4.5 L per month (₹54.5 L per year)** of revenue given up. That is 16 pp of ZipDrop's ₹28.4 L monthly list revenue.
    - **Volume: it bought no extra volume.**
      - ZipDrop 2W swaps grew +56.5% vs +53.2% for other partners. In the non-pilot cities alone it was +60.4% vs +60.2%.
      - Its share of partner 2W swaps went from 24.9% to 25.3%.
      - Swaps per active ZipDrop rider-month changed +0.57 (CI −0.03 to +1.16) on a base of 34.7.

    (Fig `q5_06`, hero)

12. **Evening congestion is a hot-city, hot-season pattern, not a network-wide one.** We measured the evening (19-22h) minus midday (12-13h) failure rate by city and season:
    - **Hot cities in Summer and Monsoon:** the gap is **+2.59 to +4.75 pp in Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur**. For example, Jaipur Summer is 12.67% vs 7.92%, and Delhi NCR Summer 11.64% vs 7.57%.
    - **Everywhere else it is small.** Over all months it is Bengaluru +0.10, Pune +0.33 and Mumbai −0.18 pp, against Delhi NCR +2.34, Hyderabad +1.88 and Jaipur +2.74.
    - **By season:** in Bengaluru, Mumbai and Pune it lies between −0.38 and +1.10 pp in every season (the +1.10 is Pune summer). In Winter and Post-monsoon it is within −0.38 to +0.18 pp in every city. The smallest cell has 11,455 attempts.
    - **So the pilot ran where there is no evening excess**, and its evening-only stockouts did not move (+0.02 pp, CI −0.14 to +0.17).
    - **Queue wait does not measure congestion here.** Mean wait for completed swaps is 243-244 s in every band (p90 423-426 s).

    (Fig `q5_08`, `q5_evening_vs_midday_failure_city_season.csv`)

## Primary vs secondary drivers (per-swap margin by partner)
- **Primary:** **contract price** (discount depth plus surcharge billability). It explains essentially the whole cm1 gap between 2W partners; their cost gaps are below ₹1/swap.
- **Secondary:** **vehicle class** (3W energy and wear are about 2x) and the network-wide **Kyron wear shock**, which raised costs for every partner.
- **Minor:** payment terms (working capital).

Timing and peak concentration matter for capacity, not for unit cost.

## Method notes
- **Data:** clean events (test stations excluded, firmware timestamps corrected). Revenue and costs come from the shared cost model. Completed swaps only for money; all attempts for congestion.
- **Segments:** Independent (pays the tariff); Partner Y and Partner N by `peak_surcharge_billable`.
- **Base price:**
  - Design: difference-in-differences on a rider-city-month panel with rider and month fixed effects. Fixed effects are absorbed by within-unit demeaning of the outcome, treatment and month dummies, with SEs clustered by unit.
  - Window: Jan-Aug 2024. It stops before the Sep-24 Kyron roll-out and the Oct-24 pilot.
  - Outcomes: log swaps (proportional) and levels.
  - Elasticity: Δln(q) / ln(1 + Δp).
- **Pilot:**
  - Treated: Bengaluru and Pune. Control: the other four cities. Pre-period: Jul-Sep 2024 (same list price). Main post-period: Oct-24 to Mar-25, avoiding summer-2025 heat; full post-period as robustness.
  - Rider outcomes use rider-city + month fixed effects (swap-weighted for shares and money). Congestion uses station + month fixed effects (attempt-weighted).
  - Timing sub-bands: the PEAK and OFFPEAK shares are split into 12-13h, 19-22h, 14-16h and 23-05h with the same design.
  - **Event study:** monthly treated × month dummies, reference Sep-2024.
    - For stockouts it runs on the **balanced panel of the 90 stations with PEAK attempts in all 18 months**. It reports the attempt-weighted treated-minus-control gap by month (group + month effects, saturated) with station-clustered CIs.
    - Why balanced: Wave 1 and Wave 2 stations enter part-way, which biased the unbalanced version downward.
    - Check: a station-FE version on the same 90 stations gives the same picture (Oct-Mar −0.10 to +0.14 pp).
  - Robustness: Kyron-share control, Mumbai-only control, long pre-period, a triple difference against Partner-N riders (who face no price change), the balanced panel and the evening-only window.
  - Inference: with only 6 cities, unit-clustered CIs overstate precision, so we add a **city-permutation test** over all 15 treated-pair assignments.
- **Congestion by hour band:** failure rates by tariff band, then split by city × season to check for aggregation (Simpson) effects.
- **Partner economics:** "current terms" = Nov-24 to Jun-25 (after the amendment).
  - Working capital = revenue × 12% (our assumption) × payment_terms_days / 365.
  - Steady-state cm1 replaces Kyron energy and wear with non-Kyron averages; all Kyron packs were retired on 2025-06-15.
  - Break-even discount = 1 − steady-state cost / list price.
- **ZipDrop amendment:** ZipDrop 2W swaps vs other partners' 2W swaps. Pre Jul-Oct 2024, post Nov-24 to Jun-25. Monthly aggregates plus a rider-level DiD with rider-city + month fixed effects. Price and margin are estimated in the four non-pilot cities; volume and the ₹/month cost use all cities.
- **Budget projections:**
  - **(c) Rollout:** DiD per-swap revenue effects × Jan-Jun 2025 monthly volumes in the four control cities. Cross-checked by re-pricing the control cities' own timing mix. The uncollected surcharge is shown net of the −₹8 off-peak credit.
  - **(d) Exclusive:** ZipDrop at **steady state** first; the Nov-24 to Jun-25 run-rate (annualised × 12/8) is shown as history. FeastFly is the comparison.

## Caveats & traps handled
- **Hour bands:**
  - The brief's "peak 12:00-22:59" is wrong in the data (14:00-16:59 is *discounted*). All shares use the data-verified bands.
  - The network-wide "evening is congested" pattern is a **hot-city × hot-season aggregate** (finding 12). Do not generalise it to the pilot cities or to winter.
- **Queue wait does not measure congestion here.** Mean queue wait for completed swaps is 243-244 s in every band (p90 423-426 s), whether congested or not. Stockout, abandonment and failure rates are the meaningful measures.
- **Summer heat** differs by city. Including Apr-Jun 2025 would fabricate a "congestion relief" effect, so the main window excludes it and we show the pre-pilot summer gaps.
- **Unbalanced station panels mislead.** An event study with stations entering mid-period showed a spurious post-pilot stockout decline (Oct-Mar −0.08 to −0.53 pp). The balanced panel shows none.
- **Kyron** confounds every cost and volume comparison across cities and over time. We handle it with a Kyron control, a Mumbai-only control, the triple difference, and the ZipDrop-vs-peers DiD.
- **Concurrent treatments:** the pilot surcharge moved billable partners' revenue in Bengaluru and Pune. Any partner comparison spanning Oct-24 must exclude the pilot cities (done for the ZipDrop amendment).
- **Inference:** only 6 cities. The permutation p of 0.067 is the floor of the design. The effect is consistent across 9 post-months and all specifications, but city-level inference cannot reach p < 0.05.
- **Rollout CI** (₹9.2-9.7 L) reflects rider-level sampling only. Transferring the pilot's ₹/swap to cities with a different timing mix adds uncertainty: re-pricing the control cities' own mix gives ₹9.2 L/month (−3.3%).
- **The timing response fades** (−1.35 and −1.27 pp in May and Jun-25 vs −2.18 pp in Oct-Mar), so the long-run congestion lever is weaker still.
- **Volume and churn:** pilot effects on swaps per rider and churn are **not identified**. Pre-trends are unstable and there is a city-wide shock, so we do not claim a volume loss. The base-price elasticity estimate depends on specification (−0.24 to +0.02); both say "inelastic".
- **Margin levels** depend on the depreciation assumption. Partner *rankings* and *gaps* do not, because cost to serve is the same across partners. The 12% p.a. working-capital rate is our assumption.
- **Association, not proof:** the ZipDrop "no extra volume" result is associational, since the counterfactual growth path is assumed equal to other partners'. Pre-trends support it.

## Recommendations
1. **Pricing / Revenue lead – roll out peak/off-peak pricing network-wide for revenue, keeping the current bands, and do not expect congestion relief.**
   - **Expected uplift:** in the four control cities at Jan-Jun 2025 volumes, **+₹9.5 L/month revenue (CI ₹9.2-9.7 L), about ₹1.14 Cr/yr**.
     - That is ₹5.0 L from independents (63,745 swaps/month × ₹7.90) and ₹4.4 L from billable partners (81,851 × ₹5.43).
     - cm1 rises **+₹9.3 L/month**, because costs do not change with price.
     - A mechanical re-pricing of the control cities' own timing mix gives ₹9.2 L.
   - **Congestion:** expect **no relief**. The projected change is +57 stockouts/month (CI −100 to +214) on 116,384 peak attempts/month, with about 1,586 independent swaps/month shifting out of peak.
   - **Do not switch to an evening-only (19-22h) surcharge network-wide.**
     - It would give up **≈₹3.05 of the ₹7.90 per independent swap** and **≈₹3.9 L/month of the ₹9.5 L/month** uplift.
     - There is no demonstrated congestion gain: the pilot cities have no evening excess, and their evening-only stockouts did not move (+0.02 pp).
   - **Instead, test congestion pricing where evening congestion exists:** a **summer (Apr-Sep) evening-surcharge test in one hot city** (Delhi NCR, Hyderabad or Jaipur), where evening failures run +2.6 to +4.8 pp above midday.
     - Randomise it at station level and measure it on 19-22h stockouts.
     - Pair it with the q2/q3 cooling and capacity fixes.
   - **Fix capacity separately:** use the budget for stations and batteries (q2/q3/q4) for that.
2. **Key-accounts lead – make the peak surcharge billable in every partner contract at renewal (ZipDrop, Swiggle Go).**
   - **Value:** non-billable partners in the four control cities run 29,898 peak swaps/month × ₹15 = ₹4.48 L gross. Net of the −₹8 credit the same tariff gives on their 4,400 off-peak swaps/month, the uncollected surcharge is **₹4.13 L/month**.
   - **Why it matters:** billable partners also shifted their riders' timing (−2.25 pp), so billing is the only pricing lever that changes fleet behaviour.
3. **CEO / Finance – do NOT sign a long-term exclusive with ZipDrop on current terms** (budget proposal d). Judge it at steady state, not on history.
   - **Steady state** (forward-looking; all Kyron packs retired 2025-06-15), on about 505K swaps/yr:
     - cm1 **−₹4.18/swap ≈ −₹21.1 L/yr**.
     - cm2 after working capital **−₹16.00/swap ≈ −₹80.7 L/yr**. cm2 adds allocated station rent and maintenance that an exclusive does not avoid, so **cm1 is the marginal test**.
   - **History:** the Nov-24 to Jun-25 run-rate (cm1 −₹1.04 Cr/yr, cm2 after WC −₹1.64 Cr/yr) includes wear on retired Kyron packs. Its cm1 loss is 4.9x the steady-state one, so do not use it to size a forward contract.
   - **Capacity it would lock in:** **19.7% of all peak-hour swaps** (72.6% of ZipDrop swaps are at peak), with no billable surcharge. The bigger cost is peak capacity, not unit margin.
   - **Break-even:** steady-state cm1 is zero at a **24.6%** discount; that is the marginal threshold. The **6.1%** threshold for cm2 after WC is a full-cost one. The current 28% sits above both.
   - **"Largest partner" is ambiguous:** by revenue and riders it is **FeastFly** (10% discount, surcharge billable, 30-day terms). Its steady-state cm1 is **+₹12.22/swap (+₹57.5 L/yr)** and its steady-state cm2 after WC is about **₹0 (+₹0.03/swap)**. Its terms are the template for any anchor-partner deal.
   - **Instead:** renegotiate ZipDrop's discount to ≤ 12-15%, with volume tiers tied to *incremental off-peak* volume and a billable surcharge. The amendment's ₹54.5 L/yr bought no extra volume.
4. **Pricing lead – re-price 3W.**
   - **The gap:** 3W swaps lose ₹14.90 cm1/swap even with healthy batteries. Steady-state energy plus wear is ₹116.21/swap against a ₹110 list price.
   - **Target:** at the current realised-to-list ratio (0.921), the cm1 break-even 3W list price is about **₹126**. This affects CargoTuk, HaulKing and 3W independents.
   - **Test it** with the same DiD design.
5. **Analytics – keep the evaluation discipline.**
   - **Design:** randomise future pricing tests at station level (more units than 6 cities).
   - **Measures:** measure congestion by stockout and abandonment, not queue wait, on balanced station panels.
   - **Confounders:** net out battery-cohort cost shocks (Kyron) before reading margin effects, and keep concurrently treated units (pilot cities) out of comparison groups.

## Cross-question links
- **q1:** cm1 erosion from Sep-24 = Kyron wear (all partners) + ZipDrop amendment (−₹10.4 per ZipDrop 2W swap; ZipDrop is ~15% of network swaps).
- **q2/q3:** the evening congestion excess is confined to Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur in Summer/Monsoon. This is consistent with the heat and thermal story; capacity and cooling, not pricing, are the levers there.
- **q4:** Kyron cost shock; steady-state economics assume its retirement on 2025-06-15.
- **q6:** churn; the pilot shows no continuation effect.
- **Budget:** (c) supported as revenue (keep current bands); (d) not supported for ZipDrop on current terms; FeastFly-type terms are the template.

## Verification
The adversarial review (`outputs/findings/q5_verification.md`) re-derived the q5 numbers with independent estimators. The updated script reproduces every recomputed value, and **no verifier point is disputed**. What changed and why:

1. **Headline, finding 8 and Fig `q5_04`: per-swap vs total ranking.**
   - **Problem:** "least valuable on every margin measure / deepest loss per swap" mixed two bases.
   - **Fix:** ZipDrop is 12/12 on *total* cm1 and cm2 after WC. Per swap it is 10/12 (CargoTuk −₹22.2, HaulKing −₹21.4 are lower) and the worst of the 10 partners with 2W or mixed fleets.
   - **Script and figure:** the script now prints per-swap ranks (cm1, cm2 after WC, steady-state cm1). The Fig 4 title now reads "...biggest volume, biggest total loss", and the subtitle names the 3W partners.
2. **Finding 11, recommendation 3 and Fig `q5_06`: concurrent-treatment confound in the ZipDrop amendment DiD.**
   - **Problem:** the comparison group's revenue rose +₹1.22/swap from the pilot surcharge (billable partners in Bengaluru and Pune, from Oct-24).
   - **Fix:** re-estimated in the four non-pilot cities. Revenue is **−₹10.40/2W swap** (CI −10.41 to −10.40, = 16 pp × ₹65) and cm1 **−₹9.77** (CI −10.45 to −9.09). The all-city −11.62/−11.03 (rider FE −11.66/−11.19) is now reported as inflated by about ₹1.2-1.3.
   - **Figure:** Fig 6 plots the price and margin panels for the non-pilot cities and annotates the clean DiD.
   - **Unchanged:** ₹4.5 L/month, ₹54.5 L/yr and the volume numbers. We added a non-pilot volume check (+60.4% vs +60.2%).
3. **Fig `q5_01` stockout panel and finding 5: unbalanced panel.**
   - **Problem:** the old event study printed all six Oct-Mar coefficients negative (−0.10, −0.26, −0.17, −0.08, −0.37, −0.53 pp; still printed by the script as a check), which contradicted the +0.05 pp DiD.
   - **Fix:** re-estimated on the 90 stations present in all 18 months. Oct-Mar is now +0.02, 0.00, +0.01, +0.15, −0.06, +0.08. The summer gaps are −3.38/−5.95/−7.47 (2024) vs −3.50/−6.93/−7.30 (2025), replacing the unbalanced May/Jun-25 −5.73/−6.03 in finding 5.
   - **Added specs:** Mumbai-only control **−0.07 pp (CI −0.25 to +0.11)**, balanced-panel DiD +0.07 and evening-only +0.02.
4. **Findings 1 and 12, caveats and recommendation 1: the 19-22h claim was an aggregation artefact.**
   - **Problem:** the network evening excess (6.17% vs 4.95%) comes from Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur in Summer/Monsoon (+2.59 to +4.75 pp). It is small elsewhere (Bengaluru +0.10, Pune +0.33, Mumbai −0.18 pp; within −0.38 to +0.18 pp in Winter/Post-monsoon in every city).
   - **Fix:** added a city × season table and Fig `q5_08`. We dropped "move the surcharge to 19-22h only" as a network recommendation and priced it: it gives up ₹3.05 of ₹7.90 per independent swap and ₹3.9 L of the ₹9.5 L/month. It is reframed as a hot-city summer evening-surcharge test.
   - **Added:** the timing decomposition (−0.98 pp from 12-13h, −1.51 pp from 19-22h).
5. **Recommendation 3, budget (d) and Fig `q5_07` panel (d): backward-looking framing.**
   - **Fix:** the recommendation now leads with steady state: cm1 −₹4.18/swap ≈ −₹21.1 L/yr; cm2 after WC −₹16.00/swap ≈ −₹80.7 L/yr. −₹1.04 Cr / −₹1.64 Cr is labelled as the historical run-rate including retired Kyron packs.

   - **Figure:** the chart adds steady-state cm1 and FeastFly bars and hatches the historical bars.
