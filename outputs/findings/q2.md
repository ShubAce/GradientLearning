# Q2 - Service failures and customer experience

Script: `analysis/q2_service_failures.py` (runs in ~9 s; every number below is printed by it). Tables: `outputs/tables/q2_*.csv`. Revised after verification (see the last section).

## Headline
Service failures are **concentrated, not even**. After vehicle-class mix, every station sits close to a flat **3.91%** Jul-Mar baseline. **80.4%** of the **62,558 excess failures** (29.5% of all 211,849) come from the **36 Gen1 stations in Delhi NCR, Jaipur and Hyderabad in Apr-Jun**. In Apr-Jun those stations handle **31.6% of attempts**; over all 18 months they carry 34.4% of attempts and 47.7% of failures.

On hot days these cabinets overheat and Gen1 charging slows from 88 to 193 min. **The same station-days see 5x the stockouts (12.0% vs 2.3%).** This is a **day-level** effect: it raises failures at every hour of a hot day, worst at 19-22h. The hourly cabinet stock level does not predict which hours fail.

Like-for-like, failure rates did **not** worsen year on year (same-station risk ratio **0.986**, CI 0.975-0.996).

## Key findings
1. **Failure taxonomy (fig 01).** There were 3,814,982 attempts and 211,849 service failures (**5.55%**):
   - Stockouts: 132,786 (3.48% of attempts, **62.7%** of failures).
   - Abandoned in queue: 67,663 (1.77%, **31.9%**).
   - System errors: 11,400 (0.30%, 5.4%).

   Rider cancellations (16,458, 0.43%) are not counted as failures.

   **System errors are a flat noise floor:**
   - 0.294-0.301% across connectivity tiers.
   - 0.299% on both firmware versions.
   - 0.292% offline vs 0.300% realtime sync.
   - No grid-outage effect (MH RR 1.00, CI 0.96-1.03).

   **By vehicle class:** 2W riders fail on 5.02% of 3,399,568 attempts; 3W riders on 9.95% of 415,414.
2. **The baseline is flat; the excess is seasonal (fig 02).**
   - **Jul-Mar baseline.** The rate is 3.91% (monthly range 3.82-4.01%), and all 18 city x charger-generation cells sit between 3.54% and 4.09%.
   - **Stations, after vehicle-class mix.** Raw Jul-Mar rates range 2.80-5.30% across 141 stations (chi2 262 on 140 df, p=2e-09), because 3W riders fail about 2.6x as often as 2W. After standardising for each station's class mix, observed/expected is 0.70-1.34 (chi2 172 on 140 df, p=0.035): close to flat, with only mild residual spread.
   - **Weekdays.** Jul-Mar rates range 3.89-3.93% (full period 5.48-5.62%).
   - **Apr-Jun.** It holds 36.6% of attempts but **55.4% of failures**: May-24 11.9%, May-25 9.5%.
   - **Hour x month heatmap.** The worst cell is **May-24 19:00 at 16.0%**; the best is 2.8%. In May (both years) the 19-22h evening fails **13.2%** vs 8.4% in other hours. Overall, 19-22h carries 38.2% of attempts and 42.4% of failures.
3. **Where: city x charger generation (fig 03).**
   - **By city, Apr-Jun:** Jaipur 13.60%, Delhi NCR 12.20%, Hyderabad 10.36%, Pune 5.55%, Bengaluru 4.47%, Mumbai 4.03%. In Jul-Mar every city sits at 3.85-4.00%.
   - **Pooled generation effect.** Within the same city and month (Apr-Jun 2025, 18 strata), Gen1 fails **2.54x** as often as Gen3 (MH RR, CI 2.49-2.60); Gen2 fails 1.35x (1.32-1.38).
   - **The pooled ratio hides a heat x generation interaction:**
     - Hot cities: Delhi NCR **3.16** (3.05-3.29), Jaipur **3.17** (3.03-3.31), Hyderabad **2.80** (2.69-2.93); pooled **3.05** (2.97-3.12).
     - Other cities: Pune 1.72 (1.61-1.84), Bengaluru 1.19 (1.12-1.26), Mumbai 1.00 (0.91-1.11); pooled 1.33 (1.28-1.39).
   - **Hot-city rates by generation.** Apr-Jun 2025: Gen1 15.20%, Gen2 8.03%, Gen3 5.06%. Across both summers: Gen1 15.31%, Gen2 8.01% (Gen3 existed only in 2025).
   - **The charger matters; the expansion wave does not.** Within the same generation, city and month: Wave1-Gen2 vs Launch-Gen2 RR 1.00 (0.98-1.02); Wave2-Gen3 vs Wave1-Gen3 0.97 (0.93-1.02).
   - **"Concentrated in", not "confined to".** Pune Gen1 also has a summer excess. Apr-Jun "other cities" hold 7.6% of excess (4,782 failures).
4. **A temperature threshold, not a slope (fig 03).**
   - **City-day failure rate by daily maximum:**
     - Below 34C: 3.92% (2,620 city-days).
     - 34-38C: 7.45% (305).
     - 38C or above: 14.25% (357).
   - **1C steps.** 34C 4.1%, 35C 5.4%, **36C 8.0%**, 37C 11.7%, 38C 13.5%, then a plateau of 14.2-14.8% from 39C to 47C.
   - **Within the same city-month**, days at 36C or above fail **2.20x** as often (CI 2.14-2.25).
   - **Official heat alerts.** The alert is a fixed rule at about **42C**: it fires on 148 of 148 city-days above 42C, 2 of 87 at 40-42C, and 0 of 3,047 at or below 40C. So it fires ~6C after failures start climbing, and it adds nothing beyond temperature (RR 1.01, CI 0.99-1.04).
   - **Grid outages and rain are negligible.** Grid-outage days RR 1.02 (1.01-1.03); rain of 20mm or more RR 1.02 (1.00-1.04).
5. **Heat mechanism: telemetry facts plus a day-level association (fig 05, hero).**
   - **Ambient to cabinet.** Cabinet temperature tracks ambient (r = 0.992) at +6.6C for every generation.
   - **Cabinet temperature to charge time.** Gen1 is flat at **88 min below 40C**, then **156 min at 45C or above** and **193 min at 50C or above**. Gen2 goes only 64 to 72 to 76 min; Gen3 42 to 43 to 44 min.
   - **Hot hours empty Gen1 cabinets.** The share of Gen1 hours with zero charged 2W packs is 0.58% below 40C, 37.3% at 45C or above, and **83.6%** at 50C or above.
   - **Exposure.** In Apr-Jun the cabinet is at 45C or above for 23.8% of station-hours in Jaipur, 22.2% in Delhi NCR, 12.5% in Hyderabad, 2.6% in Pune, 0.4% in Bengaluru and 0.0% in Mumbai.
   - **Hot days and stockouts (day level).** Gen1 station-days with a peak cabinet of 45C or above have a stockout rate of **12.00%** (5,245 station-days), vs 2.34% when the peak is below 40C (19,590), i.e. **5x**. Gen2: 5.56% vs 2.37%. Gen3: 3.35% vs 2.34%.
   - **Station-day binomial GLM** (city, year-month and weekday fixed effects; station-clustered SE; 60,893 station-days). Odds of a failed attempt when the peak is 45-50C vs below 40C:
     - Gen1: OR **4.46** (4.22-4.72)
     - Gen2: about 2.1 (2.00-2.21)
     - Gen3: 1.32 (1.20-1.46)
6. **Trap handled: the hourly stock level does not predict which hours fail (Simpson's paradox; fig 05 panel 5).**
   - **Pooled, empty hours look worse.** Hours with zero charged 2W packs show 6.88% 2W stockouts vs 3.06% in stocked hours (crude RR 2.25); abandonment 3.37% vs 1.57%. A row-shifted lag also made zero stock 3 hours earlier look predictive. Both are confounded by the day's heat and the hour of day.
   - **Like-for-like, the link vanishes.** 2W stockouts in zero-stock vs stocked hours, MH risk ratio:
     - Within the same station-day: **0.72 (0.69-0.74)**. Empty hours have *fewer* stockouts, because they are afternoon hours on hot days.
     - Holding the 19-22h window fixed as well: **0.99 (0.95-1.03)**.
     - Within city x generation x month x hour of day: **1.00 (0.95-1.04)**.
     - Abandonment: 0.73, 0.93 and 0.96 on the same comparisons.
   - **No material carry-over.** Lags use a time-based self-join, not a row shift. The pooled gradient (6.88% at 0 h rising to 13.34% at 6 h) is hour-of-day confounding: afternoon empty hours are followed by the evening peak. Within city x generation x month x hour, zero stock 1, 2, 3, 4 or 6 h earlier gives RR 1.03, 1.06, 1.04, 1.03 and 1.06. Some naive CIs exclude 1, but the effect is at most ~6%, not 3-4x.
   - **Hot Gen1 days** (hot-city Gen1, Apr-Jun, peak cabinet of 45C or above; 4,972 station-days):
     - **0-18h:** 2W stockouts run **8.6%**. Every hour is between 8.0% and 9.5%, while the empty share swings from 0.1% to 46.0% (12-17h: 8.6% with 34.2% of hours empty).
     - **19-22h:** stockouts jump to **16.8%**, with only **1.3%** of hours empty. They are 17.5%, 16.8%, 16.9% and 16.8% with 0, 1-2, 3-5 and 6+ charged packs.
     - **Night (0-8h), cabinets full** (mean hourly minimum 8.0 packs on both kinds of day): stockouts are 8.3% on hot days vs 3.2% on other days.
     - **Other Apr-Jun days at 19-22h:** 4.5%.
   - **Reading.** Heat acts as a day-level state at Gen1 stations: on a hot day they fail more at every hour, and about twice as much again at 19-22h. The evidence supports the day-level association (station-day GLM OR 4.46), not an hour-by-hour "empty cabinet causes the stockout" chain.
7. **Queue wait is not a congestion problem (fig 01).**
   - **Wait by outcome.** Completed swaps wait a median 3.5 min (mean 4.06, p90 7.1). Abandoners leave after a median **10.8 min** (3.1x), and 85.5% of them had waited longer than the completed-swap p90.
   - **Kaplan-Meier patience curve.** 99.9% are still waiting at 5 min, 90.5% at 10 min and 60.2% at 15 min; **median patience is 16.6 min**.
   - **Hazard of abandoning per minute.** 0.3% at minute 5, 2.2% at minute 8, 4.6% at minute 10, 10.2% at minute 15.
   - **Wait does not change with load.** At the lightest station-hours (under 0.1 completed swaps per slot) the mean completed wait is 244.0 s (CI 243.4-244.5). At the busiest (1.5 or more) it is 243.8 s (CI 242.7-244.9). Station means span only 241-247 s.
   - **Abandonment barely moves with demand:** 1.75% to 1.94% across demand bins. Like stockouts, it is a hot-day phenomenon, not an hourly-inventory one (finding 6).
8. **Concentration (fig 04, hero).**
   - **Station Gini.** Attempts 0.294, failures 0.383, positive excess failures **0.683**.
   - **Top 10% of stations, each curve ranked by its own measure:** 18.6% of attempts, 24.7% of failures, 42.9% of positive excess.
   - **The 15 stations with the most excess** hold **43.1% of net excess** and 24.5% of failures on 16.8% of attempts.
   - **Fair denominator.** Jul-Mar holds 63% of attempts and zero excess by construction, so compare within Apr-Jun:
     - The 36 hot-city Gen1 stations take **31.6% of Apr-Jun attempts** and produce **80.4% of excess** (failure rate 15.3%).
     - All hot-city stations take 51.8% of Apr-Jun attempts and produce 92.4% of excess.
     - The same 36 stations are the network's busiest third: **34.4% of all attempts and 47.7% of all failures** over 18 months.
   - **Robustness.** With each city's own Jul-Mar class rate as the baseline, the share is 80.5%.
   - **The problem is diluting, not shrinking.**
     - Apr-Jun 2024: hot-city Gen1 had 41.3% of summer attempts and 85.5% of excess (23,336).
     - Apr-Jun 2025: 27.4% of attempts and 77.4% of excess (39,222).
     - Absolute avoidable failures still grew (Rec 1).
   - **The 15 largest sites are not "chronic hotspots".** They are the **15 largest hot-city Gen1 sites by absolute excess** (Delhi NCR 7, Jaipur 6, Hyderabad 2; all Launch; 9 commercial hubs, 6 markets).
     - **All 36 stations fail at their city-month rate.** Against each station's own city x month x class rate, every one of the 36 sits at **observed/expected 0.95-1.05** (between-station chi2 43.6 on 33 df, p=0.10).
     - **The top 15 lead only on size and city.** Their median O/E is 1.00, vs 0.99 for the other 21. They rank first because they are larger (median **14,750 vs 10,522 Apr-Jun attempts**) and sit in Jaipur and Delhi (Jaipur 6 of 10, Delhi NCR 7 of 13, Hyderabad 2 of 13).
     - **All 36 fail in both summers.** Station Apr-Jun rates were 12.6-18.3% in 2024 and 12.0-17.9% in 2025, vs 3.5-4.2% in Jul-Mar. The top 15 had Apr-Jun medians of 16.8% (2024) and 16.5% (2025), vs 3.88% in Jul-Mar, at 4.35 vs 3.81 swaps per slot-day.
     - Tables: `q2_top15_hot_city_gen1_sites.csv`, `q2_hot_city_gen1_station_oe.csv`.
   - **Station-hour grain.** Observed Gini is 0.863, vs 0.857 for a chance-only null using each station-month's own rate (0.842 at a flat rate). The top 10% of station-hours hold 68.1% of failures (null 66.4%). Once station and month are known, hour-level clustering adds little; it is mostly small-count noise.
9. **3W riders (fig 06).**
   - **Same station and month** (MH over 2,037 strata), 3W vs 2W: stockout RR **2.08** (2.06-2.11), abandonment 2.04 (2.01-2.08), any failure **2.01** (1.99-2.03).
   - **The gap persists all year.** Jul-Mar 3W stockouts are 5.47% vs 1.97% for 2W (MH RR within station-month 2.78). In Apr-Jun the ratio narrows to 1.61 because heat adds to both classes.
   - **3W slot metadata does not describe 3W service.** 83 stations listed with **zero 3W slots** take **57.1%** of 3W attempts and completed 212,621 3W swaps, at the same 3W stockout rate (6.41% vs 6.44%). 3W demand per 3W slot is unrelated to 3W stockouts (Spearman -0.002, n=67).
   - **Empty 3W cabinets.** Zero charged 3W packs (1.57% of 3W-equipped hours) coincide with 12.2% 3W stockouts vs 6.3% otherwise. This is pooled, like the 2W figure in finding 6.
10. **No like-for-like deterioration; the network average misleads (fig 07).**
    - **Fixed Launch panel.** The 90 Launch stations served 1.65x the attempts in H1-25 vs H1-24, with failures up only 1.57x. Same station and same month, the risk ratio is **0.986 (CI 0.975-0.996)**; for Apr-Jun alone 0.989 (0.977-1.001).
    - **Hot-city Gen1 Launch stations in May.** Load rose from 52.6 to 82.5 attempts per station-day, yet the failure rate held at 19.1% then 19.0%.
    - **Busiest stations.** The 15 busiest by H1-24 load show RR 0.969 (0.949-0.988), so there is no hidden deterioration there.
    - **Network average.** The May network rate fell from 11.9% to 9.5% purely through mix. In May-25, Launch stations ran 11.9%, Wave1 6.3% and Wave2 5.0%, with Wave1 taking 16.0% and Wave2 21.1% of attempts.
11. **Customer experience: tickets (fig 08).** All rates use the 41,161 station-linked tickets; 2,219 of the 43,380 in-window tickets have no station.
    - **Stations.** More stockouts go with more "no battery" tickets per 1K attempts (Spearman **0.843**, n=150). "Long queue" tickets track abandonment (0.670). At station-month level the correlation is 0.547 (n=2,037); across months 0.886 (n=18).
    - **Complaint propensity is stable** at about 8 "no battery" tickets per 100 stockouts: 7.8 (Jan-24), 8.2 (Jan-25), 7.9 (May-25). Counting all tickets: 8.2, 8.6, 8.3.
    - **Ticket growth is not failure-driven.** Tickets per 1K attempts rose from 5.58 (Jan-24) to 10.49 (Jan-25) at the same ~3.9% failure rate (all tickets: 6.04 to 11.06). In Jan-Mar the growth came from `other` (0.51 to 3.09 per 1K), `low_range` (0.34 to 2.14) and `app_issue` (0.32 to 1.78); `no_battery_available` was flat (1.98 to 1.95).
12. **After a failure, riders do not retry: a failure is a lost transaction, not a deferred one.**
    - **Within 60 min:** after a service failure (n=184,776) the same rider completes a swap anywhere in the network 4.5% of the time, vs 4.6% after a completed swap (n=3,283,479).
    - **Within 24 h:** 55.7% vs 57.2%. The median gap to the next completed swap is 22.7 h vs 22.3 h.
    - **Short-run retention is unaffected.** New riders whose first attempt failed made a further swap within 30 days as often as those whose first attempt succeeded (99.8% vs 99.8%; n=646 vs 13,084). This is a cross-link for Q6, short horizon only.

## Primary vs secondary drivers
- **Primary: heat x charger generation, acting at the day level.** On hot days (city maximum of 36C or above; Gen1 cabinet peak of 45C or above), hot-city Gen1 stations fail more at every hour of the day. Gen1 accounts for 80.4% of excess failures and Gen2 for 9.1%.
- **Secondary:**
  - Vehicle class: 3W fails ~2x at the same station all year.
  - The 19-22h window inside hot days: 16.8% vs 8.6% earlier the same day. This is not explained by the hourly stock level.
  - Pune's smaller Gen1 summer excess (Gen1 vs Gen3 RR 1.72).
- **Not drivers:** station load and queue capacity (wait is flat with load), weekday, expansion wave (once generation is fixed), grid outages, rain, heat-alert flags beyond temperature, connectivity tier and firmware (system errors are flat), and the hour-to-hour cabinet stock level within a day.

## Method notes
- **Definitions.**
  - Service failure = stockout + abandoned + system error, per the brief.
  - Hour = firmware-corrected local hour.
  - Baseline = Jul-Mar network rate per vehicle class (2W 3.34%, 3W 8.62%).
  - Excess = observed failures minus attempts x class baseline, at station-month-class grain.
  - The Apr-Jun "hot" window is data-driven. It differs from `SEASON_OF_MONTH` (Summer = Mar-May): March sits on the baseline and June does not.
- **Group comparisons.** Mantel-Haenszel risk ratios with Greenland-Robins CIs, inside strata that hold mix fixed:
  - city x month: charger generation, overall and by city, and weather
  - station x month: 3W vs 2W
  - station x calendar month: year on year
  - station-day, station-day x window, and city x generation x month x hour: the hourly-stock trap test
- **Heat effect model.** Station-day binomial GLM per charger generation, with bands of daily peak cabinet temperature (peak over all non-missing telemetry hours of the day) plus city, year-month and weekday fixed effects. Standard errors are clustered by station (60,893 station-days).
- **Station heterogeneity tests.** Chi-square of observed vs expected failures:
  - Jul-Mar: expected = each station's attempts x class baseline.
  - The 36 hot-city Gen1 stations: expected = city x month x class rate in Apr-Jun; df = stations minus cities.
- **Lags.** Lagged stock comes from a time-based self-join on `hour_start`, not a row shift.
- **Patience.** Kaplan-Meier with abandonment as the event and completion as censoring.
- **Concentration nulls.** Seeded binomial simulations at station-hour grain (flat rate; station-month rate).
- **Telemetry coverage.** 96.8% `ok`, 2.5% `partial` (values present), 0.64% `missing`. All missing rows are at poor-connectivity stations (4.0% of that tier's hours) and are excluded null-aware. 99.33% of attempts fall in hours with usable telemetry.
- **Retry check.** For each attempt before 2025-06-01: the gap to the same rider's next completed swap anywhere in the network.

## Caveats and traps handled
- **Hour-level Simpson's paradox (new).** Pooled, empty-cabinet hours fail 2.25x as often and appear to "lead" stockouts by hours. Within the same station-day and window, or the same hour of day, the ratio is 0.99-1.00. The pooled lag gradient is hour-of-day confounding, so the hourly chain was dropped in favour of the day-level association.
- **Seasonality and mix.**
  - The naive network trend and the May-24 vs May-25 "improvement" are mix effects; same-station, same-month comparisons show no change.
  - In Jul-Mar the evening looks *better* (3.69% vs 4.05%), but that is 2W/3W mix: 3W is 6.6% of evening attempts vs 13.4% at other hours. Within class the rates are equal (2W 3.33% vs 3.34%).
- **Denominators.** Excess failures are compared with Apr-Jun attempts, not all attempts (Jul-Mar has zero excess by construction).
- **Size vs rate.** "Most excess" ranks big stations first; the O/E test shows they are not unusual for their city and month.
- **Firmware clock.** Uncorrected, the 139,490 shifted events would put only 2.3% of attempts (2.2% of failures) in 19-22h, instead of 38.0% (41.0%).
- **Generation is confounded with city and opening date.**
  - Gen3 sites exist only from 2025 (Wave sites). This is handled with city x month strata and the GLM, but it remains an association, not an experiment.
  - Load is comparable: hot-city Gen1 81.8 vs Gen3 84.1 attempts per station-day in Apr-Jun 2025.
  - The Gen3 counterfactual in Rec 1 assumes Gen3 performance transfers.
- **CI widths.** MH confidence intervals treat attempts as independent and are too narrow under station-day clustering; the clustered GLM is the robust heat estimate. This also applies to the small 1.03-1.06 lag ratios.
- **KM assumption.** The patience curve assumes service time is independent of patience. Waits are capped (completed 30 min, abandoned 40 min).
- **Synthetic-data signatures.** Queue wait is invariant to everything (no congestion physics). On hot days, stockouts occur at 8.3% even with full cabinets at night, so the simulated stockout process follows the day's heat state rather than the hourly pack count.
- **Data quality.**
  - 3W slot metadata contradicts usage.
  - Ticket categories are agent-assigned, and 2,219 in-window tickets lack a station.
  - `heat_alert` is a deterministic temperature flag (about 42C), not independent information.
- **Thin exposures.** Quarantined packs (1,372 station-hours, 10.51% failure rate) and outage hours (303) are too rare to matter at network level.

## Recommendations
1. **Network ops / engineering: retrofit or replace the Gen1 cabinets at the 36 hot-city Gen1 stations before April.** Use cooling or shading to keep cabinets below 40C, or upgrade to Gen3 chargers.
   - **Prioritise by city x volume**, not by a station "hotspot" list (all 36 fail at their city-month rate). Avoidable failures per site per summer at 2025 volume (MH counterfactual within city-month; `q2_retrofit_priority_by_city.csv`):
     - Delhi NCR: 13 sites, RR 3.16, **~835 per site**.
     - Jaipur: 10 sites, RR 3.17, **~810 per site**.
     - Hyderabad: 13 sites, RR 2.80, ~644 per site.
     - Pune: 4 Gen1 sites, RR 1.72, ~208 per site (next tier).
   - **Expected impact, if Gen3 performance transfers.** At the hot-city Gen3 Apr-Jun rate (5.06% vs 15.31%), retrofitting avoids **17,996 failures at 2024 volume and 27,158 at 2025 volume**. The MH within-city-month estimate for 2025 is 27,356. Over the two summers that is 45,154, or 21.3% of all failures. The 15 largest sites alone account for about 12,211 per summer, because they are big.
   - **Value.** Riders do not retry after a failure (finding 12), so each avoided failure is roughly one more paid swap. At ₹62.7 mean revenue per completed swap, 27,158 avoided failures are about **₹1.70M of revenue per summer** (revenue, not margin).
2. **City ops: run a heat playbook triggered by a forecast daily maximum of 36C or more** (RR 2.20 within city-month), not by official heat alerts. Alerts fire only above ~42C, after failures have already plateaued at ~14-15%.
   - **Target the 19-22h window on hot days at hot-city Gen1 sites.** 2W stockouts there run 16.8%, vs 8.6% earlier the same day and 4.5% at 19-22h on other Apr-Jun days.
   - **Actions:**
     - Redirect riders in-app to nearby Gen3 or cooler sites; hot-city Gen3 sites fail 5.06% at similar load.
     - Shape demand out of 19-22h, coordinated with the Q5 pricing work.
     - Protect afternoon charger throughput with shade or cooling.
   - **Pre-charging or higher inventory targets are not evidenced by this data.** On hot days, stockouts run 8.3% at 0-8h with full cabinets (mean 8.0 packs) and ~17% at 19-22h at every stock level.
   - Pilot at matched hot-city Gen1 sites with controls; the effect cannot be sized from observational data.
3. **Budget (links to the deep-dive): do not buy queue capacity to cut failures.** Wait time is flat at ~4.06 min regardless of load.
   - More stations help only if they are Gen3 (or cooled) sites in hot cities.
   - More batteries are not evidenced to help: hourly stock does not predict failing hours, and hot-day stockouts occur with full cabinets. Charger generation and cabinet temperature are the levers.
4. **Fleet / 3W product: fix 3W pack availability and the slot metadata.** 3W riders fail 2.01x as often at the same station all year. Closing the cool-season 3W stockout gap to the 2W level would remove about **7,800 3W stockouts per cool season** (Jul-24 to Mar-25: 7,778; 9,181 across all 12 cool months in the data).
5. **CX / app: show live charged-pack availability and an expected wait, and offer redirection to a stocked station once a rider has waited about 8 minutes.** The abandonment hazard climbs from 0.3% per minute (minute 5) to 2.2% (minute 8) and 4.6% (minute 10).
   - Staff support for May: station-linked tickets ran 13.29 per 1K attempts in May-24 vs 5.58 in Jan-24 (all tickets 14.12 vs 6.04).
   - Route `low_range` and `other` ticket growth to the battery team (Q4).
6. **Analytics / leadership: report failures as same-station, same-month change and as excess over the 3.9% baseline**, never as the raw network average, which new low-failure Gen3 stations dilute. Show excess against the Apr-Jun denominator.

**Cross-question links:**
- **Q1:** failure "growth" is seasonal and mix. Failures are lost transactions, not deferred ones.
- **Q3:** generation effect and thermal derating; wave vs generation.
- **Q4:** low-range ticket growth.
- **Q5:** the pilot PEAK window (12-22h) holds 76.3% of attempts and 77.7% of failures. Pune has a Gen1 summer excess (RR 1.72), so the pricing DiD must handle Apr-Jun heat.
- **Q6:** a failed first attempt does not lower 30-day return (99.8% vs 99.8%). Test longer horizons before blaming failures for churn.
