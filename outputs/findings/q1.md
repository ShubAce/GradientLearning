# Q1 - Network performance over time (Jan-2024 to Jun-2025)

Script: `analysis/q1_network_performance.py` (runs in about 5 s; full printed output in `outputs/tables/q1_run_log.txt`).
All rupee margin levels are **under our usage-based battery-depreciation assumption** (70% end of life (EOL), no salvage) unless stated.
Margin trends are anchored in windows with **no pack issued below 70% EOL** (every month up to Mar-25); see finding 9 for why.

## Headline
The metrics diverge. Year on year (H1-25 vs H1-24), swaps grew 2.43x and revenue 2.63x, and the same-season failure rate did not get worse (7.04% → 6.06%). But cm1 per swap fell. Same season, it went from **+₹0.27 (Q1-24) to −₹7.32 (Q1-25)**. Against the post-price-rise peak, it went from +₹6.75 (Jul-Aug-24) to −₹7.53 (Oct-24 to Mar-25). The Kyron battery packs' extra wear explains more than the whole fall: **−₹16.0 per swap, about ₹4.0–4.3M a month**.

## Key findings
1. **The metrics move in different directions** (fig q1_01, q1_02).
   - **Volume and revenue went up.** Same season, H1-25 vs H1-24: attempts 2.40x, completed swaps 2.43x, revenue 2.63x, monthly active riders 2.18x. From Jan-24 to Jun-25: swaps 2.93x, revenue 3.18x (₹6.20M → ₹19.69M a month), active stations 90 → 149. Indexed to the H1-24 average (= 100), Jun-25 stands at swaps 260, revenue 282 and active riders 242.
   - **Reliability moved with the season, not the trend.** The service-failure rate is about 3.9% from July to March and peaks every summer: May-24 11.93%, May-25 9.54%. The summer spikes are stockouts (May-24: 7.82% of attempts) and abandoned queues (3.81%). System errors (0.27–0.32%) and rider cancellations (0.39–0.46%, reported separately) stay flat all year.
   - **Unit margin went down.**
     - cm1 per swap: +₹0.64 (H1-24) → +₹6.75 (Jul-Aug-24) → −₹7.53 (Oct-24 to Mar-25). Same season: +₹0.27 (Q1-24) → −₹7.32 (Q1-25).
     - cm2 per swap: −₹17.43 (H1-24) → −₹7.15 (Jul-Aug-24) → −₹19.16 (Oct-24 to Mar-25). Same season: −₹19.25 (Q1-24) → −₹18.48 (Q1-25).
     - Total cm2 worsened from −₹2.08M a month (Q1-24) to −₹4.96M (Q1-25): growth is scaling a negative unit margin.
     - H1-25 levels depend on how wear past EOL is treated (finding 9): cm1 −₹6.39 under the per-cycle model, −₹1.13 if post-EOL swaps carry Cellora wear, +₹1.19 if wear stops at 70% EOL.
2. **"Service failures rose faster than swaps" is a seasonal artefact** (fig q1_03, `q1_failure_growth_comparisons.csv`).
   - The naive comparison, Jan-24 (winter) vs Jun-25 (peak season), shows failures 6.26x vs swaps 2.93x, with the rate going 4.0% → 8.2%.
   - Every same-season comparison reverses that:

     | Comparison | Failures | Swaps | Failure rate |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | Q1-24 vs Q1-25 | 2.43x | 2.48x | 3.98% → 3.89% |
     | Q2-24 vs Q2-25 | 1.94x | 2.38x | 9.52% → 7.92% |
     | H1-24 vs H1-25 | 2.07x | 2.43x | 7.04% → 6.06% |
     | Jun-24 vs Jun-25 | 1.88x | 2.32x | 9.85% → 8.16% |
3. **The same-season improvement is a mix effect (Simpson's paradox): the 90 Launch stations as a group did not improve** (+0.03 pp; 49 of 90 did not get worse, median −0.12 pp) (fig q1_04).
   - The Q2 network rate fell −1.61 pp. The paired station-cluster bootstrap 95% CI is −2.08 to −1.12 pp; the unpaired one, which treats the two years as independent, is wider (−3.02 to −0.25). The change splits exactly into:
     - within the Launch stations: +0.02 pp;
     - mix effect of the new stations: −1.62 pp. New stations carry 36.6% of Q2-25 attempts and fail at 5.08%, against 9.55% at Launch stations.
   - Fixed panel of the same 90 Launch stations:
     - Q2 rate 9.52% → 9.55% (+0.03 pp, paired bootstrap CI −0.22 to +0.26 pp), while handling 48.4% more Q2 attempts.
     - 41 of 90 stations got worse and 49 did not; median station change −0.12 pp (Wilcoxon p = 0.045). That is statistically detectable but operationally nil.
     - May was 11.9% in both years at these stations (11.93% in 2024, 11.89% in 2025).
   - Q1 tells the same story at a low level: network 3.98% → 3.89% (paired CI −0.16 to −0.01 pp), Launch panel −0.10 pp with 84.5% more attempts.
4. **New stations fail less because none of them have Gen1 chargers, not because they are lightly loaded** (fig q1_04, `q1_failure_by_charger_generation.csv`, `q1_q2_failure_by_city_generation_group.csv`).
   - The brief's hypothesis was that new stations are low-utilisation. That is not true: in Q2-25 they handle 4.8 attempts per slot-day vs 5.0 at Launch stations.
   - Q2 failure rate by charger generation:

     | Group | Q2-24 | Q2-25 |
     |---|---|---|
     | Launch Gen1 (51 stations) | 12.64% | 12.41% |
     | Launch Gen2 (39) | 5.25% | 5.23% |
     | New Gen2 (23) | — | 5.87% |
     | New Gen3 (37) | — | 4.69% |

     In Q1 every group is 3.85–4.08%.
   - Like-for-like cells (same city and generation) match: Delhi Gen2 8.5% (Launch) vs 8.4% (new); Hyderabad Gen2 7.2% vs 7.2%; Pune Gen2 5.1% vs 5.2%.
   - The **51 Gen1 Launch stations are 34% of stations but 60% of Q2-25 service failures** (46,376 of 77,445). The 36 Gen1 stations in Delhi NCR, Jaipur and Hyderabad alone account for 53%, at a 15.2% failure rate.
   - Within the same city, Gen1 fails at roughly twice the Gen2 rate in the hot cities (Q2-25: Delhi 16.64% vs 8.44%, Jaipur 16.57% vs 8.60%, Hyderabad 12.88% vs 7.22%).
   - Gen3 (all new sites) beats Gen2 by about 3 pp in the hot cities: Delhi 5.30% vs 8.44%, Hyderabad 4.66% vs 7.22%, Jaipur 5.25% vs 8.60%. Elsewhere the gap is small (Bengaluru 4.08% vs 4.24%, Mumbai 4.00% vs 4.03%, Pune 4.30% vs 5.15%).
5. **No hidden deterioration at the busiest stations** (fig q1_08, `q1_failure_by_demand_decile.csv`).
   - Within every Q2-25 demand decile, Launch stations fail more than new ones (D9: 12.5% vs 4.5%; D10: 10.7% vs 4.8%).
   - Launch panel grouped by 2024 demand quintile: the busiest quintile went 11.69% → 11.42% with 66% more attempts. Every quintile moved by less than ±0.5 pp.
   - Across the 90 Launch stations, demand growth and failure-rate change are unrelated (Spearman ρ = −0.01, p = 0.95).
   - The positive decile gradient in Q2-25 (ρ = 0.71) is a mix effect: the low-demand deciles are 53–73% new stations.
6. **A failed attempt is mostly a lost swap, not a delayed one.**
   - Of the 77,445 Q2-25 service failures, only 5.2% were followed by the same rider's completed swap (at any station) within 60 minutes, 11.3% within 3 hours and 58.3% within 24 hours; 2.0% had no completed swap in the rest of Q2-25.
   - Upper bound on Q2-25 revenue lost to failures: ₹5.03M (77,445 failures × ₹64.89 revenue per swap).
7. **Queue wait of completed swaps carries no load signal in this data** (fig q1_02, `q1_queue_wait_load_check.csv`).
   - The mean is 4.05–4.07 min and the p90 7.05–7.10 min in every month, including May, when about 12% of attempts fail (Q2: 4.061 → 4.060 min).
   - It is equally flat by hour of day: 4.054–4.083 min across the hours carrying at least 1% of swaps.
   - It is flat even in the most stressed cell. In May-25, Gen1 stations in Delhi NCR, Jaipur and Hyderabad failed 19.0% of attempts, yet completed swaps waited 4.056 min. Every other city × generation cell (failure rates 4.1–12.8%) waited 4.036–4.097 min.
   - Abandoned riders' waits are flat too: 11.57–11.82 min in every month.
   - Survivorship (stressed riders abandon rather than wait) is one possible explanation, but it is not demonstrated: the field shows no load response in any slice. Either way it cannot serve as a service KPI. The recommendation (use attempt-based stockout and abandonment rates) is unchanged.
8. **The CM bridge: Kyron wear erased the price-rise gain** (fig q1_05, q1_06, `q1_cm_bridge.csv`). The decomposition is exact (components sum to the change). All three windows are free of post-EOL issues.

   | Driver (₹ per swap; ₹M a month at end-period volume) | Leg 1: H1-24 → Jul-Aug-24 (cm1 +6.11) | Leg 2: Jul-Aug-24 → Oct-24 to Mar-25 (cm1 −14.28) | Same season: Q1-24 → Q1-25 (cm1 −7.59) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Base price rise (2W +₹5, 3W +₹10) | +5.16 (+0.80M) | 0.00 | +5.12 (+1.37M) |
   | Kyron share of issued packs (wear) | +0.01 | **−16.00 (−3.99M)** | **−16.04 (−4.31M)** |
   | ZipDrop discount 12% → 28% | −0.00 | −1.45 (−0.36M) | −1.70 (−0.46M) |
   | Peak/off-peak pilot, gross | +0.00 | +1.69 (+0.42M) | +1.68 (+0.45M) |
   | Fewer kWh per swap (ageing packs) | +0.71 (+0.11M) | +1.31 (+0.33M) | +2.80 (+0.75M) |
   | Segment mix | +0.14 | +0.19 | +0.46 |
   | Grid tariff / within-supplier wear / other partner discounts | +0.06 / +0.05 / −0.01 | −0.05 / +0.01 / +0.01 | +0.00 / +0.09 / −0.00 |

   - **cm2.** Same season Q1, station fixed cost per swap fell ₹19.52 → ₹11.16 (+₹8.36, about ₹2.24M a month, from utilisation). So cm2 per swap stayed roughly flat (−₹19.25 → −₹18.48) even though cm1 fell ₹7.59. Leg 2: fixed cost ₹13.90 → ₹11.63, cm2 −₹7.15 → −₹19.16.
   - **Kyron share.** The 2.8–2.9% Kyron share of issued packs up to Aug-24 is entirely 3W packs; the Kyron share of 2W issues was 0.0% until Aug-24, 18.1% in Sep-24 and 23.9–24.9% afterwards. Wear per swap went ₹37.53 (Jul-Aug-24) → ₹53.07 (Oct-24 to Mar-25).
   - **Reference wear per swap.** Kyron 2W ₹103.9 vs Cellora 2W ₹31.9 and Amptek 2W ₹31.9 (3.26x). Kyron 3W costs ₹107.2 vs ₹76.2 for Cellora 3W, so Kyron 3W is also costlier per swap, despite the brief's "Kyron 3W behaves like the others".
   - **Within-supplier change.** The within-supplier wear change is about ₹0, because the cost model charges a constant wear per pack. Pack ageing shows up in energy per swap instead (finding 11).
   - **H1-25 and Q2-25 bridges, for transparency.** They are not clean: Jul-Aug-24 → H1-25 is −₹13.14 with a Kyron term of −₹15.82 (−₹4.49M a month), and Q2-24 → Q2-25 is −₹6.51 with Kyron −₹15.61. Both Kyron terms include wear charged on packs already past EOL (₹7.58 per swap in H1-25, ₹14.39 in Q2-25). Under the two EOL-consistent treatments, the Kyron excess-wear change Jul-Aug-24 → H1-25 is −₹10.50 per swap (about ₹2.98M a month; post-EOL swaps at Cellora wear) or −₹8.19 (about ₹2.32M; wear stops at EOL).
9. **The cost model keeps charging wear after a pack passes end of life, so H1-25 and Q2-25 margin levels are not robust** (fig q1_07, `q1_post_eol_wear_variants.csv`).
   - The per-cycle wear (purchase cost × SoH loss per cycle / (initial SoH − 70)) is charged on every issue, including packs already below 70%. As a result, Kyron 2W packs were charged **1.30x their purchase cost** over the window: 401.7 cycles issued vs 308.9 cycles to 70% EOL. Cellora 2W packs are at 0.64x (643 of 1,007 cycles).
   - **122,204 Kyron swaps in Apr–Jun-25 issued a pack already below 70% SoH.** That is 6.1% of Kyron issues in Apr, 70.2% in May and 90.1% in Jun. There were none before Apr-25, and no non-Kyron pack was ever issued below 70%.
   - Those charges add ₹7.58 per network swap to H1-25 wear (₹2.15M a month), ₹14.39 to Q2-25 (₹4.30M a month) and ₹23.08 to Jun-25 (₹7.00M a month).
   - Levels under the per-cycle model / post-EOL issues at Cellora wear (₹31.85 2W, ₹76.19 3W) / wear stops at EOL:

     | Window | cm1 per swap | cm2 per swap |
     |---|---|---|
     | H1-25 | −6.39 / −1.13 / +1.19 | −17.87 / −12.61 / −10.29 |
     | Q2-25 | −5.56 / +4.44 / +8.83 | −17.32 / −7.33 / −2.93 |
     | Jun-25 | −5.04 / +10.92 / +18.04 | −16.72 / −0.76 / +6.37 |

   - So the same-season Q2 decline (−₹6.51 per swap) **reverses** once post-EOL wear is treated consistently: +₹3.49 (Cellora wear) or +₹7.88 (wear stops at EOL) vs Q2-24's +₹0.95. H1-24 → H1-25 becomes −₹1.76 or +₹0.55. Q1-24 → Q1-25 (−₹7.59) and Jul-Aug-24 → Oct-24 to Mar-25 (−₹14.28) are identical under all three treatments.
10. **Battery Ops kept issuing packs past end of life, including after their recorded retirement** (`q1_range_by_returned_soh_band.csv`).
    - 32,903 Kyron 2W issues happened after the 2025-06-15 retirement date recorded in the battery master. Their mean issued SoH was 61.7%, and the last issue was on 2025-06-30. Another 858 Kyron 3W issues came after retirement, at 69.1%.
    - Worn packs deliver less range. In H1-25, riders returning a pack at 55–60% SoH had ridden 34.9 km since their last swap, and 36.9 km at 60–65%, vs 57.0 km at 85–90% (association).
    - Battery IDs are not physically tracked (brief), so part of this may be a master-data problem rather than physical packs. Either way, a check on SoH at the point of issue would have blocked it.
11. **The falling energy cost per swap reflects pack ageing, not efficiency; riders pay much more per km.**
    - Jan-24 → Jun-25, per 2W swap: energy 2.069 → 1.571 kWh (−24.1%), while the health (SoH) of returned packs fell 98.9% → 75.4%. Charge left at return stayed flat (9.31% → 9.26%).
    - Km since the last swap fell 70.5 → 48.0 (−32.0%). Within H1-25, kWh and km both rise with the returned pack's SoH band (1.51 kWh / 34.9 km at 55–60% vs 1.78 kWh / 57.0 km at 85–90%). This is an association. The per-supplier link is weaker: returned Kyron packs took 1.670 kWh vs 1.701 kWh for Cellora (H1-25).
    - **2W price per km** (amount charged / km since last swap) rose from ₹0.791 (Jan-24) to ₹1.265 (Jun-25), +59.9%. Same season, H1-24 ₹0.819 → H1-25 ₹1.165 (+42.3%), far more than the ₹5 list-price rise.
    - **The rest of the fleet is ageing in step.** The 4,133 non-Kyron 2W packs averaged 80.0% SoH on 30-Jun-25 (53% at or below 80%, none at or below 75%). Returned non-Kyron 2W SoH fell 86.5% (Feb-25) → 80.8% (Jun-25), −1.43 points a month. At that pace (a linear extrapolation), the fleet reaches 70% in about 8 months (around Feb-2026), nearly all packs at once.
12. **Counterfactual: Kyron packs with Cellora-like wear** (fig q1_07, `q1_kyron_counterfactual.csv`).
    - **Forward per-swap cost gap** between Kyron-grade and Cellora-grade packs at about 25% of issues, measured in windows with no post-EOL issue:
      - Q1-25: +₹16.82 per swap (₹4.52M a month). cm1 would be +₹9.51 instead of −₹7.32, and cm2 −₹1.66 instead of −₹18.48.
      - Oct-24 to Mar-25: +₹16.79 per swap (₹4.18M a month).
    - The H1-25 per-cycle comparison (+₹16.63, cm1 +₹10.24 vs −₹6.39) is **not** the H1-25 margin gap, because that "actual" contains post-EOL charges. Against an EOL-consistent actual, the uplift is +₹9.05 per swap (₹2.57M a month; wear stops at EOL) to +₹11.36 (₹3.22M a month; post-EOL issues at Cellora wear).
    - Second-order energy offset: −₹0.17 per swap (net H1-25 counterfactual cm1 about +₹10.07).
    - The cap understates the economic cost of running packs past EOL. In Jun-25 it makes the actual (+₹18.04) look better than an all-Cellora-wear fleet (+₹11.41), although those packs delivered 35–37 km per swap. That is why the Cellora-wear treatment is the better economic comparator.

## Primary vs secondary drivers
- **Margin erosion.**
  - Primary: Kyron 2W battery wear, −₹16.0 per swap (−₹16.00 Jul-Aug-24 → Oct-24 to Mar-25; −₹16.04 Q1-24 → Q1-25). Kyron 2W costs 3.26x Cellora wear per swap and made up 24–25% of 2W issues from Oct-24.
  - Secondary: the ZipDrop discount amendment, −₹1.45 to −₹1.70 per swap.
  - Offsets:
    - the Jul-24 base price rise (+₹5.1);
    - the pilot's net surcharge (+₹1.7, gross);
    - lower energy per swap (+₹1.3 to +₹2.8, an ageing effect);
    - lower fixed cost per swap (+₹2.3 to +₹8.4, utilisation; cm2 only).
  - Segment mix (2W/3W, partner, city) is small (+₹0.19 to +₹0.46).
  - From Apr-25 the reported margin also carries wear charged on packs past EOL (₹14.39 per swap in Q2-25). That is partly a cost-model artefact and partly a real operating failure: the packs should not have been issued (finding 10).
- **Service failures.**
  - Primary: the Apr–Jun season, concentrated at Gen1 stations in hot cities (association).
  - There is no upward trend: same-season rates at fixed stations are unchanged, and the network "improvement" is the mix effect of new Gen2/Gen3 stations.

## Method notes
- **Data.** Clean event table (`events.parquet`, test stations excluded, timestamps fixed) scanned lazily in narrow column sets, plus `station_month.parquet` (attempt counts match the event scan exactly) and `batteries.parquet`.
- **Definitions.**
  - All per-swap figures are ratio-of-sums (swap-weighted).
  - Service failure = stockout + abandoned + system error; cancellations are reported separately.
  - Active rider or station = at least one completed swap in the month.
  - **Post-EOL issue** = a completed swap whose issued pack had `soh_out_pct` < 70 (`EOL_SOH`).
  - Clean windows (no post-EOL issue): every month up to Mar-25, including Q1-24, Q1-25, Jul-Aug-24 and Oct-24 to Mar-25.
- **Indexing.** The divergence chart indexes scale metrics to the H1-24 monthly average. cm1 per swap is not indexed (its base is about ₹0), so it is shown in rupees as the gap between revenue and variable cost per swap.
- **Failure tests.**
  - Exact decomposition of the network rate change: Δr = w_Launch × Δr_Launch + Σ_new w_g × (r_g − r_Launch,2024).
  - Station-cluster bootstrap (2,000 resamples) for the network change. Paired version: Launch stations resampled with both years (the 2024 network is exactly these 90) and new stations resampled separately (seed 59501). The unpaired version (seed 59500) is kept for comparison.
  - Paired station bootstrap and Wilcoxon signed-rank for the 90-station Launch panel; Spearman for demand growth vs failure change.
  - Demand deciles ranked within each period on attempts per active station-day, plus a fixed Launch panel grouped by 2024 demand quintile (avoids re-ranking).
  - Charger generation × city like-for-like cells; city-controlled what-ifs with the same-city Gen2 rate, and the Gen3 rate as an upper bound.
  - **Failure recovery:** for each Q2-25 failure, the time to the same rider's next completed swap (riders sorted by time, next completed timestamp back-filled). Shares use all 77,445 failures as the denominator.
  - **Queue-wait load check:** completed and abandoned waits by month, hour of day and city × charger generation (May-25).
- **CM bridge.** Exact shift-share over 12 segments (vehicle class × billing segment {independent, ZipDrop, other partner} × pilot city).
  - Revenue uses an identity checked on every row: amount charged = list price − partner discount + pilot adjustment (+₹15 peak / −₹8 off-peak, applied to billable partners too).
  - Rate effects split into base price (net of the old discount rate), discount-rate change (ZipDrop vs other partners), pilot, energy (kWh per swap vs grid tariff), and wear (supplier share at reference wear per swap vs within-supplier change).
  - Mix = Σ Δw × the earlier period's segment cm1.
  - Components sum exactly (|residual| < 1e-9).
  - Rupees per month = per-swap effect × end-period monthly swaps.
- **Post-EOL variants.** (a) Cap: post-EOL issues carry no wear, so lifetime depreciation = purchase cost. (b) Replacement: post-EOL issues carry the full-window Cellora wear per swap of the same class.
- **Sensitivity.** Wear per cycle is recomputed per pack as purchase cost × (1 − salvage) × SoH loss per cycle / (initial SoH − EOL), joined to monthly issues by pack and post-EOL flag. The base scenario reproduces event-level wear exactly (max monthly difference ₹0.00).
- **Other.**
  - Lifetime shortfall = Kyron 2W purchase cost × (1 − Kyron cycles to EOL / Cellora cycles to EOL).
  - Price per km = Σ amount charged / Σ km since last swap over completed 2W swaps with a valid km reading.

## Caveats & traps handled
- **Seasonality.** The naive start-vs-end comparison is shown and rejected; all YoY tests compare the same months.
- **Simpson's paradox / composition.** Network averages are split into the Launch panel and new stations, by charger generation, and by same-city cells.
- **Survivorship.** Queue wait is measured only on completed swaps, so attempt-based rates are used instead. The survivorship mechanism itself is not demonstrated (finding 7).
- **Cost-model internal consistency.**
  - The shared per-cycle wear keeps charging packs past 70% EOL. Margin trends are therefore anchored in windows with no post-EOL issue, and H1-25/Q2-25 levels are always quoted under all three treatments.
  - Under the model's linear per-pack wear, ageing cannot raise wear per swap, so the within-supplier term is about zero by construction.
  - Levels depend on the wear assumption (the sign flips under generous assumptions), so trends are emphasised and a sensitivity table is provided.
- **Master data.** Battery IDs are not physically tracked, and `batteries.csv` dates are unreliable (brief). The after-retirement issues may be partly a data problem. The SoH at issue is per event and internally consistent.
- **Association, not causation.**
  - Charger generation is confounded with commissioning cohort and site. The like-for-like city cells reduce but do not remove this; Gen3 sites are all new. A controlled upgrade pilot is needed.
  - The pilot effect is gross of any volume response; Q5 runs the difference-in-differences.
  - The Kyron counterfactual ignores the range effect: healthier packs would give more km per swap and therefore fewer swaps.
  - km and kWh by SoH band are associations within one period.
- **Extrapolation.** The fleet-ageing date is a linear extrapolation of the returned-pack SoH trend; actual degradation depends on cycles per pack.
- **Upper bounds.** ₹5.03M is an upper bound on lost revenue: 58.3% of failing riders swapped again within 24 hours, so some of that revenue was only delayed.
- **Allocation.** Fixed cost is allocated per station-month and the bridge treats it at network level. Monthly rupee impacts use end-period volume.
- **Statistical vs practical significance.** Wilcoxon p ≈ 0.04 on Launch stations reflects changes of about 0.1 pp, which have no operational meaning.

## Recommendations
1. **Procurement + Battery Ops (COO/CFO): replace the retired Kyron 2W packs only with Cellora/Amptek-grade packs. Gate any new supplier on its measured health loss per cycle within its first 2–3 months.**
   - Expected impact, as a forward cost gap: about +₹16.8 per swap at a 25% share of issues, **₹4.2–4.5M a month** at Oct-24 to Mar-25 / Q1-25 volume. Q1-25 cm1 would have been +₹9.51 instead of −₹7.32, and cm2 −₹1.66 instead of −₹18.48 (base assumption).

3. **Network Ops: before the Apr-2026 summer, upgrade or augment the Gen1 stations in Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur.** These 36 stations account for 53% of Q2-25 failures.

## Cross-question links
- **Q2 (failures):** failures are seasonal and concentrated at Gen1 stations in hot cities. Queue wait carries no load signal, and failures are mostly lost swaps.
- **Q3 (stations):** the charger-generation gap appears only in summer, and new stations are not lighter-loaded. Gen3 beats Gen2 by about 3 pp in hot cities.
- **Q4 (batteries):** Kyron wear per swap is 3.26x (2W). Kyron 3W also costs more per swap (₹107.2 vs ₹76.2). Packs were issued past EOL and after retirement. Fleet SoH decline drives energy and km per swap down, and the non-Kyron fleet nears EOL around Feb-2026.
- **Q5 (pricing):** pilot +₹0.48M a month gross; ZipDrop −₹0.47M a month.
- **Q6 (retention):** riders get less range per swap; price per km is up 42.3% YoY.
- **Budget proposals:** the evidence favours better batteries, an EOL quarantine and replacement planning, and Gen1 upgrades. It does not favour more batteries or more stations for load relief.
