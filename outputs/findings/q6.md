# Q6 - Root cause of new-rider retention

## Headline
New-rider retention **did not fall structurally** (Jan-Mar 2024 vs Jan-Mar 2025 cohorts: 86.3% vs 86.3% still swapping in days 31-60, p=0.98). The visible "decline" is a **dip for summer-entry cohorts**, and **early (day 0-13) service failures, the largest measured and actionable driver, explain about 40-50% of it** (40% of the 4.58 pp summer-2024 dip in the decomposition; 43-52% by logit shrinkage and direct standardisation). Riders who hit **2+ service failures in their first 14 days** have 22.6% non-retention vs 13.1% (n=2,246; adjusted OR, 2+ vs 0-1, **1.67 [1.43-1.94]**).

## Key findings
1. **There is no structural decline once seasonality and censoring are handled.** Outcome = completed swap in days 31-60 after the first attempt. 13,797 eligible new riders; overall 85.4% retained and 14.6% (2,017) not retained.
   * Same season, D31-60:
     * Jan-Mar 2024 cohorts 86.3% (n=1,750) vs Jan-Mar 2025 86.3% (n=3,046), +0.02 pp, p=0.98.
     * Jan-Apr 2024 85.2% (n=2,410) vs Jan-Apr 2025 85.1% (n=4,154), -0.15 pp, p=0.87.
     * Apr 2024 82.4% (n=660) vs Apr 2025 81.7% (n=1,108), p=0.69.
   * May and June 2025 cohorts cannot be scored on D31-60 yet, so we compared them year on year on shorter outcomes:
     * R14 (swap in days 15-28): May-24 89.4% (n=717) vs May-25 91.2% (n=1,304), p=0.19.
     * D1-7: flat in every calendar month (97.7-99.1%, all year-on-year p >= 0.16).
   * The "decline" appears only when cohorts are compared across seasons. Jan-Mar 2024 vs Apr 2025 gives -4.6 pp (p=0.0009). That is the same size as the 2024 summer dip (Jan-Mar vs Apr-Jun 2024: 86.3% to 81.7%, -4.58 pp, p=0.0001).
   * A naive "any swap after day 30" metric that ignores follow-up falls from 89.7% (Jan-24) to 77.5% (May-25) and 0.0% (Jun-25). This is pure censoring. *(Fig q6_01)*
2. **Survival analysis says the same.** Kaplan-Meier, churn = 30+ idle days, administrative censoring.
   * 90-day survival by cohort: 2024Q1 72.5%, 2024Q2 66.2%, 2024Q4 73.0%, 2025Q1 70.5%.
   * Log-rank tests: 2024Q1 vs 2025Q1 p=0.41; 2024Q1 vs 2024Q2 p=0.0002.
   * Summer-entry cohorts are alike in both years: 2024Q2 vs 2025Q2 p=0.96; 60-day survival 73.2% vs 74.1%.
   * Churn is not always final: 2,169 of 7,437 churners (29.2%) came back later. *(Fig q6_02)*
3. **Cohort retention moves with early failure exposure, but this is mostly seasonal.**
   * Across 16 monthly cohorts, corr(mean early failures, retention) = -0.89 (p<0.0001). This is an ecological correlation, and most of it is season. After removing quarter-of-year means it is r = -0.54 (p=0.032).
   * The failure rate new riders faced in their first 14 days was 7.6% / 11.1% / 8.6% for the Apr / May / Jun-2024 cohorts and 6.8% for Apr-2025, against 3.7-4.8% in other months. The cohorts with the lowest retention are May-24 (79.1%) and Apr-25 (81.7%). *(Fig q6_01)*
4. **Dose-response: the 1st early failure adds a little risk, the 2nd roughly doubles it.** Non-retention by service failures in days 0-13:

   | Failures | Not retained | Riders |
   |---|---|---|
   | 0 | 13.3% | 7,350 |
   | 1 | 12.8% | 4,201 |
   | 2 | 22.6% | 1,497 |
   | 3+ | 22.6% | 749 |

   * **The pooled 0-vs-1 inversion is a Simpson's paradox caused by exposure.** Riders who leave early make few attempts, so they have little chance to collect failures. 400 of the 472 riders with only 1-5 early attempts had 0 failures, and 65.7% of those 472 were not retained.
   * **Within early-activity bands** (non-retention at 0 / 1 / 2 / 3+ failures):

     | Early attempts | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3+ |
     |---|---|---|---|---|
     | 6-9 | 26.7% | 25.3% | 49.5% | 47.5% |
     | 10-14 | 9.1% | 11.5% | 22.2% | 22.6% |
     | 15-19 | 8.6% | 11.0% | 17.9% | 18.2% |
     | 20+ | 8.2% | 8.2% | 17.5% | 24.6% |

     The 2nd failure roughly doubles non-retention in every band. The 1st failure adds about 2.5 pp in the two largest bands and nothing in the others.
   * **Activity-adjusted logit** (Model A + log early attempts), OR vs 0 failures: 1 failure **1.23 [1.08-1.41]**, 2 failures **2.47 [2.03-3.01]**, 3+ **2.34 [1.87-2.92]**. Without the activity control: 0.90 / 1.67 / 1.41.
   * **Failure-rate model** (share of early attempts failed, with log attempts), OR vs no failures: 0-5% 0.94 [0.66-1.33], 5-10% 1.21 [1.05-1.40], 10-20% 2.10 [1.76-2.51], >20% 2.93 [2.30-3.72]. This is monotone in the failure rate. It also needs the activity control: without it, the low-rate bins look protective (0.48, 0.84).
   * **Headline exposure:** 2,246 riders (16.3%) had 2+ failures; their non-retention is 22.6% vs 13.1% for riders with 0-1 (1.73x); adjusted OR (2+ vs 0-1) 1.67 [1.43-1.94]. Counting only stockouts and abandoned queues: 23.5% vs 13.1% (n=2,067), OR 1.76 [1.51-2.06].
   * **By type:** 2+ stockouts gives 24.4% (n=1,161) vs 13.4% with none; 2+ abandoned queues gives 24.4% (n=365) vs 13.9%. System errors do not matter: riders with 1 system error had 11.7% non-retention (n=563). *(Fig q6_04)*
5. **Attempt-level trigger evidence.** Across 2,350,544 new-rider attempts there were 8,117 exits (no further attempt for 30+ days). Exits per 1,000 attempts:
   * by what just happened: 3.34 after a completed swap, **5.56 after a stockout**, **6.10 after an abandoned queue**, 3.15 after a system error, 2.79 after a cancellation;
   * by failures in the previous 7 days: 0 gives 3.09, 1 gives 4.01, 2 gives 5.52, 3+ gives 6.41.

   The **pooled** grouped GLM (no rider fixed effect) adjusts for tenure, month, vehicle class, segment, competitor and pack supplier:
   * stockout OR 1.50 [1.36-1.66] and abandoned queue OR 1.66 [1.46-1.89];
   * system error OR 0.93 [0.61-1.42] and cancellation OR 0.83 [0.57-1.20], both not significant;
   * prior failures in 7 days: 1 gives OR 1.29, 2 gives 1.75, 3+ gives 2.06;
   * exits are most likely in days 0-13 (OR 0.81 for days 14-59, 0.63 for days 60-179).

   **A truly within-rider check** (Mantel-Haenszel, each rider compares only with themselves) gives an exit OR of 1.525 [1.41-1.66] right after a stockout or abandoned queue. With rider x month strata it is 1.25 [1.15-1.37]. So the trigger effect is real but smaller once matched closely. *(Fig q6_04, q6_03)*
6. **Multivariable logit** for D31-60 non-retention: cohort-month and city fixed effects, clustered by main station; n=13,797. Km per swap and log attempts are centred within vehicle class.
   * Significant adverse effects:
     * 2 vs 0 early failures: OR 1.67 [1.37-2.02]
     * 3+ vs 0: OR 1.41 [1.14-1.75]
     * each stockout: OR 1.17 [1.09-1.26]
     * each abandoned queue: OR 1.16 [1.05-1.28]
     * first attempt failed: OR 1.28 [1.04-1.59]. Not robust: with the activity control it is 1.09 [0.88-1.34].
     * 3W vehicle: OR 1.18 [1.01-1.37], p=0.037. With km per swap dropped it is 1.16 [1.00-1.36], p=0.051; with the activity control 1.07 [0.91-1.25]. Raw 17.2% vs 14.2%.
   * Not adverse:
     * Kyron share (+25 pp): OR 0.90 [0.82-1.00]
     * PEAK share: OR 1.06 [0.96-1.18]
     * mean queue wait: OR 1.05 [0.96-1.14]
     * unresolved ticket: OR 0.90 [0.59-1.39]
     * competitor: OR 1.05 [0.80-1.37]
     * ZipDrop vs other partners: OR 1.06 [0.91-1.24]
     * independent PAYG: OR 0.87 [0.67-1.13]
     * station wave, charger generation, connectivity, channel, KYC and age: all not significant
     * no individual fleet partner differs from FeastFly (all p>0.16)
     * SoH of packs received: OR 1.07 [0.70-1.63] per +10 SoH points
   * **Battery as a natural test.** Packs got much worse for later cohorts while same-season retention stayed flat. From the Jan-24 to the Apr-25 cohort, mean SoH of packs received fell 98.7 to 82.0, km per swap 74.7 to 56.4, and Kyron share rose 3.7% to 23.5%. Battery is not a retention lever (cross-link Q4: Kyron is a wear-cost and margin problem).
   * **Failure effect range.** For 2 vs 0 failures the OR is 1.67 without and 2.47 with the activity control. Early activity is partly a consequence of failures, so the true effect is probably within this range.
   * Segment terms are sensitive to specification under the activity control: ZipDrop OR 1.57 [1.32-1.86], independents 0.62-0.68. Activity levels differ structurally by segment, so these are not used for classification.
   * Robustness on R90 (no swap in days 91-120): 2 failures OR 1.26 [1.09-1.45]. *(Fig q6_03)*
7. **Long-run survival (landmark Cox at day 14):** n=13,447, 6,394 events, strata = entry season x city.
   * 2 failures HR 1.24 [1.14-1.34]; 3+ HR 1.22 [1.09-1.36]; 1 failure HR 1.04 (not significant).
   * Competitor HR 1.20 [1.01-1.43]; 3W HR 1.11 [1.03-1.20]. Kyron, peak share, km per swap and queue wait are null.
   * Share still active 180 days after the landmark: 56.8% (0 failures), 54.6% (1), 48.4% (2), 48.3% (3+). Log-rank p=6.3e-13. *(Fig q6_05)*
8. **The failure effect is stable.** OR for 2+ vs 0-1 early failures is above 1 in **15 of 15 subgroups** and significant in 14; pooled OR 1.68 [1.49-1.90].
   * Examples: independent 1.47, partner 1.78, 2W 1.63, 3W 1.86, ZipDrop 1.96, H1-2024 cohorts 1.64, H2-2024 1.78, 2025 1.52, winter-entry cohorts 1.46.
   * The only null is Jaipur (OR 1.01 [0.73-1.40]). Jaipur riders face the most early failures (0.96 per rider vs 0.53-0.89 elsewhere). One untested explanation is that failures are so common there that they separate riders less. *(Fig q6_08)*
9. **How much of the summer dip do early failures explain? About 40-50%.** Four methods bracket it for summer 2024 (Apr-Jun vs Jan-Mar 2024, observed gap +4.58 pp):
   * **Decomposition** (pooled logit without cohort effects; counterfactual block swaps): the model predicts +2.18 pp, of which early failures account for **+1.81 pp = 40% of the observed dip** (83% of the predicted dip).
   * **Logit shrinkage:** the summer coefficient shrinks 43% when early failure groups are added, and 51% when log attempts are also added.
   * **Direct standardisation** (no model): the failure mix explains +2.11 to +2.37 pp (46-52%).
   * **Failures up to day 29** (riders active in days 14-29, n=3,533): days 0-13 failures explain 54% and the failure rate in days 0-29 explains **66%**. Summer failures that land after the 14-day window explain part of the gap.

   For Apr 2025 (observed +4.61 pp), failures explain 28-30% (decomposition +1.31 pp; standardisation +1.35 to +1.38 pp).

   **Observed retention with the early-failure effect removed** (observed + counterfactual - model prediction) ranges over 81.6-88.6% (sd 1.85 pp), against 79.1-88.5% observed (sd 2.52 pp). The dip shrinks but does not vanish:

   | Cohort | Observed | Failure effect removed | Share of dip closed |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Apr-24 | 82.4% | 83.7% | 32% |
   | May-24 | 79.1% | 81.6% | 36% |
   | Jun-24 | 83.7% | 85.3% | 61% |
   | Apr-25 | 81.7% | 83.0% | 28% |

   The reference is Jan-Mar 2024 at 86.3%. The pooled model itself reproduces only a cohort sd of 0.93 pp; its pure counterfactual has sd 0.27 pp.
   * The decomposition's "battery" block (Kyron share, km per swap) adds +1.01 pp in Apr-25 and +0.48 pp in same-season Jan-Mar 2025. This comes from lower km per swap (pooled OR 0.91 per +10 km). The observed same-season gap is -0.02 pp, though, so this modelled effect does not show up in the data. Kyron share itself is protective (OR 0.889) and the SoH of packs received is null.
   * About 2.2 pp of the summer-2024 gap stays unexplained. *(Fig q6_01, q6_06)*
10. **Policy events show no robust retention effect.** Difference-in-differences on D31-60 non-retention:
    * base price (Jul-24), independent vs partner: OR 1.37 [1.01-1.85], p=0.042. This is weak and would fail a Bonferroni correction across the 4 tests (threshold 0.0125);
    * peak pilot, independent riders in BLR/PUN: OR 1.28 [0.91-1.79], p=0.15;
    * pilot placebo on partner riders (who do not pay the surcharge): OR 1.20, p=0.16;
    * ZipDrop amendment: OR 1.19 [0.89-1.61], p=0.24;
    * competitor exposure, 2025 cohorts: 14.7% (n=640) vs 15.0% (n=3,514) without exposure, p=0.85.
11. **Proxies and noise.**
    * Declared shift is uninformative: 41.3-42.9% of early attempts fall in 17-21h whatever shift the rider declared.
    * Vehicle model is a fleet and vehicle-class proxy: Cramer's V with partner 0.25; 56.3% of ZipDrop riders ride a Zipp E2. Adding vehicle model to the model gives LR chi2=3.7, df=6, p=0.71.
    * Km per swap mostly reflects pack size (2W 61.3 km vs 3W 92.0 km) and engagement: fewer, fuller swaps mean more km per swap. That is why it is centred within vehicle class in the models.
    * Early activity volume is a **symptom**, not a driver: riders with 1-5 attempts in their first 14 days have 65.7% non-retention (n=472), 6-9 attempts 29.4% (n=1,061), 10+ attempts 11-12%.
    * Gradient boosting (5-fold CV AUC 0.60 on drivers only; 0.68 with early activity as a control) ranks stockouts as the top driver after activity (mean |SHAP| 0.181; 2+ stockouts add +0.73 log-odds). Km per swap (0.177) is close behind; it is labelled as the pack-size and engagement proxy it is. *(Fig q6_07)*

## Method notes
* **Cohort.** New riders = signed up on or after 2024-01-01: 16,400 signups, 16,341 with at least 1 attempt. Day 0 = first attempt.
* **Primary outcome:** a completed swap in days 31-60. Riders are active almost daily (median gap between active days 1 day, p99 4 days), so a month with no swap is a clear sign of leaving. It needs 60 days of follow-up, so eligibility is first attempt before 2025-05-01 (13,797 riders).
* **Robustness outcomes.** R14 (days 15-28), R90 (days 91-120), D1-7, and survival (30+ idle days, with administrative censoring so a churn event can always be observed). The 3 riders who never completed a swap are kept as censored survivors; the verifier showed this is immaterial (HR 1.240 vs 1.238).
* **Features** come only from days 0-13, so they precede the outcome window. They are:
  * counts by failure type and the first-attempt outcome;
  * queue wait (completed swaps);
  * Kyron share, SoH and km of packs;
  * price and PEAK share;
  * competitor exposure;
  * tickets and unresolved tickets;
  * main-station attributes and the rider profile.
* **Centring within vehicle class.** Km per swap and log attempts are centred within vehicle class. When the 3W dummy is in the model this is an exact reparameterisation: every other coefficient is unchanged, and the 3W term becomes the total 3W effect at the class-typical value instead of at a 2W-typical km.
* **Models:**
  * Logit with cohort-month and city fixed effects and SEs clustered by main station. Sensitivities: log early attempts, failure-rate bins, alternative outcomes.
  * Pooled grouped binomial GLM on every attempt, plus within-rider Mantel-Haenszel ORs (rider and rider x month strata).
  * Landmark Kaplan-Meier and Cox models from day 14, avoiding immortal-time bias.
  * LightGBM + SHAP.
  * Within-subgroup logits for stability.
  * DiD for the price, pilot and ZipDrop events.
  * For the summer dip: counterfactual block-swap decomposition (Fairlie-style, 30 draws, joint Wald tests per block), logit shrinkage, direct standardisation, and an extended failure window among riders active in days 14-29.
* **Driver classification rule.**
  * PRIMARY = adverse and significant in all designs tested, plus a dose-response, plus 75% or more of subgroups in the same direction, plus at least 25% of the **observed** summer-2024 dip explained.
  * SECONDARY = adverse in at least one rider-level design (or in the attempt-level design for experience variables).
  * Rider attributes (3W, independent) are judged on rider-level designs only, because per-attempt exit rates are confounded by swap frequency.

## Caveats & traps handled
* **Seasonality.** Summer stockouts create the "decline". All trend claims use same-season comparisons and cohort fixed effects. A pooled H1-2024 vs Oct-24 to Apr-25 comparison would mix seasons (54.5% vs 14.9% summer-entry riders) and is not used.
* **Censoring.** A naive metric makes recent cohorts look lost (0.0% for Jun-25). We require 60 days of follow-up and administratively censor survival. Shorter outcomes (R14, D1-7) cover the May and June cohorts.
* **Simpson's paradox and exposure.**
  * Early leavers make few attempts and so collect few failures. That hides the effect of the 1st failure in pooled rates and biases count effects **downward**; for R14, the OR for 1 failure is 0.59, an exposure artefact.
  * The activity control corrects this, but early activity is partly a consequence of failures. So we report the range (2 vs 0: OR 1.67 to 2.47) rather than a single "true" effect.
  * Landmark KM avoids immortal-time bias.
* **Ecological correlation.** The cohort-level r = -0.89 is mostly seasonal (within-season r = -0.54). Rider-level and within-rider designs carry the causal argument, not the cohort correlation.
* **Small denominators.** Shares such as Kyron share and mean queue wait are noisy for riders with few swaps. That inflates their SHAP in the drivers-only GBM, and the effect disappears once activity is controlled.
* **Association, not causation.**
  * A stockout-recovery A/B test is needed to confirm the effect size.
  * About half of the summer dip, and most rider churn (AUC 0.60-0.68), is not explained by measured factors. So early failures are the primary *explainable and actionable* driver, not the whole story.
* **Multiple testing.** The 4 policy DiDs are reported with a Bonferroni note.
* **Data.** Uses the cleaned events (test stations removed, firmware timestamps fixed). Partner riders never pay PEAK (PARTNER tariff), so PEAK exposure is identified only from independent riders in BLR/PUN.

## Recommendations
1. **Head of Network Ops: a summer stockout plan focused on new riders (primary lever).** Before April, pre-position charged inventory and extra packs at the stations where new riders start, and fix the summer charging bottleneck (links to Q2/Q3, thermal derating).
   * Model what-if: if early failures are capped at 1, non-retention falls from 16.08% to 13.36% for summer-entry cohorts (-2.72 pp) and from 14.62% to 13.17% overall (-1.45 pp). This addresses roughly half of the summer dip, not all of it.
   * That is about 15 more retained riders per monthly cohort at 1,061 new riders/month, or about 418 extra swaps in each cohort's second month (27.1 swaps per retained rider in days 31-60). At Rs 64.0 revenue per swap, that is about Rs 26,762 per cohort in its second month, or Rs 321,149 a year counting the second month only. Retained riders keep swapping for months: 180 days after day 14, 56.8% are still active vs 48.4% with 2 failures.
   * The prize is volume more than margin. Under our usage-based battery-depreciation assumption, cm1 per swap is negative in 2025 (Q1), so retention gains add volume, not margin, until unit economics are fixed (Q4/Q5). The bigger reason to fix summer stockouts is that they also hit existing riders (Q2).
2. **Head of CX: a failure-recovery trigger for riders in their first 30 days, run as an A/B test.** On a stockout or abandoned queue, send an apology and credit, reserve a charged pack for the next swap, and route the rider to a nearby stocked station.
   * **Arms:** (a) trigger on the 2nd failure (2,821 riders reached a 2nd stockout or abandoned queue within 14 days over Jan-24 to Jun-25, median day 8.0); (b) trigger on the 1st (7,609 riders, median day 4.8); (c) control.
   * **Why the 2nd failure is the default.** It is a targeting choice for cost: 2,821 vs 7,609 riders, and the 2nd failure is where activity-adjusted odds roughly double (OR 2.47 vs 1.23 for the 1st). The 1st-failure arm tests whether earlier recovery pays.
   * **Volume to plan for:** new riders suffer about 449 stockout or abandon events per month in normal months, 1,119 per month in Apr-Jun, and 1,730 in May-25. Extending the window to day 29 matters: failures in days 14-29 add explanatory power (54% to 66% of the summer gap).
3. **Product: a charged-pack availability view or reservation for new riders.** This targets stockouts and abandoned queues directly. System errors and cancellations do not drive exits, so they are a lower priority for retention.
4. **CX analytics: an early-warning list.** Flag riders with fewer than 10 attempts by day 14: they have 29-66% non-retention vs 11-12% for 10+ attempts. Use the list for onboarding calls. Activity is a symptom, so pair the list with the failure history.
5. **Leadership / BI: retire the naive retention KPI.** Report D31-60 cohort retention with at least 60 days of follow-up and same-season comparisons, so summer dips are not read as structural decline.
6. **Do not spend the retention budget** on Kyron replacement, ticket handling, declared-shift targeting or vehicle-model segments.
   * Kyron has no retention effect (SoH OR 1.07, n.s.); its problem is wear cost and margin (Q4).
   * **Monitor** the 10 competitor stations (exits per attempt OR 1.42).
   * **Monitor** 3W onboarding (weak: OR 1.18 [1.01-1.37], Cox HR 1.11) as secondary.
   * A network-wide price rollout shows no robust retention penalty (pilot DiD p=0.15), but the CIs are wide and base-price sensitivity among independent riders is borderline (p=0.042). Links to Q5.
