# Q8 · Support-ticket text: does the free text add to the category and resolution fields?

Script: `analysis/q8_ticket_text.py` (runs in under 10 s) · Figures: `outputs/figures/q8_*.png` · Tables: `outputs/tables/q8_*.csv`
Scope: 43,380 tickets (44,000 raw, minus 86 outside Jan-24 to Jun-25 and 535 raised at the two test stations). Every ticket has a comment, and there are only 89 distinct comment strings.
Revised after verification (see the Verification section at the end).

## Headline
**Once the comments are read and checked against the event log, battery health is VoltRelay's largest complaint driver: about 21,458 of 43,380 tickets (49.5%; 53.5% in Apr–Jun 2025), against 39.5% for stock-outs and queues combined (41.1% in Apr–Jun 2025).** The official `low_range` category shows only 6,551 (15.1%). Two filing patterns hide the rest:
- The `other` bin (9,361 tickets, 21.6%) consists entirely of vehicle-phrased complaints. They contain no battery or range word, but they carry the battery fingerprint.
- `app_issue` (5,717, 13.2%) uses app wording. It carries the same fingerprint (about 97% battery-like by 3W mix), and the event log shows no app failures behind it.

The range is a floor of 36.7% (low_range plus vehicle-phrased `other`, 2.43x the official count) and an upper bound of 49.9% (adding all of `app_issue`, 3.30x). Elsewhere the text adds little. Priority, urgency words, "surcharge" wording and the absent competitor mentions carry no usable signal. The peak/off-peak pilot shows no billing-complaint effect once a like-for-like control city is used. CSAT survives its non-response bias (IPW moves it +0.002).

## Key findings
1. **Mis-filing is systematic, not random** (Fig 01).
   - Each of the 89 templates is filed under exactly one agent category.
   - Text and category disagree on 11,278 of 43,380 tickets (26.0%):
     - the whole `other` bin, 9,361 tickets (21.6%), is vehicle-phrased: "general issue with the vehicle performance", "bike problem lag raha hai overall", "scooter weak lag raha hai aajkal";
     - 1,917 QR-scan tickets (4.4%) sit inside `app_issue`.
   - Of the 15,912 tickets with range wording or vehicle wording, 13,403 (84.2%) blame the vehicle (scooter/bike/gaadi), not the battery.
   - 56.4% of tickets are Hinglish and 10.3% use typo variants (staion/battry/rnage).
   - **0 of 89 templates mention a competitor, switching or praise.**

2. **Structured evidence that the text cannot influence shows that `other` and `app_issue` are battery complaints** (Fig 02; `q8_battery_fingerprint_by_category.csv`, `q8_kyron_dose_test.csv`, `q8_misfiled_vs_correct_battery_check.csv`). The first three rows separate the battery categories from the service categories. The last two barely do.

   | Signal | low_range | other | app_issue | stock-out / queue / billing |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Share of tickets from 3W riders (3W = 10.4% of swaps) | 1.5% | 1.0% | 1.7% | 19.9% / 17.6% / 20.3% |
   | Same-season step-up, tickets per 1K 2W swaps (H1-25 vs H1-24) | 4.5x | 4.4x | 4.0x | 0.85x / 0.94x / 0.35x |
   | Kyron dose outside summer, ratio of ratios (Jul–Aug → Oct–Dec 24) | 1.75 | 1.71 | 1.77 | 0.86 / 0.88 / 0.65 |
   | Kyron O/E on the ticket's own pack vs inserted packs, city-month, Sep-24 to Jun-25 | 1.08 (1.03–1.14) | 1.06 (1.02–1.11) | 1.06 (1.00–1.12) | 0.99 (0.95–1.04) / 1.04 (0.97–1.11) / 0.82 (0.72–0.93) |
   | Pack SoH at insertion minus same supplier, city and month (points) | −0.057 | −0.070 | −0.014 | −0.008 / +0.022 / +0.038 |

   - **Vehicle class.** Range loss is a 2W phenomenon. 3W riders use app chat as much as 2W riders (44.1% vs 45.4% of their tickets), so genuine app problems would also reach them.
   - **3W mixing model.** `app_issue` has a 1.71% 3W share, against 1.17% for low_range + other and 19.4% for stock-out + queue + billing. That makes 97.0% of `app_issue` battery-like (95% CI 94.9%–99.1%), or 94.1% if genuine app tickets followed the swap mix.
   - **Dose test.** Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur received 39.2% Kyron 2W packs vs 8.5% elsewhere. Outside summer, each battery category responds on its own (1.71–1.77). The placebos do not: stock-out + queue 0.87, billing 0.69 for all riders (0.65 for 2W riders).
   - **The ticket's own pack separates little.**
     - Kyron share of linked 2W packs: low_range 37.9%, `other` 37.4% (p=0.58 vs low_range), `app_issue` 37.4% (p=0.65), queue 36.4% (p=0.22), stock-out 35.7% (p=0.035), billing 19.5%.
     - Kyron is 30.9% of the packs riders insert but 24.0% of the packs issued (Sep-24 to Jun-25). Against inserted packs, Kyron is only 6–8% over-represented on battery tickets and 4% on queue tickets.
     - So supplier mix does not cleanly separate battery tickets from queue tickets; only billing differs.
   - **SoH.** Within supplier, city and month, the pack the rider inserted is at most 0.07 SoH points worse (`other` −0.070, SE 0.016; low_range −0.057, SE 0.020). The link identifies the supplier cohort and the time, not an individual bad pack.
   - **Ticket-level check of the category** (`q8_ticket_linked_attempt_validation.csv`). This uses the attempt at which the rider inserted the ticket's pack, within the 48 h before the ticket.
     - 94.4% of `no_battery_available` tickets follow a failed_no_charged_battery attempt.
     - 75.4% of `long_queue` tickets follow an abandoned_queue attempt.
     - low_range (94.8%), `other` (96.5%), `app_issue` (94.3%) and billing (93.0%) tickets follow completed swaps.
   - **No app failure behind app/QR wording.**
     - Only 1.9% (app text) and 2.2% (QR text) of these riders logged a system error in the 7 days before the ticket, the same as every other text issue (1.9%–2.3%). Cancellations: 3.3% and 3.2%, vs 2.9%–3.5% for the other issues.
     - At station level, QR tickets per 1K attempts vs system-error rate: ρ=−0.07 (p=0.40, 141 stations).

3. **True battery-complaint volume and trend** (Fig 03; `q8_monthly_battery_complaints.csv`).
   - Full window: official 6,551 (15.1%); floor 15,912 (36.7%, 2.43x); central 21,458 (49.5%); upper 21,629 (49.9%, 3.30x).
   - Station availability: stock-outs 26.5% + queues 13.0% = 39.5% of tickets.
   - The central estimate is the largest driver. The floor beats stock-outs alone but not stock-outs + queues.
   - Apr–Jun 2025 (15,290 tickets): battery floor 39.9%, central 53.5%, upper 54.0% vs stock-outs 28.4% + queues 12.7% = 41.1%.
   - Per 1K 2W swaps (official `low_range` / + vehicle-phrased `other` / + `app_issue`):

     | Month | Official | + other | + app_issue |
     |---|---|---|---|
     | Jan-24 | 0.38 | 0.93 | 1.32 |
     | Aug-24 | 0.74 | 1.73 | 2.46 |
     | Sep-24 | 1.60 | 3.84 | 5.17 |
     | Jun-25 | 3.24 | 7.87 | 10.58 |

   - The text corrects the **level**, not the trend: `other` runs at 1.33–1.60x `low_range` in every month (median 1.43).
   - **The Sep-24 step is linked to Kyron by the city dose test, outside summer.**
     - Battery-group tickets per 1K 2W swaps, Jul–Aug-24 → Oct–Dec-24: the three Kyron cities 2.34 → 8.05 (3.44x); the other cities 2.20 → 4.37 (1.98x). Ratio of ratios 1.74.
     - Implied excess: about 1,113 battery-group tickets in Oct–Dec-24 in Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur. That is 371 a month, 42.4% of the 2,626 battery tickets there.
     - The same calculation on stock-out + queue tickets gives −135.
     - (The monthly r=0.92 with Kyron share correlates two trending series and is not used as evidence.)
   - **Per pack, the Kyron signal is small** (`q8_kyron_within_city_inserted.csv`). Within each city, Sep-24 to Jun-25, per 1K attempts that inserted the supplier's pack:
     - Kyron packs draw 1.07–1.51x (median 1.16x) the battery-group tickets of Cellora+Amptek packs, vs 0.96–1.13x (median 1.03x) for the stock-out + queue placebo;
     - that is an excess of about 510 tickets, or 4.0% of 12,757 linked battery-group tickets.
     - The complaint signal works at city and cohort level (where Kyron went, and when), not through the individual pack ID on a ticket.
   - **After the Kyron plateau, complaints kept rising (6.27 in Oct-24 → 10.58 per 1K 2W swaps in Jun-25), consistent with fleet ageing.**
     - Supporting evidence: reported "dying at" km fell 54 → 46 km over the same period and tracks pack SoH within month (0.73 km per SoH point).
     - But monthly correlations cannot separate ageing from trend. The r=0.89 with the share of packs below 80% SoH is matched by the stock-out + queue placebo (r=0.80) and turns negative in first differences (r=−0.67, n=8).
     - The rate also rose 6.27 → 7.98 from Oct-24 to Jan-25 while 0.0% of issued packs were below 80% SoH.

4. **The comment carries a rider-reported range number that the category cannot** (Fig 04).
   - 1,349 tickets say "scooter dying at X km" (1 implausible value dropped; 1,330 from 2W riders).
   - Median km: Kyron-linked 45 vs Cellora/Amptek 54 (Mann-Whitney p=4.7e-47).
   - Correlation with the linked pack's SoH: r=0.67 (n=945), 0.57 km per SoH point overall.
   - Within month (month fixed effects): 0.73 km per SoH point (p=7.8e-79). The link is not just a shared trend.
   - The monthly median fell 58 (Jan-24) → 54 (Oct-24) → 46 km (Jun-25). It tracks the event log's 2W median km per swap, 70.3 → 48.6 (monthly r=0.96).
   - Values are truncated near 60 km (26 of 1,330 above 60) because only riders who complain report a number. The medians describe complainers, not the fleet.

5. **No detectable pilot effect on billing complaints: pilot cities fell like Mumbai** (Fig 05; `q8_pricing_did_robustness.csv`, `q8_billing_rate_by_city.csv`).
   - Pilot vs Mumbai, the only control city with a Kyron share like the pilot cities: IRR 0.94 (95% CI 0.78–1.12, p=0.46); per active rider 0.91 (0.76–1.09, p=0.31).
   - **Where the apparent 1.29 comes from.** Against all four control cities the IRR is 1.29 (1.13–1.48). That figure comes from faster-falling billing rates in the three Kyron cities from Sep-24, before the pilot.
     - Inside the control group, Kyron city × post-Sep-24 vs Mumbai gives IRR 0.67 (0.56–0.80).
     - Completed swaps per active 2W rider per month rose 25.7 → 30.2 there vs 24.2 → 26.0 elsewhere (Jul–Aug → Oct–Dec-24). Shorter range means more swaps per rider, which deflates every per-swap rate.
   - Billing tickets per 1K swaps, Jan–Jun-24 → Oct-24 to Jun-25:
     - pilot: Bengaluru 1.89 → 1.15 (0.61x), Pune 2.20 → 1.21 (0.55x);
     - low-Kyron control: Mumbai 1.92 → 1.18 (0.61x);
     - Kyron cities: Delhi NCR 1.76 → 0.67 (0.38x), Hyderabad 1.84 → 0.65 (0.35x), Jaipur 1.94 → 0.74 (0.38x).
   - Independent riders, who pay the peak price, show no effect either: IRR 1.10 (0.83–1.46, p=0.50).
   - The earlier robustness checks (city trends 1.34; per active rider 1.18; placebo at Jul-24 1.14, p=0.17) all use the four-city control and inherit the same confound.
   - **The wording cannot explain billing complaints either.** "Surcharge laga diya bina bataye" stays at 34.2% → 34.4% of billing tickets in pilot cities (χ² p=0.98). It is also 32.7% in control cities that never had a surcharge.
   - 3W billing tickets per 1K 3W swaps rose 2.34 → 2.62, while 2W fell 2.00 → 0.79. This is consistent with a per-swap denominator effect on 2W, not with pricing.

6. **Urgency, repeat claims, competitor mentions and churn: no usable signal.**
   - Agent `priority` is independent of template (χ² p=0.56, Cramér's V 0.045) and does not change handling. Median resolution is 13.4 h (high) vs 13.0 h (low), Kruskal-Wallis p=0.12; unresolved 12.1% vs 11.7%.
   - "Empty station again, this is the third time this week" is not borne out: 6.7% of those riders had ≥3 logged stock-outs in the prior 7 days, vs 7.9% for other stock-out wording.
   - The category itself is validated: 94.5% / 94.0% of stock-out tickets had ≥1 logged stock-out in the prior week, vs 22.4% of billing tickets.
   - **Lapse** (last swap within 30 days after the ticket):
     - 6.6% of the 28,090 tickets raised before Apr-25.
     - Later months are right-censored (Apr-25 10.1%, May-25 13.7%), so the all-ticket figure of 8.1% (38,001 tickets) overstates it.
     - No issue group differs from billing after month, city, vehicle, fleet and tenure controls: ORs 0.88–1.13, all p≥0.11. A refit on tickets before Apr-25 gives 0.95–1.06, all p≥0.52.
   - Wording markers do not predict lapse either:
     - "third time this week": 9.0% vs 8.3% (p=0.29);
     - "late ho gaya delivery mein": 10.2% vs 9.0% (p=0.15).
   - With 0 competitor or switching mentions, tickets cannot measure switching intent.

7. **CSAT: the non-response bias is real but harmless; the blind spot is coverage** (Fig 06; `q8_csat_naive_ipw_scenarios.csv`).
   - Unresolved (5,107) and duplicate (923) tickets are never surveyed: 13.9% of all tickets.
   - Resolved tickets respond 39.9% of the time: 43.7% in the fastest quartile vs 28.3% in the slowest.
   - Scores do not depend on speed (3.54 vs 3.61; ρ=0.013, p=0.12).
   - IPW CSAT is 3.559 vs naive 3.557. The difference is +0.002 (95% CI −0.001 to +0.007, 40 bootstrap refits); response model AUC 0.58, ESS 14,018 of 14,918.
   - Bounds outside what IPW can see:
     - non-respondents 0.5 points lower → 3.26; 1.0 point lower → 2.96;
     - unresolved tickets scored 2 → 3.37; scored 1 → 3.25.
   - CSAT by issue ranges from 3.50 (queue) to 3.61 (stock-out).

8. **Resolution is identical across true issues** (`q8_resolution_by_true_issue.csv`).
   - Unresolved 11.3%–11.9% (χ² p=0.85); median resolution 13.0–13.4 h; p90 28.7–29.8 h.
   - Battery-health complaints are "resolved" like any other ticket, although an agent cannot fix a degraded pack.

9. **Ticket rates per 1K swaps** (Fig 07; `q8_city_ticket_rates_h1_2025.csv`, `q8_station_ticket_rates.csv`).
   - Network: 6.32 (Jan-24) → 17.74 (Jun-25); same-season H1-24 10.08 vs H1-25 14.68.
   - At station level, stock-out+queue ticket rate vs service-failure rate: ρ=0.85 (141 stations with ≥5K swaps). These categories are trustworthy.
   - H1-25 by city:
     - stock-out+queue: 3.18 (Mumbai) to 7.09 (Jaipur) per 1K swaps, a 2.2x spread with summer spikes;
     - battery group: 6.86 (Mumbai) to 10.81 (Jaipur) per 1K 2W swaps, a 1.6x spread, highest in the three Kyron-heavy cities. Those cities are also the hottest (n=6). Heat and Kyron cannot be separated by city alone, which is why finding 3 uses the non-summer dose test.

## Primary vs secondary drivers (of complaint volume)
- **Primary: battery health.** It is the largest and fastest-growing complaint driver: 4.0–4.5x same-season growth per 2W swap, and 49.5% of tickets (floor 36.7%). Within it:
  - the Sep-24 step is linked to Kyron by the city dose test (1.74; placebos 0.69–0.87), about 1,113 excess tickets in Oct–Dec-24 in the three Kyron cities;
  - the continued rise to Jun-25 is consistent with fleet-wide ageing (reported km 54 → 46; 0.73 km per SoH point within month), but the monthly series cannot separate ageing from trend.
- **Secondary: stock-outs and queues.** Together 39.5% of tickets. Flat same-season per swap (0.85x / 0.94x), with summer spikes concentrated in Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur.
- **Not a driver: the peak/off-peak pilot.** No detectable billing-complaint effect (pilot vs Mumbai IRR 0.94).

## Method notes
- **Taxonomy** (`q8_comment_taxonomy.csv`, all 89 templates):
  - normalise (lower-case; staion→station, battry→battery, rnage→range), then 10 ordered regex rules (first match wins); every template matched;
  - flags for Hinglish (Hindi function words), typos, vehicle blame, urgency (repeat / business impact / persistence) and the km figure;
  - a template-level review column records the reviewed true issue and the evidence used.
- **Battery-complaint estimates:**
  - floor = `low_range` + `other`;
  - central = floor + f × `app_issue`, where f comes from a two-group mixing model on the 3W share (parametric bootstrap CI, swap-mix sensitivity);
  - upper = floor + all of `app_issue`.
- **Ticket → attempt link.** For each ticket with a battery_id, take the attempts at which this rider inserted that pack and keep the one closest in time. An as-of join gives the rider's last (and previous) inserted pack before the ticket.
- **Battery fingerprint:**
  - 3W share of tickets by rider vehicle class;
  - same-season (H1-24 vs H1-25) tickets per 1K 2W swaps;
  - **Kyron dose test:** change in tickets per 1K 2W swaps from Jul–Aug-24 to Oct–Dec-24, Kyron cities vs the rest, per category, with placebo categories. Excess = observed minus (Kyron cities' Jul–Aug rate × the other cities' change);
  - **Kyron O/E:** observed Kyron packs on 2W-pack tickets / Σ city-month Kyron share of *inserted* 2W packs (`supplier_in` at attempts with a battery_in_id; city = station city, else rider home city), with an exact Poisson 95% CI;
  - **SoH gap:** the pack's SoH at the linked insertion (within 72 h of the ticket) minus the mean of same-supplier packs inserted in that city and month;
  - **within-city supplier ratio:** tickets per 1K attempts inserting the supplier's 2W pack.
- **Pricing DiD:** Poisson GLM on city-month billing counts, offset log(swaps) or log(active riders), city and month fixed effects, Pearson-scaled SEs. Checks:
  - placebo at Jul-24; city-specific linear trends;
  - Mumbai-only control; Kyron cities vs Mumbai inside the controls;
  - independent riders only; city-level phase rates.
- **Lapse:** logit with month, city, vehicle class, fleet affiliation and log tenure; rider-clustered SEs. Tickets before 2025-06-01; the level is reported on tickets before 2025-04-01 (right-censoring), with a refit on that sample.
- **CSAT:**
  - response model is a logistic regression on resolution-time decile, category, channel, priority, month and city (resolved tickets only, because unresolved/duplicate tickets are never surveyed);
  - stabilised weights r̂/p̂; 40 bootstrap refits;
  - pattern-mixture δ-adjustment and unresolved-ticket scenarios for what IPW cannot fix.
- **Station validation:** Spearman correlation across 141 stations with ≥5K completed swaps.

## Caveats & traps handled
- **What the battery_id is.**
  - 99.94% of 30,330 linked tickets carry a pack that this rider inserted at a recent attempt: median 4.8 h before the ticket, 97.7% within the previous 48 h.
  - It is the rider's last inserted pack before the ticket in 82.9% of cases, and the one before that in 13.9% (the rider swapped again before raising the ticket).
  - So it traces the attempt the ticket is about, and supplier expectations must use inserted packs. Kyron is 30.9% of inserted vs 24.0% of issued 2W packs.
  - The issued-pack basis used before verification inflated Kyron O/E for battery categories from 1.06–1.08 to 1.35–1.38, and the within-city ratio from a median of 1.16x to 1.70x.
  - About 70% of tickets carry a battery_id.
- **Simpson-type trap.** Kyron went mainly to three cities that also complain more. A network-wide inserted-pack expectation gives O/E 1.22 for `low_range`; the city-month adjustment cuts it to 1.08 (billing 0.64 → 0.82). City-level H1 comparisons are also confounded by the hot-city summer spike (H1-24 ratio 1.57 before any Kyron pack), so the dose test uses non-summer months.
- **Control-group trap (pricing).** Three of the four control cities are Kyron cities. Their per-swap rates fell faster because Kyron raised swaps per 2W rider, so the four-city DiD mistook a battery effect for a pricing effect. Mumbai is the like-for-like control.
- **Per-swap denominators move with range loss.** More swaps per rider in Kyron cities makes per-swap rates understate the battery rise there, and deflates other categories.
- **Trending series.** Monthly correlations (r=0.92 with Kyron share; 0.89 with packs below 80% SoH; 0.96 for reported vs logged km) are not used as causal evidence. Where a claim rests on them, the dose test or the within-month km slope backs it.
- **Templated text.** The comments are 89 templates whose choice within a category looks random (identical priority, resolution, CSAT and lapse across templates). Text signals beyond the category exist only where the template itself differs: the `other` bin, the km figure and QR. The `app_issue` attribution rests on structured fingerprints, not on wording.
- **Truncated km and censored lapse.** "Dying at" values cluster below 60 km (complainers only). Lapse after Mar-25 tickets is right-censored.
- **Seasonality.** All growth comparisons are same-season (H1 vs H1, or non-summer windows).
- **Association, not causation.** This applies to lapse after a ticket (complainers may already be disengaging) and to the Kyron dose test (a city-level natural experiment with three treated cities).

## Recommendations
1. **Head of CX: fix the taxonomy at source.**
   - Retire `other` as a catch-all and add "battery/vehicle performance".
   - Auto-label every ticket with the 89-template rules (deterministic, 100% coverage), and capture battery_id and reported km as required fields on range complaints.
   - Impact: the battery KPI moves from 3.24 to 7.87 per 1K 2W swaps in Jun-25 (low_range + vehicle-phrased `other`; 10.58 with `app_issue`). Battery health becomes visible as about half of all tickets (49.5% central, 36.7% floor) instead of 15.1%.
2. **Fleet/Procurement: track supplier risk with city and cohort complaint rates, reported km and SoH telemetry, not with the battery_id on tickets.**
   - Expected impact: the Kyron rollout added about 1,113 battery-group tickets in Oct–Dec-24 in Delhi NCR, Hyderabad and Jaipur (371 a month, 42% of the battery tickets there; the stock-out + queue placebo gives −135). Stopping a similar rollout would avoid that load.
   - A KPI keyed on ticket battery_id would be weak: Kyron packs draw only 1.16x the battery tickets per inserted-pack attempt, vs 1.03x for the placebo.
   - Instead, track per new pack cohort and city:
     - battery-group tickets per 1K 2W swaps, against a placebo category;
     - the median reported "dying at" km (54 → 46 km since Oct-24);
     - pack SoH.
   - Stage supplier rollouts city by city so that the dose comparison is built in, and write a complaint-rate and range threshold into supplier acceptance terms.
   - Cross-link: q4 and the budget question (batteries vs stations).
3. **Support ops: stop treating battery complaints as resolvable tickets.**
   - Route battery-group tickets (Apr–Jun-25: 39.9% of tickets on the floor, 53.5% central) to fleet ops to quarantine and test the pack, not to a generic 13 h resolution flow.
   - Drop or rule-base `priority`: today it has no relation to the issue and no effect on resolution time (13.4 h high vs 13.0 h low).
4. **Pricing: complaint risk is not a reason to hold back a rollout decision.**
   - The pilot shows no detectable billing-complaint effect (pilot vs Mumbai IRR 0.94, 0.78–1.12). Base the rollout decision on margin and demand evidence (q5).
   - As low-cost hygiene, add an in-app surcharge notice and a structured "billing reason" field. The comment text cannot separate surcharge confusion from wrong deductions.
5. **CX analytics: report CSAT with its coverage and response rate.**
   - Survey unresolved and duplicate tickets (13.9%, never surveyed today); the plausible range is 3.25–3.56.
   - IPW is not needed for the headline (+0.002), but publish the response rate by resolution-speed quartile (28% vs 44%) as a data-quality KPI.
6. **Product/CX: add a structured "considering switching / competitor" tag.** No comment mentions competitors, so tickets cannot inform churn-risk or competitor responses today (cross-link q6 retention).

## Verification
The verifier (`outputs/findings/q8_verification.md`, `analysis/verify/q8_verify.py`) confirmed the descriptive core and asked for six fixes. All were applied in `analysis/q8_ticket_text.py`, the script was re-run end to end, and every number above is printed by it.

1. **Pricing (refuted, fixed).**
