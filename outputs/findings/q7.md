# Q7 · Data-quality audit, cleaning impact and anomalies

Script: `analysis/q7_data_quality_anomalies.py` (runs in about 9 s; full printed output in `outputs/tables/q7_run_log.txt`). Scorecard: `outputs/tables/q7_data_quality_scorecard.csv` (26 rows).
Every number below is printed by the script. "Raw" means `data/parquet`; "clean" means `data/clean`. Margin levels are under our usage-based battery-depreciation assumption (70% EOL, no salvage).

## Headline
The documented cleaning decisions hold up under independent tests. For example, the firmware fix moves v3.2.0 stations' 00–05 h share from 18.6% to 3.5% (reference 3.7%), and the pilot tariff engine's own clock confirms it: 11.6% → 100% of PEAK rows fall inside the true PEAK hours. The audit also found 13 undocumented issues. Three of them change conclusions:
- **122,204 swaps issued packs below the 70% end-of-life SoH**, 33,761 of them after the packs' recorded "retirement". The cost model charges ₹12.9M of wear on these swaps, which is the largest single sensitivity on the margin story.
- **tariff_code labels none of the 233,264 partner swaps that carry pilot pricing.** They are 54.0% of all pilot-priced swaps.
- **Failure rates switch regime on month boundaries because temperature does.** The 1-Jul-2024 drop from 9.6% to 3.7% happened only in the three hot cities. Cities that got the same price rise but stayed below the heat threshold did not move. So the drop is heat, not price.

## Key findings

**A. Documented issues: does the fix work, and what would leaving it untreated cost?**

1. **The firmware fix is validated by an independent field, and the one edge bug is identified row by row** (fig q7_01).
   - Scope: 139,490 attempts (3.66% of 3,814,982 clean attempts) at v3.2.0 stations, 10-Mar to 14-Apr-2025.
   - Hour profile: share of attempts at 00–05 h goes from 18.6% (raw) to 3.5% (fixed), against 3.7% at unaffected v3.2.1 stations in the same weeks. Mean hour goes from 10.40 to 15.13 (reference 15.18). The busiest hour moves from 14 h to 19 h (reference 19 h). Total-variation distance to the reference drops from 50.8 to 1.07 pts.
   - **Tariff clock.** The pilot schedule read off the data (modal code by corrected hour, pilot-city independents after 1-Oct-2024; every hour is at least 99.92% one code; Q5 found the same bands):
     - PEAK: 12:00–13:59 and 19:00–22:59
     - OFFPEAK: 23:00–05:59 and 14:00–16:59
     - STD: otherwise

     PEAK is **not** billed across 12:00–22:59.
   - Results on the true bands:
     - Of 8,863 PEAK rows at v3.2.0 pilot stations in the window, 11.6% sit inside true PEAK hours on the raw clock and 100.0% on the corrected clock.
     - Across all codes, the code agrees with the hour schedule for 10.4% of 14,711 rows raw and 100.0% fixed.
   - Cost of no fix: the network-wide 12–22 h share in the window would read 65.7% instead of 76.4%. The worst failure hour at v3.2.0 stations would appear as 10 h instead of 16 h.
   - Edge bug: the bug window is defined on **true** local date. Zero v3.2.0 events are logged on 14-Apr 18:30–23:59, against 1,618 expected if the window ran on logged time. So the first ~5.5 h of 10-Mar were logged on the 9-Mar evening and never shifted:
     - 0 v3.2.0 events appear at corrected 10-Mar 00:00–05:29 (121 expected).
     - There are +147 excess events on 9-Mar 18:30–23:59 (z = 3.6).
     - **Row-level proof:** in the whole post-pilot period, only 16 rows contradict the tariff schedule. All 16 are v3.2.0 rows logged on 9-Mar-2025 between 18:31 and 22:51 and billed OFFPEAK, which is their true time of 00:01–04:21 on 10-Mar.
     - KPI impact is about 147 attempts (0.0039%). See *Proposed cleaning fixes*.

2. **The test stations are not the "small number of zero-value transactions at unusual hours" that the documentation describes** (scorecard DQ05).
   - Size: 62,031 attempts (1.60% of raw), 59,134 completed swaps, ₹3,930,760 revenue.
   - Only 2,897 zero-value rows, all of them non-completed attempts.
   - Night share is 3.8%, against 3.6% for the network, so the hours are not unusual.
   - Volume is 1,643 swaps per station-month, against 1,656 for a real Bengaluru station. They were used by 3,300 real riders; 8.0% of those riders' attempts were at test stations, and 9 riders used only test stations.
   - If kept in: Bengaluru swaps +8.1%, Bengaluru revenue +8.2%, network revenue +1.7%. Bengaluru failure rate moves only from 4.109% to 4.121%. Bengaluru's Jun-25/Jan-24 growth falls from 3.16x to 3.06x.
   - Excluding them is right because they are labelled internal assets. Bengaluru volume and revenue figures carry a one-sided **+8%** sensitivity to this decision.

3. **Zero-filling telemetry would invent stockouts exactly where visibility is worst** (fig q7_04).
   - 9,384 station-hours have all sensors blank (0.64% of 1,461,456). 100% of them are at poor-connectivity stations, where they make up 4.0% of hours.
   - The 36,747 "partial" rows have every value populated.
   - With zero-filling, stockout-hours (zero charged 2W packs) would rise from 18,530 to 27,914 (x1.51), and at poor-tier stations from 2,729 to 12,113 (x4.44). Poor-tier mean stock would be understated by 4.0%.
   - The blanks are not informative. In the same station-hours, riders' stockout rate is 3.73% when the cabinet was blank and 3.68% when it reported (χ² p = 0.57).

4. **There are no near-duplicates under any rule** (fig q7_05).
   - The documented rule (same rider, station, event type and both battery IDs, ≤180 s, ≥1 offline_batch record) finds 0 pairs.
   - The rule can see retries: a sync retry re-sends the *same* record, so it would repeat the battery IDs.
   - Direct check: only **6** same-battery_in pairs within 300 s exist network-wide, against 3.8 expected by chance. That is out of 17,107 same-rider pairs ≤300 s with both packs recorded.
   - Looser rules find:

     | Rule | Pairs | Share of attempts |
     |---|---|---|
     | D1 same rider, station and event type, ≤180 s, offline_batch involved | 1,368 | 0.036% |
     | D2 same rider, station and event type, ≤180 s, any sync mode | 5,633 | 0.148% |
     | D3 same rider and station, ≤180 s, any event type | 6,373 | 0.167% |
     | D6 same rider, station and event type, ≤600 s | 18,654 | 0.489% |

   - These pairs have no retry signature:
     - The gap density is flat: 931 pairs per 30-s bin at 0–120 s vs 933 at 120–600 s (χ² uniformity p = 0.20).
     - offline_batch is involved in 24.3% of D2 pairs, against 24.1% expected from station offline shares.
   - Dropping the D2 pairs anyway would cut swaps by 0.16% and move the failure rate from 5.553% to 5.561%. Keep them, and keep the documented rule.

5. **Revenue logic is right, but only because amount_charged is used.**
   - All 228,307 non-completed attempts (5.98%) carry a list price and tariff code but ₹0 charged. Using list − discount on every attempt would overstate revenue by ₹15,049,697 (+6.6% on ₹229.2M).
   - The tariff identity holds for 163,612 of 163,922 PEAK swaps (list + ₹15) and 34,734 of 34,833 OFFPEAK swaps (list − ₹8).
   - 7,194 swaps (0.20%) carry unexplained ±₹5/±₹10 deviations: net ₹7,961, gross ₹59,049. They do not trigger billing-dispute tickets: 0.83% get one within 7 days, against 0.87% of normal swaps (p = 0.78).

6. **CSAT: re-weighting barely moves the mean; the bias is in tickets that are never scored** (fig q7_06).
   - Response rate is 43–45% in resolution-speed deciles 1–8 and 27% / 21% in deciles 9–10. Unresolved (5,163) and duplicate (937) tickets have 0% response.
   - Naive mean 3.560 (95% CI 3.542–3.582); IPW 3.563; post-stratified 3.563 (3.543–3.583). The response model has AUC 0.574, and scores vary little with speed (3.52–3.63), so re-weighting changes little.
   - Unresolved tickets are 12.0% of the ticket population. **Scenario:** if they would score 1–3, population CSAT would be 3.26–3.50. Without that assumption (any score 1–5) the range is 3.26–3.74.
   - So the naive 3.56 is a resolved-ticket CSAT, not a customer CSAT.

7. **The smaller documented issues are handled correctly and move KPIs very little.**
   - **km outliers:** 15,287 of 3,787,124 readings (0.40%). The 2W mean goes from 58.54 to 58.05 km and the standard deviation from 17.99 to 10.83. The cut is not arbitrary: valid readings span 22.1–136.7 km, and outliers sit at −20..−1 and 320..420 km. Outliers are random across riders (dispersion 0.97) and suppliers (0.4% each), so Kyron's range gap is untouched (48.4 vs 60.8 km after cleaning).
   - **SoC/SoH > 100:** 9,684 of 14.7M readings (0.066%). Mean SoH moves by 0.0002 pts. The share of attempts with such a reading falls from 4.65% (Jan-24) to 0.09% (Jun-25) as packs age. Within Q1-2024 it is spread across stations like chance (dispersion 1.06), so this is drift on new packs, not faulty cabinets.
   - **home_city spellings:** 585 riders (2.9%) are recoded. Exact-match counts would undercount each city by 2.6–3.7%. There are 0 mismatches against the home_zone prefix.

**B. Undocumented issues**

8. **Battery IDs are not physical** (DQ01, high severity).
   - A rider returns the pack they were last issued in only 0.032% of 3,566,737 consecutive swaps.
   - Of 3,580,175 consecutive re-issues of the same pack, 81.3% happen in a different city. 239,049 (6.7%) happen in a different city within 60 minutes, and 44,274 within 10 minutes (strict seconds).
   - Per-pack SoH trajectories remain valid. Per-pack location, rotation and turnaround cannot be measured.

9. **End-of-life packs are still being issued, and the cost model keeps charging wear on them** (DQ04, high severity).
   - 122,204 completed swaps (3.41% of 3,586,675) issued packs below 70% SoH. The first was on 2025-04-03, and 100% were Kyron.
   - 33,761 of them came after the recorded retirement on 15-Jun-2025, using all 1,438 "retired" packs at a median SoH of 61.8%.
   - The retirement changed nothing in the field. Kyron's share of issued packs was 24.3% in the 7 days before and 24.5% after. Stockouts did not jump (5.03% → 5.35%).
   - **Cost-model consequence (sensitivity).** The cost model charges ₹12,896,314 of wear on these swaps (₹105.5 per swap). That equals ₹17.8 per swap across all May-25 swaps and ₹23.1 across all Jun-25 swaps (table `q7_wear_past_eol_sensitivity.csv`).
   - Under 70%-EOL, no-salvage book depreciation these packs are already fully written off. Dropping the charge changes cm1 per swap as follows:

     | Month | cm1 per swap as modelled | Without wear past EOL |
     |---|---|---|
     | Apr-25 | −₹6.3 | −₹4.6 |
     | May-25 | −₹5.4 | +₹12.4 |
     | Jun-25 | −₹5.0 | +₹18.0 |

   - A replacement-cost view (each cycle consumes part of a future pack) keeps the charge. So report both views. This is the largest single sensitivity on the "eroding margin" headline. Flagged to Q1 and Q4.

10. **Peak/off-peak pricing is hidden inside the PARTNER code** (DQ02, high severity).
    - 163,312 PARTNER swaps carry +₹15 and 69,952 carry −₹8, worth ₹1.89M net. They follow the pilot schedule exactly: the +15 rows fall in true PEAK hours 100.00% of the time and the −8 rows in true OFFPEAK hours 99.97% of the time.
    - **tariff_code labels none of these 233,264 pilot-priced partner swaps, so it misses 100% of partner pilot exposure.** (0 partner swaps are coded PEAK or OFFPEAK.)
    - Size of the gap:
      - 43.0% of the 541,863 post-pilot partner swaps in Bengaluru and Pune
      - 64.8% of the 359,944 post-pilot Bengaluru/Pune swaps by partners with peak_surcharge_billable = Y
      - **54.0% of all 432,019 pilot-priced completed swaps** (198,755 coded PEAK/OFFPEAK plus 233,264 hidden)
    - The pattern appears only for Y-flag partners, only in pilot cities and only after 1-Oct-2024. The PEAK and OFFPEAK codes therefore cover independent riders only.

11. **Station and battery metadata contradict activity.**
    - **3W slots:** 237,310 3W attempts (6.2%) happen at the 83 stations that list 0 3W slots. The 3W share there is 11.0%, against 10.7% at stations with 3W slots, and the 3W stockout rate is the same (6.41% vs 6.44%). The slot metadata does not constrain 3W activity (DQ12).
    - **Commission and manufacture dates:** 5,154 of 6,500 packs (79.3%) were used a median 191 days before their recorded commission date, and 4,506 before their manufacture date. Where lot codes are valid yymm, the code month matches the manufacture month for only 15.1% of packs. Six Kyron 3W codes (KY-2413…2418) are not valid months.
    - **Firmware field:** station_firmware never changes within a station. 1,083,456 attempts are labelled v3.2.0 before that firmware was installed on 10-Mar-2025, so the field cannot date firmware changes.

12. **Other undocumented issues**
    - payment_mode is 'partner_invoice' on all 3,877,013 rows.
    - The PREPAID and PROMO_FREE codes never appear. The 372,083 prepaid_pack swaps (10.4%) are billed exactly like pay-as-you-go.
    - 746 KYC-unverified riders made 139,764 swaps.
    - Queue waits are hard-capped at 1,800 s (143 completed swaps) and 2,400 s (34 abandons).
    - Tickets: 86 fall outside the window, 535 reference test stations, and 40 were created before the rider signed up.
    - All 9,484 "other" tickets (21.6%) are three vehicle-blaming templates ("scooter weak…", "bike problem…", "vehicle performance").
    - **Checks that passed:** 0 events before sign-up, before commissioning or after decommissioning; 0 pack-versus-vehicle class mismatches.
    - **Energy accounting is consistent:** correlation r = 0.982 with the energy needed to refill the pack. Gen1 chargers draw 8.0% more grid energy per kWh delivered than Gen3 (Gen1 1.128, Gen2 1.077, Gen3 1.044).

**C. Anomalies**

13. **No rogue stations. The spread in failure rates is heat × charger generation, and one station is a borderline flag** (fig q7_03).
    - **Stage 1** (controlled for city × month × vehicle class): 41 stations sit above and 39 below the 99.8% Poisson limits. But over-dispersion is φ = 35.7, so the variation is systematic, not noise. Pooled observed/expected: Gen1 1.14, Gen2 0.92, Gen3 0.63; Launch 1.07, Wave1 0.88, Wave2 0.58.
    - After widening the limits for over-dispersion, 0 high and 3 low outliers remain: STN-JAI-150, STN-DEL-058 and STN-JAI-148. All three are Wave2 Gen3 sites in hot cities, which is the heat × generation pattern below.
    - **The generation effect exists only under heat** (table `q7_station_oe_heat_generation.csv`):

      | Context | Gen1 | Gen2 | Gen3 |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Outside Apr–Jun, all cities | ≈1.00 | ≈1.00 | ≈1.00 |
      | Apr–Jun, cool cities | 1.16 | 0.96 | 0.88 |
      | Apr–Jun, hot cities (Delhi NCR, Jaipur, Hyderabad) | 1.27 | 0.69 | 0.44 |

      Outside Apr–Jun every generation sits at 0.97–1.00.
    - **Stage 2, quasi-Poisson GLM on station design and load.** Fit statistics:

      | | Spec A: additive design terms | Spec B: + generation × hot city |
      |---|---|---|
      | Residual scale | 15.44 | 4.31 |
      | Deviance explained | 78.2% | 93.8% |
      | 99.8% band at the median station | ±34% | ±18% |
      | Stations outside the band | 0 | 1 (STN-HYD-081) |

      - Spec A's "0 anomalies" is an artefact of misspecification. It leaves 17 small stations (fitted failures < 300; 16 Wave2, 15 of them in cool cities) at 1.41x their fit. That excess inflates the scale and widens the limits. Spec B fits the same 17 stations at 1.06x.
    - **Rate ratios.** Spec A's Gen3 0.69 (95% CI 0.60–0.78), Gen2 0.81 (0.78–0.85) and Wave2 0.76 (0.66–0.89) are **heat-averaged, not structural**. Under spec B, relative to Gen1 in the same city type:
      - Cool cities: Gen2 0.92, Gen3 0.92 (p = 0.051).
      - Hot cities: Gen2 0.70, Gen3 0.51.
      - Wave2: 0.87 (0.80–0.94).
      - Highway fuel-pump sites: 0.95 (p = 0.024).
      - No effect from connectivity (1.00) or load (0.99, p = 0.45).
    - **Collinearity.** Wave2 is entirely Gen3 (30 of 37 Gen3 stations). Only the 7 Wave1 Gen3 sites separate the two terms, so the Gen3 and Wave2 effects are weakly identified.
    - **STN-HYD-081 is borderline** (Hyderabad, Wave1, Gen3, residential, owned lot). It sits at 1.22x its fit, z = 3.10 against a limit of 3.09. It is not a robust rogue station:
      - It falls back inside the limits (z = 3.04; 0 stations outside) when load is measured as attempts per active day instead of the slot-based measure, which inherits the unreliable slots_3w.
      - Its cell is poorly identified. The 4 Wave1 Gen3 hot-city sites run 1.12x the fit as a group, and its sister site STN-HYD-083 sits at 1.21x (z = 2.31).
      - Its stage-1 O/E is 1.06 in Jul–Mar and 0.50 in Apr–Jun, against 0.99 and 0.44 for its generation and city-heat peers.
    - **Load metric robustness:** spec B with attempts per active day as the load term gives the same fit (scale 4.26, 93.9% of deviance explained).

14. **Battery cohorts: the fast fade is a lot cohort (KY-2407/08/09), not shown to be supplier-wide** (fig q7_08).
    - Kyron 2W packs lose 0.0947 SoH pts per cycle against 0.0291 for Cellora/Amptek 2W (x3.26). But **all 1,385 Kyron 2W packs come from lots KY-2407/08/09.** These are the same three codes that are the only fast-fading Kyron 3W lots.
    - Those 3W lots lose 0.0748–0.0788 pts/cycle, +122% to +136% against their own peers (BH-adjusted q ≈ 0). The later Kyron 3W lots (KY-2410…2418, 177 packs) fade normally, at 0.0314–0.0343.
    - So the data point to a 2407–09 cohort defect, consistent with Q4. "Supplier-wide" cannot be tested for 2W because no later Kyron 2W lot exists.
    - **The 3W lot effect is not an exposure artefact.** Over each pack's first 150 cycles (identical median first use, 03-Jan-2024), the slope is 0.0787 (76 packs) vs 0.0332 (177 packs). In the same calendar window (Jan–Jun 2025) it is 0.0812 vs 0.0349.
    - The fast 3W lots account for all 53 retired Kyron 3W packs (24 + 18 + 11). **23 packs from them are still in service** at 70.0–73.1% SoH, losing 0.072 pts/cycle.
    - bms_firmware has no practically relevant effect. Within supplier × pack groups, BMS-version medians are close: Kyron 3W 0.0334–0.0348 (Kruskal p = 0.063), Kyron 2W 0.0945–0.0953 (p = 0.18). The only significant group is Amptek 2W, at 0.0289–0.0294 (p = 0.008).

15. **Failure rates step at month boundaries because temperature steps there; the 1-Jul-2024 drop is heat, not price** (fig q7_07).
    - 7-day failure rate before vs after the 1st of the month, with mean city max temperature over the same days:

      | Pivot | Failure step | City max temp step |

    - **It is a threshold, not a calendar effect.** City-month failure rate is 3.95% in the 86 city-months at or below 34 °C mean max temp, 5.27% at 34–36 °C (4), 10.07% at 36–38 °C (8) and 15.3% above 38 °C (10). The correlation is 0.745 over 108 city-months. March warms by 6.7 °C (2024) and 6.4 °C (2025) without a failure step (−0.00 / +0.07 pts), because it stays below the threshold.

    - Monthly rates: Mar-24 3.95%, Apr 6.48%, May 11.93%, Jun 9.85%, Jul 3.92%.

## Primary vs secondary drivers
- **Time anomalies.**
  - Primary: month-level heat above about 34–36 °C city-month mean max temperature, consistent with Q2/Q3. The cross-city contrast on 1-Jul-2024 and the flat March step separate heat from the calendar.
  - Not drivers: the 1-Jul-2024 price rise (cool cities flat under the same rise), the firmware bug, competitor entry, the Kyron retirement.
  - Day-level heat alerts are not a usable test: the weather barely varies within a month.
- **Station failure anomalies.**
  - Primary: heat × charger generation. Gen1 in hot cities in Apr–Jun runs 1.27x its peers against Gen3 0.44x; outside Apr–Jun every generation is at ≈1.00.
  - Secondary: expansion wave (Wave2 0.87 after the heat term, weakly separated from Gen3) and location type (highway fuel pumps 0.95).
  - Not drivers: connectivity tier, load, firmware.
  - No rogue stations; one borderline flag (STN-HYD-081).
- **Data-quality risk to headline KPIs.**
  - Primary:
    - Wear charged past end of life: ₹12.9M, or ₹17.8–23.1 per swap in May–Jun-25.
    - tariff_code hiding pilot pricing: 54.0% of pilot-priced swaps.
    - Test-station inclusion: Bengaluru +8%.
    - Season mis-binning: 899K attempts in Mar and Jun.
  - Secondary: km, SoC/SoH, city spellings, queue caps (all below 0.5% effect).

## Method notes
- **Before vs after.** The raw parquet minus test stations is "before"; applying the clean.py rule gives "after". Each scorecard row records:
  - the detection method
  - rows affected over the relevant table (attempts, swaps, readings, station-hours, riders, packs or tickets)
  - the decision
  - the KPI impact
- **Firmware validation.**
  - Hour profiles of v3.2.0 vs v3.2.1 stations in the same weeks, compared with total-variation distance and χ² homogeneity. With n = 139K even a 1-pt difference is "significant", so TVD is the effect size.
  - Independent clock: the true pilot tariff schedule, read off the data as the modal code per corrected hour. We compare the tariff code with the schedule on the raw and the corrected clock, and list every row in the pilot period that contradicts the schedule.
  - Window edges: 30-minute volume bins compared against v3.2.1 volume, scaled by the pre-bug v3.2.0/v3.2.1 ratio.
- **Duplicates.** Consecutive attempts per rider, sorted by time with event_id breaking ties; seven rule variants. The retry signature tested has three parts:
  - a gap-density spike
  - offline_batch over-representation against station-specific expectations
  - repeated battery IDs, against a chance expectation of Σ 1 / (packs of that vehicle class)
- **Telemetry.** A zero-fill simulation; an MNAR test that joins event outcomes to the same station-hour at poor-tier stations.
- **CSAT.**
  - Logistic response model (speed decile, category, channel, priority) with Hajek IPW.
  - Post-stratification on speed decile × category × channel cells, with 400 bootstrap resamples for the CI.
  - A pattern-mixture scenario for unresolved tickets (scores 1–3), plus the unrestricted 1–5 range.
- **Wear past EOL.** The cost model's wear_cost on completed swaps whose issued pack has soh_out < 70, summed by month. cm1 per swap is recomputed without that charge.
- **Station funnel.**
  - Stage 1: indirect standardisation. Expected failures = attempts × the failure rate of peers in the same city, month and vehicle class, excluding the station itself. This controls for city mix, seasonality and commissioning timing.
  - Over-dispersion is estimated from 10%-winsorised z-scores (Spiegelhalter 2005).
  - Stage 2: quasi-Poisson GLM with the stage-1 expectation as offset, using a Pearson-scaled funnel. Spec A uses additive design and load terms. Spec B adds charger generation × hot city (Delhi NCR, Jaipur, Hyderabad). Spec C is spec B with attempts per active day as the load term.
  - Diagnostic: pooled observed/fit by wave × generation × hot city; a well-specified model gives ≈1.00 in every cell.
- **Lots.**
  - Per-pack SoH-loss slopes from clean.py, using packs with ≥50 cycles. Each lot is tested against the rest of its own supplier × pack-type group (Mann–Whitney, Benjamini–Hochberg). A lot is flagged at q < 0.01 and |difference| > 10%.

- **Time.**
  - Robust z of the daily rate against a centred 29-day rolling median and MAD.

  - City-month threshold table and correlation.
  - Cross-city contrast on 1-Jul-2024: same price change, different temperature change.
  - Heat-alert days compared within the same city-month.
  - Difference-in-differences for the firmware window (system errors) and competitor entry (8 weeks either side).

## Caveats & traps handled
- **Price vs heat on 1-Jul-2024 (resolved).** The failure drop falls on the same day as the base-price change. The cross-city contrast resolves it: only the hot cities moved. Any before/after price analysis still needs a control group, such as cool cities or a same-season comparison.
- **Heat vs calendar.** In this data the weather itself is month-level (within city-month SD of daily max temp 1.52 °C), so heat shows up as month steps. Day-level heat tests are structurally uninformative. Use city-month temperature or city × month effects. Temperature and failures are associated with a threshold; the mechanism (Gen1 derating) is documented by Q3. Causation is not proven here.
- **Misspecified funnel limits hide or invent anomalies.** Dispersion-widened limits are only as good as the model. The additive model inflated the scale to 15.4 (±34% band) because it missed the heat-conditional generation effect.
- **Collinearity.** Wave2 is all Gen3, so the Gen3 and Wave2 rate ratios are weakly identified. The load term uses swaps per slot-day, which inherits the unreliable slots_3w; the slot-free alternative gives the same fit.
- **Simpson-type trap in station rankings.** Wave-2 sites operated only through the high-failure months. Raw failure rates would penalise them, so the month-level standardisation is essential.
- **Survivorship in the funnel.** It covers the 150 real stations, including STN-DEL-051 (decommissioned) up to its last month.
- **Season definition trap.** common.py's SEASON_OF_MONTH puts March (3.9% failure rate) in "Summer" and June (9.85% / 8.16%) in "Monsoon". Season dummies therefore mix regimes. 898,816 attempts fall in the mis-binned months.
- **MNAR.** IPW corrects only for observed drivers of response (AUC 0.574). Unresolved tickets have no responses at all, so only a scenario range is possible.
- **Battery IDs.** Per-pack SoH and wear numbers remain valid because each ID's trajectory is internally consistent. Rotation and location are not.
- **Edge-bug estimates.** The 121 / 147 figures are model-based (scaled v3.2.1 volume). 16 rows are identified exactly, through the tariff schedule.
- **Wear-cost convention.** The ₹12.9M past-EOL wear is a sensitivity that depends on the depreciation view (book vs replacement cost). It is not an error in the brief's cost model.
